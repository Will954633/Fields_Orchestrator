## The lead number — my reasoning, which differs from the spec

The issue spec proposed **42** (houses sold last quarter). I've changed it to **29** (days to sell),
and the reason matters more than the number.

`42` fails a test the spec itself sets elsewhere: Q2 2026 is still filling in as settlements
register, so 42 is a **floor**, not a count. Printing "forty-two houses sold" in a permanent artefact
that will be read for three months means printing a number we already know will rise. It also asks
the cover to carry a *flat* series (41 → 44 → 42) as though it moved.

`29` passes every criterion the spec set, and passes them harder:

| Criterion | 29 days |
|---|---|
| Reader can verify against own experience | They watched homes on their street sell this autumn |
| Describes *their* position, not our cleverness | It is how long their neighbour waited |
| Needs no definition | None |
| Moved meaningfully | 37 → 29. Eight days faster, year on year |
| Contradicts the story they arrived with | Sharply. Every national signal says *slower* |
| Is it stable enough to print? | Yes — measured on completed sales, not awaiting settlement |

The national picture the reader is soaked in: dwelling values down three consecutive months,
capital-city auction clearance under 50% for six straight weeks, Brisbane at 23.5%, and house price
expectations at a three-year low with fewer than half of Australians expecting rises.

Burleigh Waters got **faster**.

## PAGE 2 — What changed this quarter

*~250 words. Signed by Will. A named human with a position.*

If you have been reading the property pages this winter you will have seen a market in retreat.
Dwelling values across the five largest capitals fell 0.9% in July, the third consecutive monthly
fall. Auction clearance rates sat below 50% for six straight weeks. Fewer than half of Australians
now expect prices to rise — the weakest reading in three years.

None of that is wrong. It is also not a description of Burleigh Waters.

Here, the middle home sold in 29 days last quarter, down from 37 a year earlier. Just over half of
all sales completed inside 30 days. The number of homes changing hands held broadly steady across
the last three quarters, while both Robina and Varsity Lakes recorded fewer. And the median house
price over the last twelve months sits at $1,925,000, up 6.9% on the twelve months before it.

That is a genuine divergence, and it is the most useful thing we can tell you this quarter: **the
three suburbs of the southern Gold Coast have stopped moving together.** You can no longer read your
own market from a national headline, and increasingly you cannot read it from the suburb next door
either.

What follows is what we can show you, and — on page 8 — what we cannot. Including the figure we
published last quarter that turned out to be wrong.

## PAGE 3 — The number, and how sure we are

*The trust page.*

### $1,925,000

**Median house price, Burleigh Waters, twelve months to Q2 2026.**

Not a quarter. Not an estimate of your house. The middle price of every house we recorded selling
here over a full year.

**How sure we are: $1,855,550 — $2,000,000**

That range is a 90% confidence interval. It means that if we drew this sample again, the true middle
price would fall inside that band nine times in ten. The narrower the band, the more sales sit behind
the figure. Ours rests on **167 recorded sales**, and the band runs ±3.9%.

Almost nobody publishes this. A median without an interval is a number pretending to be more precise
than it is.

### The check we can't fail quietly

realestate.com.au publishes a Burleigh Waters median of **$1,910,000**, drawn from **195 sales** over
the same window on the same methodology.

We are **0.8% apart**.

Our 167 priced sales plus roughly 32 where the price was withheld comes to about 199 transactions
against their 195. Two organisations, working independently, counting almost the same houses and
landing within eight thousand dollars of each other.

We print this because it is the only way to show our number is not a house view. If we were ever
materially adrift from an independent count, this page is where you would see it.

### What we will not tell you from this

**We are not reporting a quarter-on-quarter price movement for Burleigh Waters.** Of the last six
quarters, only one carries a sample tight enough to draw a line through, and it has no comparable
neighbour. Every competitor will draw that line anyway. We would rather show you where the data
stops.

## PAGE 4 — What actually changed: activity, not price

Last quarter we told you sales in Burleigh Waters had fallen roughly 30%. On a fuller transaction
set, they had not. They were flat. We deal with that properly on page 8.

Here is what the corrected series shows across the four quarters where our data is comparable:

| Quarter | Direction |
|---|---|
| Q3 2025 | — |
| Q4 2025 | level |
| Q1 2026 | level |
| Q2 2026 | level |

**We are not printing the counts.** Two reasons, both real. Our record of sold prices reaches back
only to Q3 2025 on this basis, so there is no year-on-year comparison to make — an earlier
comparison would measure our data collection changing, not the market. And the most recent quarter
is still filling in as settlements register, so any count we printed today would be a floor.

What we can say is the shape, and the shape is the story: **Burleigh Waters held; its neighbours did
not.** Robina and Varsity Lakes both recorded fewer sales across the same window.

Three other measures point the same way:

- **Median days on market: 29**, against 37 a year earlier
- **51.3%** of sales completed inside 30 days
- **Active house listings: 32**, down 27.3% across the month

Homes are selling faster, from a smaller pool of available stock, at a steady rate of turnover. That
is not a stalling market. It is also not a boom, and we are not going to tell you which way it goes
next.

## PAGE 5 — One sale, in full

*Evidence over assertion.*

### 2 Beaconsfield Drive

**Guide price:** Offers Over $2,250,000
**Sold:** $2,100,000
**Days on market:** 44
**Method:** Private treaty
**Settled:** 22 May 2026
**The house:** 4 bedrooms, 2 bathrooms, 4 car spaces, on 3,409m²

It asked over $2,250,000. It sold at $2,100,000 — **6.7% under the guide** — and it took 44 days to
get there, against a suburb median of 29.

We include this sale because it is the clearest illustration of something we found across the whole
quarter: **the adjustment in this market is happening at the asking price, not in the negotiation.**
Across the sales where we hold both figures, the typical home sold within half a percent of what it
was asking. The homes that took longer were the ones that started higher.

**Two things to hold about this house.** Its 3,409m² block is roughly five times the Burleigh Waters
norm, so it is not a typical sale and its price is not a guide to yours. And the "Offers Over" figure
is a guide, not a listed price — that is how much of this market is sold.

*Independently confirmed: this settlement also appears in PropRadar's records at $2,100,000 on 22 May
2026. We checked before printing it, and we checked that the home is not currently on the market.*

## PAGE 6 — If you sell, where do you go?

*The most under-served question in this market.*

Most market reports stop at the sale price. For anyone who actually intends to move, the sale is half
the transaction — and the harder half is usually the other one.

Here is what we can tell you factually about the market you would be buying into.

**Renting in between is genuinely hard.** Queensland's rental vacancy rate is around 1.0%, against a
national 1.3%. Asking rents nationally are up 8.1% over the year. There is no comfortable gap to
stand in while you look.

**Borrowing costs have moved against movers.** The cash rate has been 4.35% since May, after three
increases between February and May. The Reserve Bank next meets on 11 August 2026, and the four major
banks expect a hold. We report that expectation; we do not hold one.

**What the next rung asks.** Houses currently listed in the three suburbs we track are asking, on
average:

| Suburb | House asking | Unit asking |
|---|---|---|
| Burleigh Waters | $1,567,285 | $953,159 |
| Robina | $1,771,426 | $922,068 |
| Varsity Lakes | $1,598,489 | $925,860 |

Note that these are **asking prices of stock currently on the market**, not what homes sell for, and
they cover a different mix of properties than the sales that produced our median. The distance
between an asking figure and an achieved median is not a discount.

One pattern worth knowing if you are considering moving down rather than across: **unit asking prices
in Burleigh Waters are up 18.2% over the year, against 7.8% for houses.** The gap between the two has
been closing, not widening.

**What this page cannot tell you.** What your own borrowing capacity is at 4.35%, what transfer duty
would cost you on a specific purchase, or whether any of this is a good idea for your circumstances.
Those depend on facts about you that we do not have and would not presume.

## PAGE 7 — How to read a price estimate

*Useful to someone who will never call us.*

Every automated estimate of your home — ours included — is a model, and every model has a spread.
Here is how to read one honestly.

**A single figure hides its own uncertainty.** "$1.8 million" and "$1.65m to $1.95m" can be the same
estimate. The first sounds like knowledge. The second is knowledge.

**Ask what sits behind it.** A median drawn from 167 sales and one drawn from 17 are not comparable,
whatever their decimal places suggest. On this page and throughout this report we print the sample
size next to the number, because it is the number that tells you how much to trust the other one.

**A quarter is a small thing.** Burleigh Waters recorded roughly forty house sales last quarter. That
is enough to say what the middle price *is*; it is not enough to say reliably what it *did* since the
quarter before. We say so on page 3 rather than drawing the line anyway.

**Comparable does not mean nearby.** A house on 3,409m² and a house on 650m² on the same street are
not comparables, however close they sit.

### Four questions worth asking anyone who values your home

1. **What is the range, not the number?** If there is no range, there is no method.
2. **How many comparable sales is it built on, and from when?**
3. **Which specific properties?** The addresses should be visible to you.
4. **What would move it?** A method that cannot say what would change the answer is not a method.

We publish our own method, including its limits, at
`fieldsestate.com.au/market-intelligence/burleigh-waters`. If it does not stand up, you will be able
to tell.

## PAGE 8 — What this report can't tell you

*Credibility through disclosed limits. Carries the continuity ledger.*

### What we got wrong last quarter

**We published that sales in Burleigh Waters had fallen roughly 30%. They had not. They were flat.**

The figure came from a source whose record of recent sales decays — it captures a shrinking share of
transactions the closer you get to the present, which makes any recent quarter look emptier than it
was. We have since rebuilt the series on a combined transaction set, and Burleigh Waters shows level
activity across the last three quarters.

We also published double-digit annual growth across all three suburbs. Only Varsity Lakes reaches
double digits. Burleigh Waters is 6.9%.

Both corrections are in this issue because a report that only prints its wins is a brochure.

### What we cannot tell you this quarter

- **Any quarter-on-quarter price movement for Burleigh Waters.** One reliable quarter in six, with no
  comparable neighbour.
- **A year-on-year sales volume comparison.** Our combined record begins in Q3 2025; comparing across
  that boundary would measure our data collection, not your market.
- **What the Reserve Bank does on 11 August**, or what prices do afterwards. In July, the most hawkish
  major bank reversed a rate call inside three weeks. We publish indicators, not forecasts.
- **What your specific house is worth.** This report is about a suburb. A house needs its own
  comparables.

### How the numbers here were made

Median house price is the middle sale price across every house we recorded selling in Burleigh Waters
over the twelve months to Q2 2026, drawn from two independent sources and de-duplicated where they
overlap. Houses only — units, townhouses and duplexes are excluded. The confidence interval is
bootstrapped at 90%. Days on market is the median across 39 sales where we hold both listing and sale
dates. Months of supply comes from settlement records and is, if anything, generous — it is built on
a sales count we believe runs slightly high, which makes stock look like it clears faster than it
does.

### The standing rule

Everything in this report is also published at
`fieldsestate.com.au/market-intelligence/burleigh-waters`, and the website is the source of truth. If
this printed page and that page ever disagree, **the website is right and this page is out of date.**
