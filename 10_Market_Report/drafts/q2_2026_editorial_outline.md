# The Fields Quarterly — Q2 2026 · Editorial Outline (draft for discussion)

## The thesis (one line)
**"You're reading that the market is crashing. Your street is telling a different story."**
National cooling is real and frightening — record auction withdrawals, listings at a six-year high, Sydney and Melbourne values slipping, and a tax reform that's now law. But the southern Gold Coast is *not* the national market: double-digit annual growth, some of the tightest supply and smallest vendor discounts in the country. The honest caveats: momentum is easing, and a 2027 tax change is a genuine overhang. **Data, not reassurance for its own sake — and never advice.**

*Why this angle:* people are seeing "market down" headlines and are worried; homes are being withdrawn; owners are nervous about listing. The most useful thing we can publish is an honest, data-led read of how the national story does and doesn't apply *here* — plus what the CGT change actually means for their suburb.

---

## The data we're anchoring on

**National (the fear) — July 2026**
- Sydney dwelling values −1.2% (June), 3.7% below the Jan-2026 peak; Melbourne −1.0%, 4% below peak *(Cotality)*.
- Total listings ~131,400, **+7.7% YoY** — a six-year high; buyers gaining the upper hand *(Cotality / MPA)*.
- Auction share fell ~45% → ~30%; clearance ~43% (weakest May since 2020); **withdrawals 20.9% nationally** (Sydney 44%, Melbourne 41%) *(Domain)*.
- **National median vendor discount 3.6%** *(Cotality)*.
- CGT + negative-gearing reform **now law**; CBA cut dwelling-price-growth forecast to **3%** (from 5%) to Dec 2026.

**Southern Gold Coast (the counter-story) — our market-metrics data**
| | Median | YoY | QoQ | Absorption | Vendor discount | DOM | Withdrawn |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Robina** | $1,450,000 | +10.9% | +1.8% | 2.2 mo | 0.7% | 37.5 d | 25 |
| **Burleigh Waters** | $1,710,000 | +12.2% | −7.3%* | 3.6 mo | 0.4% | 33 d | 19 |
| **Varsity Lakes** | $1,350,000 | +15.5% | +0.8% | 1.8 mo | 2.3% | 26 d | 14 |

*\*BW's −7.3% QoQ is mean-reversion off a thin, composition-skewed Q4-2025 peak ($1.93M on 61 sales), not a trend reversal.*
- All three absorption rates are **sub-4-month = seller-favourable**; discounts **0.4–2.3% vs the national 3.6%**.
- Conviction Index composite **94.4** (below baseline — momentum cooled) *while* absorption still favours sellers. Both true; that tension is a story.
- Crash-risk = low: QLD wages +3.4% (leads prices, r=0.94) and spending $22.3B both rising; lending $12.2B falling but lags.
- GC tailwinds: values +16.8% annual (city-wide sources), supply lag, tight vacancy, population growth, 2032 Olympics; GC unit median (~$956k) has eclipsed Sydney (~$927k).
