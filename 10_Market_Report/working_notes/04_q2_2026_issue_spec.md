# Q2 2026 Issue Spec — Burleigh Waters edition

**Drafted:** 2026-08-01 · **Status:** for Will's reaction, not yet approved
**Format decided:** one report per suburb. Burleigh Waters first (highest median, steadiest data,
and the suburb whose story most contradicts the national narrative).
**Data:** `working_notes/03_q2_2026_evidence_pack.md` — every figure is what the live site serves.

---

## The lead number

**42.**

Standfirst: *"Forty-two houses sold in Burleigh Waters last quarter. Forty-four the quarter before.
The market you're reading about isn't the one on your street."*

**Why this number, against the criteria we set:**

| Criterion | Does it pass? |
|---|---|
| Reader can verify it against their own experience | Yes — they can count the sold signs |
| Describes *their position*, not our cleverness | Yes — it is their street's activity |
| Needs no definition | Yes |
| Moved meaningfully since last issue | Yes — but in the opposite direction to what everyone expects |

It also does the single most useful thing available to us this quarter: it **contradicts the story
the reader is soaked in**. National coverage says stalling; Burleigh Waters volume is flat (41 → 44
→ 42) and prices are up 6.9%. That is a genuine, defensible, counter-consensus fact, and it answers
the dominant fear ("have I missed my window?") in one line without giving advice.

**Rejected alternatives:** `$1,925,000` — it is our number, not theirs, and a median as a hero
figure invites the single-figure trap. `+6.9%` — weaker than it sounds now that the double-digit
claim is gone, and growth rates read as our analysis rather than their street.

**Note for future issues:** the lead number is chosen per issue against the criteria above. Some
quarters will lead with a sentence instead. It is a decision, not a template.

## The spine

> **Burleigh Waters didn't do what the country did.**

Prices rose, activity held, and the three suburbs of the southern Gold Coast have stopped moving
together. A reader here cannot read their own market from a national headline — or, increasingly,
from the suburb next door.

## Page plan — every page states its job

Eight pages. Not a target; it is where the content that genuinely earns a place runs out.

| # | Page | The job it does | Reader question answered |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Cover** — `42`, standfirst, one photo | Stop them. Contradict the headline they arrived with. | *Have I missed my window?* |
| **2** | **What changed this quarter** — ~250 words, signed by Will | Establish a human author with a position. Name the national story honestly, then the local counter-fact. | *Is this another agent pitch?* |
| **3** | **The number, and how sure we are** — median $1,925,000 with the $1,855,550–$2,000,000 range, n=167, and the realestate.com.au cross-check at $1,910,000 | **The trust page.** Nobody else publishes a median with its confidence interval or checks itself against a competitor in print. | *What is my house worth — and can I believe you?* |
| **4** | **What actually changed: activity, not price** — the volume series, flat | Correct the "sales halved" story the reader has absorbed, and that we ourselves published last quarter. | *Is the market falling?* |
| **5** | **One sale, in full** — a real Q2 settlement: asking, days on market, result, why | Evidence over assertion. The strongest device in the previous issue, promoted from p2 to its own page. | *Does this hold up on a real house?* |
| **6** | **What it costs to move** — equity against the cost of the next purchase at 4.35% | The **most under-served fear in the market**. Nobody writes this. It is the true blocker for movers, not the sale price. | *If I sell, can I afford to buy?* |
| **7** | **How to read a price estimate** — what a range means, what a single figure hides, the questions to ask anyone who values your home | The trust wedge. Agents rank third-least-trusted profession in Australia; this is the page that is *useful* to someone who will never call us. | *Who can I trust?* |
| **8** | **What this report can't tell you** — method, sample sizes, the quarters too thin to read, what we got wrong last quarter | Credibility through disclosed limits. Carries the continuity ledger. | *Are they honest when it costs them?* |

**Dropped:** the Fields Conviction Index. It is unfalsifiable to a reader, needs a paragraph of
explanation, and asks for trust before we have earned it. **Keep computing it** — the time series is
a genuine asset and the continuity spine — but it does not appear in this issue.

**Also dropped:** the CGT/policy spread (an investor issue in an owner-occupier report), the
three-suburb survey (this is a Burleigh Waters report), and any quarter-on-quarter median claim.

## Editorial constraints, from the data

- **No QoQ median narration.** Burleigh Waters' Q2 quarterly median carries ±7.9% and is flagged
  `reliable: false`. Page 3 uses the 12-month median only.
- **No "double-digit growth".** +6.9%. The old 12.2% was PropRadar's and is gone.
- **No "sales halved".** Volume is flat. We published −30% last quarter; page 8 says so.
- **No year-on-year volume comparison.** onthehouse only reaches back to August 2025.
- **Absorption gets a caveat or gets cut.** It derives from PropRadar counts we demoted for the
  median; the "seller's market at 3.6 months" read is probably understated.
- Standing rules apply: no advice, no forecasts, exact prices, no forbidden words.

## Continuity — what page 8 must resolve

The published Q2 issue left three questions and made claims we now know were wrong. Page 8 grades
them by name. This is the mechanism that makes each issue answerable to the last, and it starts here.

1. *"Which gives first — the seller who trims, or the buyer who returns?"* → Neither, in Burleigh
   Waters. Volume held and prices rose.
2. *"Can Burleigh Waters hold?"* → Yes, on this quarter's evidence.
3. **We got the volume wrong.** We published a 30% fall. On a fuller transaction set it was flat.
   Say so plainly and say why — a single incomplete source.

**New questions to carry into Q3:** does the flatness survive Q2 filling in? Is Varsity Lakes'
−62% real or thin-quarter noise? Does the PropRadar denominator overstate the seller's market?
