Two-reel campaign · cold + warm retargeting

Same home.
Three valuations.

Two vertical reels built on one verified, uncomfortable fact: honest valuers, following standard practice on the same home, can land more than $200,000 apart — because the answer depends on which three comparable sales they pick. Reel One lands the fact and earns the click. Reel Two turns it into a “can your estimate be trusted?” reliability check that captures a name and mobile first, and the address second. Neither claims we're more accurate — the claim is that we show the evidence the number is built on.

01

The idea & the number

The grievance isn't “estimates are wrong.” It's “I have no idea.” An owner with six estimates spanning a third of their home's value doesn't need a seventh number — they need to see what a number is built on. That's the one thing we can show and an agent's gut can't. The reel opens on the dispersion and closes on the evidence.

Where $469,000 comes from

  • 512 sold homes, $1M–$2M, Robina / Varsity Lakes / Burleigh Waters (2026-08-06 run).
  • For each, every possible 3-comparable estimate was enumerated (same type/beds/baths, land ±20%, sold in the prior 12 months).
  • Median gap between the highest and lowest defensible estimate: 32.9% = $469,000.
  • A near-perfect estimate exists in the pool ~3 times in 4 — the method just can't tell which.
  • On air we say “over $200,000” — true under every pool screen (strict $227.5K → statutory $652K), so it's unassailable while the measured median is far higher.

3·a

Presenter treatment — female talking-head

the human layer
She is an unnamed presenter, not a persona. Fields is a one-person business (Will) — so she is never introduced by name, never called a Fields “agent / valuer / consultant”, and never implies she personally valued anyone's home. She's a host reading the finding — first person plural (“we tested 512 homes”), the same voice as the site. This keeps us clear of the no-persona rule and the Property Occupations Act (no implied agent/appraisal service).

Composition: bookend (recommended). She owns the human beats — hook, challenge, CTA — and hands to full-screen data for the $469,000 proof, where a face would only compete with the number. Face → number → face is also the retention pattern in our filming rules (visual change every 2–3s, eye contact on the hook).

Recording spec (our filming rules)

  • Eye contact never breaks — the single biggest retention lever.
  • Frame mid-chest to above head, eyes in the top third; natural / neutral background.
  • Deliver slightly faster than natural, over-deliver energy a touch.
  • Text from frame 1 (most watch muted), white + Fields green/copper, change every 2–4s.
  • Record beats in short bursts; jump-cut between them; 5–10% push-in on the hook.

Alternative compositions

  • Full-frame throughout — she stays on screen over every beat, data as overlays around her. Most human, but the $469k has to share the frame.
  • Corner PiP — data is hero, she's a small persistent insert. Keeps the number dominant; lower trust than full face.
  • Bookend ✓ — the recommended cut above.
3·b

How we source her — the decision

Real presenter
Filmed host

A real person (creator / contractor) delivers the lines. Most authentic, zero impersonation risk, works muted-or-not. Needs casting + a short shoot. recommended

AI-generated
Veo 3 / avatar

Synthetic presenter with synced speech. Fast & cheap, but uncanny risk and must not read as a real Fields staffer — generic host register only, and disclosure if it looks like a real person.

Licensed UGC
Creator marketplace

Brief a Meta/creator-marketplace creator to shoot it. Real face, native Reels feel, licensed — middle ground on cost and turnaround.

This is the one call I can't make for you: it changes the next build step (a casting/shoot brief vs a Veo/avatar talking-head prompt). Whichever we pick, she stays an unnamed host — never “your Fields valuer”.

2·b

The 30-second test — it must be real

What the test asks

  • Do you currently have an online estimate?
  • Approximately when was the home last renovated?
  • Does the estimate show the comparable sales behind it?
  • Enter the property address to examine it.

What it returns (tangible)

  • Reliability: Low — “Your estimate does not expose enough evidence to assess its reliability.”
  • or “We found enough local sales to perform a more detailed comparison.”
⚠ A real diagnostic, not a decorative quiz. The answer must not always resolve to “contact an agent.” The reliability read has to reflect the actual inputs — whether an estimate discloses its comps, and whether we have enough local sales to compare — or the promise in the hook is broken. Keep the read evidence-based (does it show its working), never a swipe at a named site.

not different ads

Prediction: most attention — visually immediate & credible.

Prediction: more provocative, slightly more confrontational.
Variant C · Direct challenge

“How accurate is your home's online estimate? Take the 30-second test.”

Prediction: most submissions — action & effort are explicit.
Hybrid · likely winner

“Three websites. One home. Three different values. Take the 30-second test to see whether yours can be trusted.”

Combines A's credibility with C's explicit action.

04

Compliance pre-flight (Rule 5)

✓No single figure as “your home is worth X.” The number on screen is a gap — exactly the differences/savings framing ads are cleared to show.
✓Landing page shows methodology + confidence disclaimer — /analyse-your-home does. Mandatory pre-flight for a $ claim is met.
✓No advice, no predictions. The reel states a measured fact and shows evidence; the viewer draws the conclusion.
✓Source never named. “Our test” / “homes that sold near you” — never “Domain” / “onthehouse”.
✕Never claim we're “more accurate.” Our own 512-home run retracts that — it's falsifiable. The claim is determinacy & disclosed evidence, not accuracy.
✕Forbidden words — no “stunning”, “nestled”, “boasting”, “rare opportunity”, “robust market”.
⚠ Why we say “over $200,000”, not a single measured figure. The spread depends entirely on which comparable pool you screen on. Every one of the three measured pools below exceeds $200,000 — so “over $200,000” is true no matter which screen a critic applies, while the actual measured median sits far above it.
