# E4 — What is here that we have not realised we have?

This is the open-ended one. No target area. The question is:

**What does this business already possess — data, code, capability, or accumulated artefact — whose
value it has not recognised or is not using?**

We have spent months building infrastructure: property data across ~40,000 Gold Coast cadastral
records, sold histories, comparable-sales valuations, floor-plan and satellite image analysis, editorial
generation, market metrics with confidence intervals, off-market discovery decks at scale, analytics,
several research "brains" over external corpora, and a large body of fix-history and internal briefs.

Assets are routinely built for one purpose and left there. Look for:

- **Data we hold that nobody queries.** Collections written by a pipeline and read by nothing. Fields
  computed and never rendered. Check what actually reads a collection before concluding it is unused —
  but a genuinely unread dataset with real information in it is a finding.
- **A capability built for one narrow use that generalises.** Something scoped to one suburb, one
  property type, or one page that would work across all of them for little extra effort.
- **An asymmetry no competitor has.** Something we can compute that a normal agency cannot, because we
  have the data or the pipeline and they do not. Be specific about why it is hard to copy.
- **A question our data can answer that nobody is asking.** Especially a question a homeowner would
  find genuinely useful and cannot get answered anywhere else.
- **Work that is nearly finished and abandoned.** Something at 80% that would pay off if completed —
  check for parked or superseded projects that were parked for reasons that no longer hold.
- **An accumulated by-product with unrecognised value.** Logs, transcripts, decision records, or
  analytics that were a means to an end and are themselves informative.

Two disciplines matter here more than anywhere else in this battery:

1. **Verify the asset exists and holds what you think.** Count the documents. Read a sample. An
   imagined asset is the easiest thing in the world to invent and the most expensive to chase.
2. **Say why it is valuable in business terms** — how it leads to a homeowner enquiry, saves money, or
   creates something a competitor cannot match. "This is interesting data" is not a finding.

Rank by expected value. One genuinely non-obvious, verified, high-value observation beats six
plausible ones.
