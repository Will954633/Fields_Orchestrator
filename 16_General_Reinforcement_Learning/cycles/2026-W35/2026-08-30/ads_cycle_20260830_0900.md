## 2. The numbers, with denominators

`ads_signal.py` (14d): **$796.08 spend · 17 form conversions · 0 test-market · blended
$46.83/conversion.** But blended cost-per-*conversion* is the wrong metric for this domain.
The objective is cost per identified **seller**. Classifying all 17 by what the lead
actually told us:

| Bucket | n | Spend | Cost each |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Owner + own home address** (Owner Market form) | 4 | $105 | **$26.20** |
| **Stated `selling_intent = yes`** (Home Owner Funnel) | 1 of 2 leads | $199 | **$199.00** |
| Buyers (Carousel + Subscriber forms) | 8 | ~$202 | — (not the objective) |
| **Intent unknowable** (Narratives: name+phone, no qualifier) | 3 | $145 incl. WhatsApp arm | unclassifiable |

**The finding: asking for the ADDRESS costs ~7.6× less per identified seller than asking
for selling INTENT — $26.20 vs $199.00.**

**Denominators are n=4 and n=1. This is directional, not statistical**, and I am not going
to dress it otherwise. What raises it above one cycle's noise is that an *independent*
dataset says the same thing: `reward_ledger.py` over n=13 conversions ranks
`submitted_address` as the ★reward milestone at **53.1× lift** and `searched_address` at
**45.7×** — far above every other milestone measured. Two unrelated measurements pointing at
the same mechanic (a concrete self-interested ask beats a commitment/threat ask) is the
reason I believe it; the paid n alone would not be.

Three caveats I am not hiding:
- **17 submissions ≠ 17 people.** `Mkwoodrick@yahoo.com` ("Michele") submitted twice across
  two campaigns with different phone numbers, inflating the Carousel arm by one.
- **2 of the 4 "Burleigh Waters" owners live in Burleigh Heads** — the ad's targeting spills,
  which is exactly what broke the resolver (§3).
- **$145 bought 3 contacts we cannot classify at all.** The Narratives forms capture name +
  phone and ask nothing about intent, yet sit in `SELLER_FORM_IDS`, so they are announced as
  sellers on zero evidence. That is a deliberate 08-28 choice (it beat a contactless generic
  alert) — I flag it as a *measurement* hole, not a bug, and left it alone.

## 3. What I did autonomously

### Fixed: the per-suburb ad was overriding the address the person typed

`om_needs_manual_link: True` on 3 of 4 paid address leads. All three were texted *"I'm
finalising your link and will text it through shortly."* Only Marjorie ever got a follow-up,
and only because Will did it by hand.

Two compounding faults in `03_Facebook/Owner_Market_Carousel/owner_market_sms.py`:

1. It passed **the ad's** suburb to the resolver, not the address's. `2/9 cedar court
   Burleigh Heads` was searched inside `burleigh_waters` — it sits in
   `Gold_Coast.address_search_index` **verbatim** under `suburb_key: burleigh_heads`.
2. Token-strict matching: `6 Campelle st varsity` missed `6 CAMPELLES AVENUE VARSITY LAKES`
   on a plural and an abbreviation. Also in the index, exactly.

**Neither was a coverage gap. We already held both properties.** Per Rule 8 I verified that
before writing it, rather than concluding "not in our data".

Added `resolve_address_locality()`: unit-aware street-number parse (`2/9` → `9`),
singular/plural name variants, structured query on `street_no`/`street_name`, and scoring
where **the suburb the person wrote outranks the suburb the ad was for** (the form's suburb
survives only as the weakest tiebreak and as the no-match fallback — previous behaviour).

Tested against all four real leads plus the built-in test address:

| Typed | Result |
|---|---|
| `2/9 cedar court Burleigh Heads` | ✅ now → `burleigh_heads`, canonical row |
| `6 Campelle st varsity` | ✅ now → `6 CAMPELLES AVENUE VARSITY LAKES` |
| `21 Gardendale crescent` | ✅ unchanged (no regression) |
| `10 Heidelberg Circuit, Robina` | ✅ unchanged (no regression) |
| `18 glauca street burleigh heads` | ❌ still unresolved — **honest limit:** Glauca St runs 1–15 in the index; number 18 is genuinely absent. Real data/typo gap, not papered over. |
