# Prices are falling across the country. Will 5 Chantilly Place fall too?

## Southern markets have been falling for four months and Brisbane, previously positive, just tipped down in July

The falls are real where they are being measured. Cotality's national Home Value Index fell -0.7% in July 2026, its largest monthly decline since December 2022, with Sydney down -1.4%, Melbourne -1.2%, Brisbane -0.6% and Adelaide -0.2% over the month (Cotality Home Value Index, July 2026). The RBA cash rate stands at 4.35%, raised in February, March and May 2026 and held at the 16 June meeting (RBA, 16 June 2026). Headline CPI rose 3.8% over the year to June 2026 and the trimmed mean 3.6% (ABS, June quarter 2026). The Westpac-Melbourne Institute House Price Expectations Index fell -8% in July 2026 to a three-year low, though 47% of respondents still expected rises (Westpac-Melbourne Institute, July 2026). That is a fair picture of a national market under pressure.

## The Key Question: Property markets are declining in major national cities; will the value of 5 Chantilly Place decline too?

It follows that a local owner might fear the same. So let us work carefully through the data and read it the way an analyst would — what that national move does, and does not, say about 5 Chantilly Place. This piece answers in four steps, from your home outward: whether its own estimate is falling now, what Robina as a whole is doing, why the two can move differently, and which forces could shape its value from here. It does not tell you what to do; none of it is a forecast; and where the data runs out, it says so.

## 2. What is Robina doing?

Your home is one data point. The suburb around it is the next ring out, and it is measured two ways that our own audits found hold up on a partial sample: the rolling median price, and how long homes take to sell.

On price, the Robina rolling 12-month house median has **risen +5.8%** year-on-year, across 265 sales matched between Domain and onthehouse.

**And the trajectory of your own home agrees with the suburb: over the same 18 months your home's estimate moved +10.9% and the suburb median +9.2% — the same direction.**

**Your home has been moving with its suburb, not against it.**

Here an analyst pauses. Both price figures above point up — the Robina median over the year, your own home over the last eighteen months — but a price is a lagging number: it confirms what buyers have already done, not what they are about to do. The more forward-looking signal is liquidity — how quickly homes are selling — and it has begun to move: Robina's median time on market has stretched to 34 days last quarter, from around half that a year ago. In the research, this side turns first. US Federal Reserve work published in 2025 found the supply of homes for sale leads price growth by roughly a year, and housing-search studies find that as demand eases, homes take longer to sell before the median itself gives way.

So a market where prices are still rising while time on market lengthens is one whose early momentum may be easing — a signal to watch, not a fall. And 34 days is still a quick sale, under the 35-day mark that usually separates a sellers' market from a balanced one; the honest reading is heat coming out of a fast market, not a market in retreat.

**Price figures alone cannot tell you where things go next — they arrive too late.**

For that you have to look underneath them, at the demand that drives a market: who is moving here, whether there is work, and what people can afford. That is where this piece turns next.

## 3. Why is Robina holding up differently?

So two records point different ways at once: nationally, values are falling; Robina's median has risen +5.8% over the year. The question an analyst asks next is why — and the answer is not in the price figures at all, but in who is moving, where the work is, and what the same money buys. Read what follows as context, not a promise about what comes next.

### Who is moving

Queensland recorded a net interstate migration gain of 21,595 people in 2024–25 — the largest of any state — with the biggest inflows from New South Wales (+13,020) and Victoria (+6,920); New South Wales alone accounted for 60.3% of that net gain (Queensland Government Statistician's Office, from ABS, 2024–25). Queensland has recorded a net interstate migration gain in every quarter since June 1981 — the only state to do so (Queensland Government Statistician's Office, from ABS, to 2025). The Gold Coast was among Australia's fastest-growing regions, expanding 2.3% in 2023–24 (Centre for Population, from ABS, 2023–24).
In 2023–24 the Gold Coast's own growth leaned more on overseas than interstate migration; the interstate flow is unambiguous at the Queensland level. The interstate inflow is strongly positive but has moderated from its 2020–21 pandemic peak. Population is the demand side of housing; these are the flows behind it. A place people are moving to, and the places they are leaving, are not under the same pressure.

### Where the work is

Labour demand is strongest here on a per-person basis. For every 1,000 people employed, Queensland has about 23 job vacancies open, against 21 in New South Wales and 20 in Victoria (ABS Job Vacancies, Australia (cat. 6354.0), 2026 Q2). Melbourne's unemployment, around 5.0% over the three months to July 2026, sits about a point above Queensland's (4.1%) and Sydney's (4.2%) (ABS Labour Force, Australia (cat. 6202.0)). Queensland added about 56,000 jobs over the year to July 2026.

### What the same money buys

Robina's median house, about $1,490,000, sits on a 655 m² block; this home on 907 m², 4.6 km from the beach (Fields records). The same money in Sydney is below the city's median house price. Where it buys a house at all, it is an outer estate: in The Ponds, about 34 km from the CBD, the median sold house is around $1,525,000 on about 413 m² (onthehouse sold records, 18 sales). The blocks are the fact; the beach and the commute are the context.

For a buyer moving north, the equity that reaches an outer-fringe block in Sydney reaches more land, closer to the water, here.

None of this forecasts a price. It sets out the demand side — people, work, value — that a national average cannot see, and leaves the reading to you. ## 4. What will happen in the future?

No one can tell you what your home will be worth next year, and anyone who names a figure is selling you a guess. But unknowable is not the same as unreadable. A market leaves a trail before it moves, and the honest way to read where it is heading is to watch the signals that turn first — not the price, which you have already seen arrives late.

So we did the work. Across our analysis of 27 economic series across eight Gold Coast suburbs, 2015–2025, we set out to separate the indicators that lead Robina's prices from the ones that only confirm them, and the answer runs against what most people watch. The number on every front page — interest rates — is a lagging one: interest rates follow Gold Coast prices by about a year (they lag, at r = 0.79) — the Reserve Bank moves in response to the economy, not ahead of any one suburb. What leads, in that data, is money in people's pockets: household spending is the single strongest gauge of market strength here (r = 0.91); Queensland wage growth has run about three to four months ahead of house prices; retail spending, a proxy for confidence, has led by four to five months. And underneath all of it, a 1% rise in real household income has, over the period, moved Gold Coast prices about 1.7% (Fields, [What drives Gold Coast house prices](https://fieldsestate.com.au/articles/what-drives-gold-coast-house-prices)).

So the question worth asking is not where prices go next, but what those leading indicators are doing now. Two of them we can read straight from the Bureau of Statistics for Queensland, and both are below — each as the change on a year earlier.

Wage growth — the indicator with the longest lead in our analysis — has eased over the last two years, from the high fours to about 3.4% a year (ABS Wage Price Index, 2026 Q2). It is still positive, but no longer accelerating. In our data, accelerating wages preceded price growth three to four months on, and fading wages preceded softer conditions; this is neither — it is flat.

Household spending — the strongest gauge of market strength in our analysis, and a proxy for the confidence that precedes a purchase — has held up, running about 6.1% a year (ABS Monthly Household Spending Indicator, June 2026). Where pay growth has eased, the till has not.

Set the two against each other — pay growth easing, spending holding — and beside the roughly 23 job vacancies per thousand workers and the migration north from the last section, and you are looking at the forward part of the picture in one place. We draw no arrow from it.

Closer in, your own house is what a buyer here weighs directly: a 907 sqm block, a pool, and a position our model reads at a +11.0% premium to the suburb — the features the comparable sales show buyers paying up for.

## What this can't tell you

We publish this method's mean absolute error: about 10.5% in this price range. Eight sales sit behind your home's estimate -- a small number, stated plainly so you can weigh it. The Robina median rests on 265 recorded sales, which is a sample of the suburb's activity rather than all of it. Its 90% confidence range runs $1,450,000 to $1,550,000. Sales within 2.0 km of one home, over 6 months, cannot show you a whole market or what any single buyer would do.
