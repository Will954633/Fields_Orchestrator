# What the evidence actually supports

**Compiled:** 2026-08-08 · For the direct-mail programme to `/off-market` engagers and homeowners.

This is the foundation the strategy options rest on. It exists because the last plan in this folder
was costed on a response rate that does not exist.

**Evidence sources, in descending order of trust:**

| Tier | Source | Why it ranks there |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Randomised controlled trials — Opower/Allcott, Gerber & Green GOTV, FCA OP12, Cochrane/Edwards | Real control groups, real behavioural outcomes, published methods |
| 2 | JICMAIL (UK) — ~5,000 campaigns, matchback / unique code / test-and-control | Industry-funded but audited, campaign-level, denominators disclosed |
| 3 | Postal-operator research (Royal Mail, Canada Post, Australia Post) | Real studies, but the funder sells mail, and most measure *attention*, not response |
| 4 | Coaching corpus (Brain 1) + YouTube channels (eXp, BLAC SALT) | Practitioner assertion. Useful for *method and language*. Never for whether a channel works |
| 5 | Trade-press statistics and vendor pages | Assume fabricated until traced. Several were |

## 1. Numbers that failed verification — do not use any of these again

| Claim | Where it came from | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **"Direct mail gets 4.4%"** | Attributed to the ANA/DMA Response Rate Report | The figure **is not in the report**. Traces to DMA **2012**. Already retracted once, in the lead-funnel verdict §4.6, and it was **still live in `00_SCOPING.md` until today** |
| "9% house / 4.9% prospect" | ANA | From the **2018** edition, superseded and re-dated ever since |
| ANA 2023's actual DM rates (15.6% / 10.8%) | ANA 2023 | **N=26 and N=25**, self-reported, ~21% using actual metrics. The report's own text: *"should not be considered benchmark data"* and it had *"insufficient data to report … by format"* |
| **"VDP personalisation lifts response 2–3×"** | Everywhere | No retrievable primary source. Circulating variants: 36% / 135% / 300% / 500% |
| "Oversized envelopes / dimensional mail pull best" | ANA by format | The ANA report explicitly says it **could not compute this** |
| **"80% of sales happen between the 5th and 12th contact"** | Sales training | Traceable — to a **1942 survey, n<40**, about *in-person* sales calls. SMEI, the successor body it is credited to, publishes it as an example of a statistic you should not trust |
| **"It takes 7 touches"** | Everywhere | Descends from Thomas Smith, *Successful Advertising*, **1885** — who said **twenty**. The only measured link in the chain (Krugman 1972) says **three** |
| "33 touches" / "12×/year doubles ROI per NAR" / "8.4 contacts" | Real-estate coaching and mail vendors | Keller's 2004 book; the NAR publication **does not exist**; the 8.4 has no source of any kind |
| "$503 cost per lead, per a NAR survey of 5,400 professionals" | 2026 real-estate listicles | **Fabricated.** No such NAR publication. The category's 2026 search results are substantially AI-generated |
| "Fridge magnets: 78% recall after 12 months" | Magnet manufacturers | Vendor claim. No RCT, no holdout, no panel measurement exists |

⚠ **The folklore is wrong in an expensive direction.** "80% between the 5th and 12th" says yield
concentrates *late*, which is precisely the argument for paying for touches 5–12. Every controlled
measurement shows the opposite shape. If the programme is losing money at touch 4, nothing in the
evidence suggests touches 5–12 rescue it.

## 2. Numbers that survived

### 2.1 Response — the governing arithmetic

JICMAIL Response Rate Tracker, FY2025, ~5,000 campaigns, responses measured by matchback, unique
tracking code or test-and-control:

| List | Response | ROI | What 1,000 pieces buys |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cold addressed** | **0.9%** | £3.20 per £1 | **~9 responses** |
| Warm / house list | **7.2%** | £9.00–£11.50 | ~72 responses |
| Unaddressed door drop | 0.5–0.6% | £2.90 | ~5 responses |

**Cold CPA is roughly 8× warm CPA.** At Australian postage this is the number the whole programme
has to survive: 1,000 cold pieces at ~$1.10 (C5 Promo Post) is ~$1,100 for ~9 responses, i.e.
**~$122 per response in postage alone**, before print, data, artwork or conversion.

**Consequence:** the single highest-leverage decision available is *not* creative, format or
cadence. It is **whether the list is cold or warm.** Everything that moves an address from cold to
warm is worth more than everything else on this page combined.

### 2.2 The finding this programme should be built on

Mail carrying **the recipient's own record** behaves completely differently from mail carrying an
argument.

| Evidence | Design | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Opower home energy reports** — Allcott 2011 (*J Public Econ*), Allcott & Rogers 2014 (*AER*) | 600,000+ households randomised; recurring personalised multi-page report about **their own home**, mailed repeatedly | **1.4–3.3% sustained behaviour change**, **no habituation across 60 consecutive months**, effects persist after mailing stops (decaying 10–20%/yr) |
| **Gerber & Green**, 85 studies / 220 comparisons | Generic mail vs mail showing the household **its own record** | Generic **~0.5–0.75pp**. Own-record ("Self") mailer **+4.9pp** — roughly **10×** |
| **FCA Occasional Paper 12** (Dec 2015), RCT, **300,000+ insurance customers** | Letter containing **last year's premium** — a number about *that* recipient | **+3.2pp (11–18% relative)**. Simplification, bullet points and helpful leaflets produced **nothing**. A reminder sent two weeks later produced **no significant effect** |

Opower is the closest structural analogue that exists to what Fields would be doing, it is the
best-quality evidence in the whole file, and it says the format works and does not wear out.

**Content specificity beats frequency by roughly 16×.** That single sentence should govern the
budget: money spent making each piece more specifically about *that address* returns far more than
money spent sending more pieces.

### 2.3 Frequency and sequence length

| Finding | Source | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| **Diminishing returns after ~6 mailings per household** | Gerber & Green, New Haven 1999 — the only experiment that deliberately extended a sequence to find its ceiling (up to 8 pieces) | RCT |
| +0.5pp per additional mailer, **linear over 1–3**, no early saturation | Gerber & Green, New Haven 1998, n=31,098 | RCT |
| **Repetition of *persuasion* content does not accumulate at all** — 5, 9 and 12-piece advocacy sequences produced null or *negative* results | Cardy 2005; Gerber/Green/Green 2003; Cubbison 2015 | RCT |
| **One mailing per month per sender** is the average stated tolerance ceiling; over-mailing hurts asymmetrically more than under-mailing helps | van Diepen, Donkers & Franses, ERIM 2006, n=213 | Survey |
| A sender's own mailings act as **short-term substitutes, ~63% cannibalisation** | van Diepen et al., *JMR* 2009, three Dutch charities | Observational, controlled |
| **Spacing exposures beats bunching them** — even when spreading moves them further from the purchase occasion | Sahni, *QME* 2015 (Dick Wittink prize) | Field experiment |
| **Repeat sends to the same addresses: 2.5× response, +37% ROI** vs one-off campaigns | JICMAIL FY2025 | Campaign panel |
| Mail stays live in-home **7.6 days**, handled **4.6 times** over 28 days; modelled response peaks around **three weeks** after drop | JICMAIL panel; BBC TV-licence curve via Recast | Panel / model |

**Verdict on weekly.** Weekly is not supportable by anything found. It stacks pieces 2 and 3 inside
piece 1's own response window, so the result cannot be read; ~63% of what piece 2 "earns" was piece
1's anyway; and it breaches the only measured tolerance figure by a factor of four.
**Fortnightly-to-monthly is the defensible band, and monthly is the safer end of it.**

The premise in `Houses_Surrounding_A_Just_Listed.md` — *"Send an update every 2 weeks"* — sits at
the aggressive edge of the defensible band. It is arguable **only** because each piece would carry
genuinely new information about a live event (see the options doc), not a repeated argument.

⚠ **The biggest open question in the whole evidence base:** the ~6-piece ceiling was measured over
*weeks* in an election run-up, not over *months*. Whether the ceiling is per-sequence or per-window
is unknown. Nobody has tested it.

### 2.4 Format

| Finding | Source | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| **Postcards underperform.** Double postcard vs one page in an envelope: **OR 0.47** — roughly halved response | Cochrane / Edwards 2009, RCT | RCT (single trial, survey context) |
| Canada Post's own neuroscience ranked the **postcard worst of five physical formats** and *below* email-on-a-smartphone | Canada Post / True Impact 2015, n=270 | Lab, postal-funded |
| **Handwritten address: OR 1.25** (7 trials, n=5,091, low heterogeneity). Hand-signed: OR 1.24 | Cochrane / Edwards 2009 | RCT meta-analysis |
| **Stamp vs franking: null** (OR 0.95, ~14,000 participants) — a well-powered null | Cochrane / Edwards 2009 | RCT meta-analysis |
| **Booklet vs stapled pages: no effect** (OR 1.10, CI 0.99–1.23). Binding spend does not buy response | Cochrane / Edwards 2009, 3 trials, n=5,681 | RCT meta-analysis |
| **QR realistic scan rate: plan 1–3%.** JICMAIL's measured ceiling is **9.4% of all mail items prompting a website visit by any route** | JICMAIL | Panel |

**The envelope decision has four independent lines behind it, two of them controlled** — the
Cochrane RCT above, Canada Post's own neuroscience ranking the postcard worst of five formats, an
Australian operator watching bare flyers stay in the letterbox while his enveloped piece *"made it
inside"*, and Brain 1's *"personalized letters in an envelope that go to an owner continue to be
another source of getting your appraisals"*, set explicitly against bulk unaddressed DLs. **This is
the best-supported single decision in the whole project.**

**So:** a personally addressed **envelope** containing a short, personalised, unbound multi-page
piece. Hand-address and hand-sign at pilot scale. Do not buy binding. Do not use stamps. **Do not
use a postcard** — and note that a postcard is what `00_SCOPING.md` currently proposes.

**The piece must work without the QR.** At 1–3%, a design whose value is gated behind the scan is a
design that fails for 97% of recipients.

### 2.5 Australian specifics

| Fact | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **Promo Post ~$0.825–1.10** per article same-state, **minimum 4,000 pieces** · PreSort **$1.530–1.895** · unbarcoded **$2.615** (a 138% penalty) · unaddressed ~$0.405 | Barcoding is worth more than any creative decision. The 4,000 minimum is easily met across three suburbs — but not by a 200-piece pilot, which pays PreSort rates |
| **A photo of their home requires addressed mail.** "Letterbox drop" and "their own house on the front" are mutually exclusive | The concept as originally scoped is an *addressed* campaign, at 3–4× the cost |
| In Queensland, ignoring a "No Junk Mail" sign is a **statutory offence** (s107, Waste Reduction and Recycling Act) | Unaddressed drops carry an unmeasurable legal hole. Addressed mail does not |
| **45% of Australian sellers find their agent by researching online**, 39% referral, **10% via marketing material** (InfoTrack/REA 2024) | AU sellers are less referral-locked than US sellers — which favours the search-led model Fields already has working |
| **There is no Australian direct-mail response data.** Australia Post's flagship mail study is from **2013** and measures attention only (99% opened, 58% read thoroughly, ~3 min) — **no response data** | Everything above is extrapolated from UK/US. **Fields' own holdout test would quickly be the best Australian data in existence.** That is worth something in itself |

## 3. What the practitioners add — and its standing

The coaching corpus and the two YouTube channels contain **zero measured response rates for any
printed piece**. Across ~2.5 million words of transcript there is one costed physical asset with no
result attached, one volume figure with the result truncated away, and an explicit admission that
*"many agents never implement the systems in order to track the results from the money being spent
on the mailers."* Read the whole category as **survivor testimony**: 80 of 100 new agents quit
within two weeks, and every success quote comes from someone who did not.

What is still worth taking:

**A convergence worth noting.** Steve Robertson (eXp) independently arrives at **six mailers a year
to ≤750 homes** — the same number as the Gerber & Green ceiling, reached from the opposite
direction. That is not proof, but it is the only place coaching doctrine and RCT evidence agree on
a figure.

**Farm size.** 1,000–2,000 homes is the strongest multi-voice finding in Brain 1 (~10 separately
named agents), with the failure mode named repeatedly: *"I was trying to dominate too big of an
area… four suburbs… 20,000 properties."* Fields currently has **26,297 decks and 0 homes touched** —
maximum breadth at zero depth, the exact inverse.

**Pick the farm by turnover, not by affection** (Brad Korn) — *"that gated community with 50, 60,
100 homes… there's no more than five or six of them selling a year. That is not a neighborhood you
want to spend your time marketing to."* Fields can compute annual turnover per street from its own
sold data. **This is a query, not a guess.**

**The Law of Because.** *"I will only call people with a strong because"* — the script being *"The
reason I'm calling is because number 27 Smith Street has just come onto the market."* Fields holds
every sale event in four suburbs plus 53,313 historical events, so it can manufacture a genuine,
specific, data-backed reason to write to any address, automatically. This is the one place the data
asset maps exactly onto a well-attested mechanism.

**The near-unanimous warning.** Single-shot "just listed / just sold" cards are described as a known
money-loser by the people who sell farming systems: *"the postcard just ends up in the local
landfill"*; *"do not waste money on sending out one card… and then you never send another card
again."* And from a recipient's side: *"I literally grab the mail and I go stand in front of the
trash can… the other one is just like every door delivered one-page glossy realtor mail."*

**Mail alone is rejected from inside the mail doctrine.** *"They still work. But they work in
combination, not in isolation."* And what mail does is warm, not close: *"Prospecting does not get
you listings, it gets you contacts that one day will become a listing."*

**Frequency to the same list beats reach — stated directly, and it matches JICMAIL.** *"You are
better off doing 5,000 letterbox drops a week to the same people each week than just doing one
person once in a month and doing 20,000 in a whole area."* That is the same shape as JICMAIL's
measured **2.5× response and +37% ROI for repeat sends to the same addresses**, arrived at
independently. It is one of only two places where doctrine and controlled data agree.

**The only per-drop conversion figure in the entire corpus** is a single anecdote: *"I dropped about
a thousand and I got quite a few appraisals but three listings out of it"* — **0.3%**, with the
appraisals left unquantified. Every other figure quoted (2% appraisal strike rate, "90% conversion",
60–70% market share) has a **different denominator**, and several count people who had already
engaged. **Do not average them.** The one figure with a real sample size is external to the
coaching corpus: **homes advertised both in print and online are 20% more likely to sell**, across
**503,000 properties** — but that concerns *listing advertising*, not farming mail.

**Quitting early is the most-named failure mode**, which is exactly what makes the category
unfalsifiable: *"whatever you do, you've got to do it for 12 months. None of this I'll try it for
two"* · *"why they give it up is they do it, and then they do it, and then they don't get a direct
call from it."* The lag curve is the most internally consistent claim in the corpus: **0–3 months no
ROI · 3–6 months appraisal calls begin · 6 months+ sole-of-mind.**

**⚠ Two standing warnings on this corpus.** 81% of the carried evidence in the direct-mail query
came from a single source (RealEstate_Gym / Tom Panos), and the tool's own quote verifier scored
that brief at **79.2% fidelity — 4 misattributed citations and 7 quotes found in no unit.** Several
of the "fabricated" strings are section headings the model wrote rather than invented quotes, but
**anything quoted from Brain 1 into public-facing work must be re-verified against the cited unit
first.**

**Time horizon, stated flatly.** *"When I'm thinking about farming, I'm thinking about the next
three to five years. I'm not thinking about three to five days."* Brain 1's own clusters: 3–6 months
to a first inbound appraisal request, ~2 years to a named household listing with you.

## 4. The line — where personalisation becomes intrusion

One finding sets a hard boundary and it comes from the strongest study in the set.

Gerber & Green measured **own-record** mail at **+4.9pp** and **neighbour-record** mail at
**+8.1pp** — the neighbour version is markedly more effective **and it generated documented opt-out
backlash and complaints.** The more powerful lever is the one that damages the relationship.

**The rule that follows:** personalise about the **property**, using public data, never about the
**person**. "Four homes sold near 20 Heidelberg Circuit" is property data. "We noticed you were
looking at your home's page for three minutes on Tuesday night" is surveillance, however true and
however well intentioned.

This bears directly on the trigger design: **behavioural triggering is legitimate; behavioural
disclosure is not.** What we know may decide *whether* we write. It must never appear *in* what we
write.

## 5. The five design instructions this evidence yields

1. **Warm beats everything.** 0.9% → 7.2% is an 8× swing, larger than any creative, format or
   cadence effect measured anywhere in this file. Design the programme so the list gets warmer.
2. **Each piece carries new data about *their* address.** Not a repeated argument — repetition of
   persuasion measures null; repetition of own-record data does not habituate over 60 months.
3. **Monthly, not weekly. Six pieces, then stop and read the result.** Fortnightly is defensible
   only where each piece reports a genuinely new real-world event.
4. **Addressed envelope, unbound, hand-signed. No postcard, no binding, no stamps.** Works without
   the QR.
5. **Hold out a control group from day one.** No one — not JICMAIL, not the ANA, not a single
   coach, not one Australian operator — has published a controlled test of homeowner farming mail.
   Without a holdout the programme is unfalsifiable by construction, which is exactly the critique
   that sinks the whole category.
