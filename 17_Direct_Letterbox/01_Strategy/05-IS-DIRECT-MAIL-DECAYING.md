# Is direct mail decaying — or is it one proposition that died?

**2026-08-08** · Written in response to: *"An Australian operator says the 'what's your home worth'
letter has decayed because the number is now free online. Does the data speak to anything that IS
working, or has the industry found nothing — and is that why direct mail is failing?"*

**Short answer: the channel is not decaying. One proposition inside it died, the data names the
mechanism precisely, and the thing that replaces it is the best-evidenced format in the whole file.**

---

## 1. The channel-level numbers are flat, not collapsing

JICMAIL, the only measured series that exists (~5,000 campaigns, matchback / unique code /
test-and-control):

| | FY2024 | FY2025 | Move |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cold addressed** | 0.9% | **0.9%** | **flat** |
| Warm / house list | 7.9% | **7.2%** | −9% |
| Unaddressed door drop | 0.6% | 0.5% | −17% |

And in the other direction, in the same dataset: **retail cold and warm DM response rates grew
+21% and +26% year on year.** Whatever is happening, "direct mail is dying" is not what the numbers
show. Cold prospecting mail has been mediocre (0.9%) and *stayed* mediocre.

The one structural change worth knowing is operational, not behavioural: **Australia Post
discontinued Clean Mail on 1 July 2025** (that volume now lodges as unbarcoded PreSort) and
introduced a Letters Lodgement Correction Fee in March 2025. Cost is moving. Response is not.

---

## 2. What actually died — and the research names the same mechanism the operator did

The Australian operator's version:

> *"a lot of people can find out roughly what their home is worth very quickly by lots of different
> websites and so needing an agent to give them a price update for the most part is it's not really
> what people are after anymore."*

The research arrived at the same conclusion independently, and gave it a name — **self-service
leakage**:

> *"If the mailer's value proposition is 'a number,' and you deliver the number, the transaction is
> complete. You have taught the homeowner that the number is free and commoditised."*
> — `../02_Research/02_Web/W5-real-estate-farming-reality-check.md`

**That is not a channel failure. It is an inventory failure.** The price-drive letter worked because
the agent held something the homeowner could not get. Zillow, PropTrack, Domain and every AVM took
that inventory away. The letter became a slower, less convenient version of a free website — and it
would have decayed identically had it been an email, a phone call or a knock on the door.

The medium is fine. The agent ran out of scarce goods.

---

## 3. What IS working — and it is one family, not a scatter

Every mail intervention in the research file with a real control group and a real effect belongs to
the same category: **mail that carries a record only the sender holds, delivered as a cadence.**

| Intervention | Design | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Opower home energy reports** | 600,000+ households randomised (6.2M receiving by 2013); recurring multi-page report about **their own home** | **1.4–3.3% sustained change. No habituation after 60 consecutive months.** Effects decay only 10–20%/yr after mail *stops*. **Treatment effects in years 3–5 are 50–60% stronger if continued.** Still working at year five |
| **Gerber & Green "Self" mailer** | Household shown **its own voting record**, 85 studies / 220 comparisons | **+4.9pp** vs **<0.5pp** for generic mail — ~10× |
| **FCA Occasional Paper 12** | RCT, **300,000+** insurance customers; letter carrying **last year's premium** | **+3.2pp (11–18% relative).** Simplification, bullets, helpful leaflets: **nothing.** A reminder two weeks later: **nothing** |
| **Health "your results" mailings** | Mailed FIT kits, HPV self-sampling | **The largest effect sizes in the entire research file** |
| **JICMAIL warm vs cold** | ~5,000 real campaigns | **7.2% vs 0.9%** — 8× |
| **Bertrand, Karlan, Mullainathan, Shafir & Zinman**, *QJE* 2010 | Direct-mail field experiment, consumer credit | Showing **fewer** example loans, **not** suggesting a use, or including a photo each raised demand **as much as a 25% cut in the interest rate** |

Read that table as one sentence: **mail still works precisely as well as the sender's private
information is valuable, and not one bit more.** Opower's proposition was never "here is a number" —
it was "here is your household, measured, again, in context, forever." Five years in it was still
moving behaviour, and *increasing* in effect where it continued.

**Nobody is doing this in Australian residential real estate.** That is the entire opportunity, and
it is why the strategy recommends the recurring per-address structure over the one-shot.

---

## 4. "Has the industry found anything?" — mostly no, and for a reason worth stating

Across **2.54 million words** of eXp Realty and BLAC SALT transcript, and the whole Brain 1 coaching
corpus, there is **not one measured response rate for a printed piece.** One costed drop with no
outcome. One drop cost compared against one digital spend. One hand-audit of 30 listings. And an
admission from inside the field:

> *"many agents never implement the systems in order to track the results from the money being spent
> on the mailers."*

So when the industry says mail is decaying, it is reporting a **change in belief, not a change in a
series** — because there is no series. You cannot observe decay in something nobody recorded.

This matters because it cuts both ways, and both cuts are useful:

- **"Direct mail is dying"** is as unfalsifiable as **"you stopped too early."** They are the same
  folk mechanism pointed in opposite directions, and each is used to explain a result that was never
  measured.
- Which means **the decay claim should be held at the same low confidence as the doctrine it
  contradicts.** It is one operator's opinion with a plausible mechanism — strengthened by the
  research naming that mechanism independently, but still not a measurement.

## 5. The uncomfortable test for Fields

If our answer is *"the number is commoditised but our working is not"*, that has to survive being
challenged, not just asserted.

**Where it holds up.** Free AVMs give a point estimate with no working. They do not show which sales
they used, what each was adjusted for, or how wrong they usually are. Fields' adjusted-comparables
approach narrows the raw spread by **~40% at the median (n=512)** — a measured, checkable
differentiator. And the free number is not merely free, it is **systematically optimistic**: the
Zestimate is off by a median **~7.5% on listed homes** and materially more off-market, and one
peer-reviewed analysis found Zestimates **exceeded** actual transaction values in **63%** of cases.
That is a legitimate, sourced, non-advisory thing to put in front of a homeowner whose ranked fear
#3 is *"the number in my head might not be real."*

**Where it is weaker than we would like.** Domain and PropTrack property pages already show recent
nearby sales. Cotality reports already show comparables. The genuinely scarce goods are narrower
than "we have data they don't":

1. the **per-feature adjustment** — what each nearby sale becomes once restated for *this* home's
   land, condition and floor area;
2. the **width** — an honest statement of how little four sales can settle;
3. the **refusal** — declining to draw a line through a quarter that cannot support one.

That is a real moat, and it is a *narrow* one. It should be defended precisely and never
over-claimed — the standing rule that we never say "more accurate than an agent or Domain" applies
here with full force.

## 6. What follows for the artefact — three instructions

**1. Do not look like a price-drive letter.** Response decays by *category recognition*, not by
quality. A piece that opens with a valuation offer inherits the price-drive letter's dead response
rate no matter how good the analysis inside it is. This is the strongest argument yet for the
`anchor` variant opening the sequence — *you already have a number for this address; where did it
come from?* — because it starts on the reader's existing belief rather than on our offer.

**2. The anti-leakage move is to give *more*, not less.** This is the important one, and it resolves
what looks like a contradiction with our own TEASE guardrail.

The instinct on reading "self-service leakage" is to withhold — which is exactly what one BLAC SALT
voice recommends (*"do not put the sold price on them… they will never click"*). **That is the wrong
lesson and we should not take it.** The leakage is not caused by giving information. It is caused by
the deliverable being *a single number*, because **a number completes and an argument continues.**

Hand someone a figure and the interaction is over. Hand them four named sales, each adjusted, with
the adjustments shown and the width stated honestly, and you have given them strictly more — and
left them with a live question (*is my home more like the one that adjusted up, or the one that
adjusted down?*) that only more evidence resolves. Generosity is the retention mechanism here, not
scarcity. Which is fortunate, because the alternative is leverage over something that matters to
them, and it is already blocked in `guardrails.py`.

**2b. The corpus's own give-vs-withhold fight resolves on *when*, not *whether*.** Brain 1 carries a
genuine cross-source contradiction: give-give-give (Panos *and* Serhant, independently) against
*"the more value you give away for free, the less valuable you are"* and *"do not conduct property
analysis without client commitment first"* (Voss/Shull). Both are right in their own frame, and they
are not arguing about the same variable:

| | Give freely | Withhold |
|---|---|---|
| **What it is for** | Building the relationship over months, unsolicited | Servicing a live listing prospect |
| **When** | Long before any listing conversation | At or near the listing appointment |
| **Risk** | Slow — 12+ month lag, and the payoff is often a *referral*, not a listing from that person | Free spec work for someone who has already decided |

**✅ Re-run 2026-08-09 against a balanced corpus.** This section originally carried a warning that the
*withhold* side was in the corpus as a full book (Voss & Shull, *The Full Fee Agent*, 102 units)
while the *give* side's most prominent voice — **Sabri Suby's *Sell Like Crazy*** — was absent
entirely. It has now been ingested (**64 units**), along with five other books that had never made it
in. The question was re-asked against 1,049 relevant units with **no single source above 39%** —
the first time this corpus has cleared its own concentration warning. Full brief:
[`../02_Research/01_Brain1/D1-give-away-rerun-with-suby.md`](../02_Research/01_Brain1/D1-give-away-rerun-with-suby.md)
(⚠ 67% quote fidelity — every quote below was re-verified against its cited unit by hand).

**The axis is not *when*. It is *whose thing you are analysing*.**

| | Educate **the market** | Analyse **their asset** |
|---|---|---|
| Who says give it away | Suby, Carr, Pulizzi, Scott | — |
| Who says withhold | — | Voss & Shull |
| What it is | teaching everyone | spec work auditing *them* |
| Risk | slow | you can be fired before you are hired |

They are not arguing about the same object, which is why both read as obviously right. Suby's own
framing (k90092, k90093, verified): *"Value-based marketing is about offering value to your customers
**without asking for a sale in return**"* … *"why don't we run an ad and **give away some useful
information**, something that educates our prospects, and then offer to give away some more useful
information?"* And the mechanism he claims for it (k90099): *"If this is what they're giving away for
free, **imagine what their paid products/services are like!**"*

**Two things in Suby that bear directly on this programme, and one that stings:**

1. **The free report *is* the instrument.** His whole model is a High-Value Content Offer — a free
   report that gets someone into a funnel. That is precisely what the Owner-Subject Article is, which
   makes him the closest thing to a named advocate for the artefact we have already built.
2. **The title does the work** (k90096): *"the title of your free report is the number one factor in
   the pulling power of your HVCO and will largely be responsible for the amount of people that enter
   your funnel."* Actionable, and it argues for treating the piece's opening line as the single
   highest-leverage element — consistent with promoting the `anchor` variant to piece 1.
3. ⚠ **He does not support an organic-only position** (k90085): *"If you're solely relying on free
   traffic, referrals, joint ventures, or other channels like these, then you **don't have a
   predictable and dependable way to grow your business**."* Suby is emphatically pro-paid-advertising.
   Fields is 92% organic with Facebook paused. Recording it because it cuts against us, and the
   honest read is that our strongest advocate for giving the analysis away is also an advocate for
   paying to distribute it.

**⚠ And the uncomfortable one, which the earlier version of this section hid.** The Owner-Subject
Article is **analysis of *their* property, delivered unsolicited, before any commitment** — which is
the exact thing the withhold side prohibits, not the education it permits. Voss/Shull would call it
spec work. Three honest responses, and we should pick deliberately rather than by default:

- It is *not* an appraisal-for-a-listing-decision — no CTA, no price opinion, no ask — so it may sit
  outside the prohibition on its own terms.
- Suby's HVCO defence covers it only if it functions as a funnel entry, which requires the reader to
  be able to take a next step. Our piece deliberately offers none.
- Or it genuinely is spec work, and the mitigation is Option 3's opt-in — *"would you like me to let
  you know what it sells for?"* — which converts it from unsolicited analysis into a requested one.
  **That is a third, independent argument for the opt-in.**

**Two disagreements that do NOT dissolve, and both are live for Fields:**

- **Is free uptake a leading indicator or a mirage?** Pulizzi and Scott count audience as the asset.
  Blank & Dorf (*The Startup Owner's Manual*) count consumption without a committed action as noise
  that masks willingness to pay. Fields is pre-revenue with 26,297 decks and zero customers. **A
  swipe through a valuation page is not yet evidence of a seller**, and this is precisely the
  measurement Option 0 exists to settle.
- **Trust-then-commit, or commit-then-educate?** *"I prospect for the relationship today"* against
  *"never educate without control"* (u0163, verified). **Panos's own corpus holds both positions,
  same era** — so it is a live doctrinal split, not something recency can resolve. That maps exactly onto the structure already proposed — the automated per-address
analysis is the free nurture layer, the heavy posted appraisal is the commitment-gated escalation
(Option 5). It is also the same answer the lead-funnel verdict reached: **ungate the analysis, keep
the artefact.**

**⚠ And the sharpest challenge in the whole corpus, which does not come from the dominant source.**
Voss/Shull's *Favorite/Fool*: *"They already had a Favorite before they even called you, and no
listing presentation will change that."* If true, an unsolicited report to someone who already has a
Favorite is wasted, and one sent to someone for whom you *are* the Favorite was unnecessary.

That is a real hit, and it should not be waved away. But note what it actually refutes: it kills the
*late* giveaway, not the *early* one. Fields' own external research says **81% of sellers contact
only one agent and 59% hire the first they contact** — so the game was never the bake-off. It is to
**be the Favorite before the moment arrives**, at a moment nobody can predict, because 78% of
sellers are triggered by an unpredictable life event. Favorite/Fool does not argue against a
sustained programme. **It is the strongest available argument for why the programme must be
sustained, and why a one-shot mailer cannot work** — which is the same conclusion Opower reaches
from the data side.

**3. Fund a cadence or do not start.** Opower is unambiguous: a single mailer peaks ~10 days after
arrival and **decays to nothing within about three months**, while a two-year programme is
**2.5–4.2× more cost-effective**. A one-shot test will under-read its own result and tell you the
channel does not work. Front-load the frequency (monthly for the first four), then taper — novelty
dies at about four reports, usefulness does not.

---

## 7. The answer in one paragraph

Direct mail is not failing. **Cold, generic, agent-branded prospecting mail** was never good (0.9%),
was never measured by the people using it, and has now lost the one scarce asset that justified it —
so it is genuinely dying, and the operator is right about his own letter. **Warm, recurring,
record-carrying mail** measures well, is working at scale in energy, insurance and public health,
shows no habituation over five years, gets *stronger* when continued, and is being done by nobody in
Australian residential real estate. Those two things look identical from the outside because they
are both envelopes. Fields' entire question is which one it is building — and the answer is
determined by whether the deliverable is a number or the working behind it.
