# How Agents Win Expired & Withdrawn Listings

## Deep Brief — Approach, Script, Timing, Follow-Up Cadence

*Brain 1 synthesis over the coaching corpus (RealEstate Gym / Tom Panos, Sell It / Ryan Serhant, Agent School / Steinwede & Tesolin, plus Chris Voss–Full Fee Agent, Andrew Winter, and Fields' own seller-book units). Today: 2026-08-10.*

## Read this first — two caveats that govern the whole brief

**1. Source concentration.** The instructions attached to this task state plainly that **52% of the evidence comes from a single source, RealEstate_Gym.** That means the doctrine below reflects *that source's teaching*, not a demonstrated industry consensus. Wherever a claim rests only on RealEstate_Gym material, I say so. Where an independent source — the US brokerage/coaching units (the `u90…`/`u9…` and `k0…` ids), Ryan Serhant (`u2790`–`u3078`), Chris Voss (`k01…`, `u1020`), or Andrew Winter (`k00…`) — corroborates or contradicts it, I name that source. The convergences that survive across sources are the most trustworthy part of this brief; the single-source claims are the least.

**2. The corpus is uneven on the exact question.** Several retrieval passes returned an honest verdict of **no dedicated expired-listing playbook** — one pass concluded the units "do not contain the specific operational playbook for expired/withdrawn listing acquisition that your question seeks." That is true of *parts* of the corpus. But other passes surfaced substantial, specific material — Paul Biller (u0164), Josh Tesolin (u0877, u1958, u2653), Alex Jordan (u2202, u1866), Bayron Atherton (u2202, u2186), Tom Panos (u0507), and a large US expired-listing body (u901083, u901996, u902006, u900790, u901001). ## Part 1 — Why expired/withdrawn owners are the highest-quality prospect there is

Every source that addresses the topic agrees the expired/withdrawn owner is *pre-qualified* in a way no cold contact ever is.

Paul Biller's framing is the clearest in the corpus. He would "rather contact someone who is in the mindset of selling, and has tried to sell, rather than someone who might not even be thinking about it" (**u0164**) — and he notes the vendor has already sunk real money: they have "most likely spent marketing money in our area, it's an average of about 10 to 15,000 dollars, and their genuine sellers" (**u0164**). The intent is proven; the sign has literally been in the ground.

The Young Guns panel calls this the peak opportunity of the current cycle: "Expired listings are the greatest, and that's happening more now than, I think, what we've seen in the last four or five years" (**u901338**). Manos Findikakis says flatly that "expired listings are fastest conversions in any market" (**u900537** — noted without method). Tom Panos names them a top-two prospecting source: "My two favorite in prospecting will be expired listings and buyers" (**u0507**).

The US coaching body frames the same population as an untapped seam. One coach: "there is an abundance of low-hanging fruit that exists in the market that people either don't go for, or they're scared to go for" (**u900944**). Another reframes the whole database around it — "75% of transactions in your database are happening in your asleep file, not in your active" file, and "They're buying. They're just not buying from you" (**u900372**). Expired/withdrawn owners are the archetypal "asleep file" contact.

**The psychological trigger** matters for the whole approach: the vendor's *original reason for selling usually still exists.* Tom Panos: "Do you realize there are people that were on the market in the last few years that never sold? Guess what? The reasons they came on the market back then are reasons that might still exist now" (**u0203**). And they are, quietly, agent-shopping. Josh Tesolin's read: withdrawn owners are "thinking either they're not gonna sell anymore or they're going to change real estate agent" (**u2250**).

> **Source check:** This section draws roughly equally from RealEstate_Gym (u0164, u0507, u0203), Agent School / Tesolin (u2250, u0877), and the US body (u900944, u900372, u900537). This is the best-corroborated conclusion in the brief.

## Part 2 — The timing: when to make contact

This is where the corpus is unusually specific, and where two independent geographies converge on nearly the same number.

### The 45–90 day window (Australian sources)

- **Withdrawn — go at day 45.** Josh Tesolin: "I'd go at the 45 day... I wouldn't say anything bad about the agent. They're going to do that to themselves" (**u2653**). Corroborated by u2205, which describes owners "gone 45, 60, 90 days and the home hasn't sold."
- **Expired — days 60–90 is the re-interview phase.** "expired listings are back again anything at day 60 from day 90 they're interviewing for their next agents" (**u2186** — *corrected 2026-08-10: this brief originally cited u2202; the quote is in u2186*), and the same practitioner family filters on listings over 60 days (**u2174** — *note: "expired listings over 60-day filter" is an entity label on that unit, NOT a quotation; do not quote it*). Manos-adjacent doctrine (u0452): if "it's not sold by that sort of 60, 70 day mark, something's wrong... You've got the wrong agent."
- **A diagnostic clock, not just a calendar.** Multiple RealEstate_Gym units set the *staleness* threshold much earlier, which is why the owner is receptive by day 45–60: "The first two weeks are the most important, then the interest drops off" (**u0055**); "by week four your property is stale" (**u2493**); "after 14 days...it tapers off very quickly" (**u2267**). The buyer's leverage flips fast: "after week one and week two...the buyer has all the leverage" (**u901948**).

### The ~50-day psychological trigger (Tom Panos)

Panos gives a single number and a strategy to match it: "after 50 days, they're psychologically looking for another real estate agent. You want to be on that shopping list" (**u0507**). His metaphor for how you *win* at that moment is the last-man-standing:

> "What you've got to do is be the Steve Bradbury. He's the guy that won the Olympic gold medal because everyone else fell down... And what you've got to do is be the last man standing and that's how you win expired listings." (**u0507**)

### The US performance-gate clock (RealEstate_Gym-adjacent US coaching)

American coaches run a *tighter* diagnostic clock focused on showings/offers rather than the withdrawal date — this is how they decide the listing was mispriced and therefore winnable:
- "if we're getting no showings in that first seven days, y'all, we're too high... If we're not getting any offers in the first 14, we are too high" (**u901421**).
- "14 days on the market, no showings, no offers, the market spoken" (**u902186**).
- "Properly priced homes sell 1-3 weeks typically" (**u900788**).

**Synthesis of timing.** Two independent doctrines land close together: contact **withdrawn owners at ~45 days**, **expired owners at ~50–90 days**, timed to arrive *just as the owner's own frustration peaks and they begin agent-shopping.* The earlier 7–14-day gates aren't when you contact the owner — they're the evidence you'll *use* in the conversation to prove the listing was mispriced.

> **Source note:** The 45-day (Tesolin), 60–90-day (Atherton), and 50-day (Panos) numbers are three *different* sources agreeing on the shape. The 7/14-day gates are predominantly US RealEstate_Gym material and should be read as that source's doctrine.

## Part 3 — The approach and the script

There is **no single canonical verbatim script** in the corpus. What there is: a set of converging *openings*, one dominant *strategic frame*, and a well-specified *questioning method*. I give the verbatim lines that exist and flag where the exact wording is not in the corpus.

### 3a. The dominant frame — approach as a buyer's advocate, not a listing-hunter

The single most repeated tactical instruction, across three sources, is: **do not open by asking for the listing. Open by offering to help them buy / help their transaction.**

- Mat Steinwede (Agent School): "Don't chase an expired listing, another agent's listing to get their listing. Chase them to help them buy... when they are on your turf, you can actually have a good question-based presentation and find out where they're at" (**u0864**).
- A parallel unit: "You go there more from a buyer's perspective first to say look, you know, I noticed that your properties on the market, have you looked at, you know, where you're looking at moving" (**u0798**).
- Alex Jordan: "I'd either call them or doorknob them. And I would try to help them on their transactions journey" (**u2474**).

The mechanism: reframing yourself as helping their *next* move disarms the defensiveness of an owner who's just been through a failed campaign, and earns you the on-your-turf conversation where the real diagnosis happens.

### 3b. The door-knock opening (verbatim)

The corpus gives one repeated door-knock opener, from Tom Panos, built around **brevity + a value pretext**:

> "I know you're not selling and I'm going to be brief. The reason I've stopped by is we're doing an appraisal down the road today at four o'clock while we're doing an appraisal. Would it you be open minded enough to receive a report on your home as well as we're doing the work anyway?" (**u0241**)

The shorter variant, "I know you're not selling and I'm going to be brief" (**u2188**), recurs. Alex Jordan adds the *purpose* of the knock — it is not to close today: "The purpose of a doorknob is to just...start and plant that seed, create a relationship... we want to get rejected... Because we want to prove to that owner that we're persistent" (**u2202**).

A soft, indirect knock-approach also appears: "I might be mistaken, but I've been talking to some people in the street and they said that one of these homes looks like it might come on the market" (**u901749**).

### 3c. The phone opening — "call for a reason" (verbatim principle, paraphrased opener)

The corpus is emphatic that a call must lead with a *reason and value*, never a bare pitch:
- "The secret to cold calling whether it's door-to-door or whether it's by telephone is to call for a reason... What value are you adding?" (**u2359**).
- Bayron Atherton's "law of because calling principle" — only call with a strong purpose (**u2186**).
- Tom Panos's minimal qualifier: "Have you made the decision that you will be appointing an agent in the near future?" — which he calls "the magic question" (**u0545**; also u0165, u2276 as "have you made a decision that you'll be appointing an agent in the near future?").

Mat Steinwede's own opener is deliberately minimal: "How do you have any thoughts of selling? Simple as that. Nothing else" (**u0740**).

> **Not in corpus:** A full word-for-word *expired-listing telephone script* (the "I see your home came off the market, may I ask what happened?" style script that this genre of coaching often contains) is **not present verbatim** in these units. The pieces above are the closest the corpus offers. I am not inventing the missing connective tissue.

### 3d. The questioning method — silence + tonality to surface the real problem

This is the best-developed *technique* in the corpus and it comes from several sources at once.

**Steinwede/Tesolin, the tonality-and-silence move:**
> "I've been watching your property for a while now and noticed it hasn't [sold]... what's been going on there? And then all you do is shut up." The instruction is explicitly about "the tonality of someone that's asking a question... And then all you do is shut up" (**u2174**).

**Tesolin's Plan-B question** — designed to expose the withdrawn owner's contradiction and real motivation:
> "can I ask you if it wasn't gonna sell today? Can I ask you what's your plan B?" (**u1793**).

**The three diagnostic questions** (u900382, off-market context, applies directly): "We need to know their motivation. We need to know the destination. We need to know their timing." Echoed by the US body: "If you know their motivation, time frame, and where they want to go... the pricing conversation is easy" (**u902045**).

**The soft-close question** (Tesolin, u1958): "You've been on the market for a while. You haven't sold... if you're not sold in a couple of weeks, would you be open to a conversation?"

**Voss / Full Fee Agent** supplies the deeper listening doctrine underneath all of this: "If you're explaining, you're losing" / "You build trust by listening, not talking" (**k01331**); tactical empathy — making people feel *understood*, not agreed with (**k01299**); and labeling the owner's negative emotion to defuse it (**k01340**). Serhant's negotiation units converge: "The person that speaks least wins the negotiation" (**u1020**); "he or she who speaks most loses" (**u2746**).

### 3e. The one thing you must NOT do — don't rubbish the previous agent

Tesolin: "I wouldn't say anything bad about the agent. They're going to do that to themselves" (**u2653**). This is stated once but it is a clean, admissible instruction and it is consistent with the buyer's-advocate frame — you position on your own value, and let the failed campaign speak for itself.

## Part 4 — What actually wins the re-listing: the diagnosis is (almost always) price

Across sources the corpus is close to unanimous that the reason the listing failed — and the lever you must pull to win and then sell it — is **price**, delivered via **service or evidence.**

- Root-cause framing: "If the property isn't selling, the truth is simple, guys. It's overpriced" (**u900058**); "if the property is not sold in a few weeks, it's either the marketing is poor or the price is too high" (**u0700**).
- The two-things rule: "People won't reduce price based on two things. Service or evidence" (**u0110**). (Note the phrasing: they *only* move on service or evidence — everything else is noise.)
- The withdrawn-owner revival mechanic: "withdrawn listings, you get a new listing, you got fresh energy... new price... new marketing... you're going to get offers and it's going to sell" (**u2653**); "The reason people don't like auctions is because of the communication" (**u0049**).
- The magical-buyer line, repeated across at least four units: "When the price is adjusted the magical buyer always appears" (**u1938**, also **u2722**, **u1622**).
- Reframe the *language* of a reduction so the owner doesn't feel attacked: price at a level "that allows us to reject five offers than get no offers" (**u1802**); frame it as "the market telling them not you" (**u2717**); "The good news is we now know what your home's worth" and "Your home has become a reference point that sells other homes in the market" (**u2416**, **u2391**).

**The US body adds concrete pricing mechanics** (read as RealEstate_Gym doctrine):
- Get ahead of the market, never chase it: "It's like catching a falling knife" (**u901002**, **u900057**); "the longer we wait to adjust the price, the less you're going to get" (**u901002**).
- Underprice by design to manufacture competition: "pricing 5-10% below active comparables to attract competition" (**u902060**); "price just under active competition to guarantee showings" (**u902240**).
- A systematic reduction cadence: "For every one listing that you take, you need to get a minimum of one reduction per week" (**u900790**), and "almost every property that we list as an expired, we get it reduced almost... every case. And then it sells" (**u900790**).
- Buyer-search-band pricing: round numbers, and price at bracket edges — "$350K not $349K" so it appears in two search brackets (**u901571**, **u901421**).

**The value-articulation point that wins it from a competitor** (this is why the owner picks *you*, not why the house sells): "in the absence of value, people will select you on fee" (**u901225**, **u902060**: "fee is always only an issue in the absence of value"). And you must actually *ask*: "asking for business is non-negotiable" (**u901225**), the rule of three — "we should ask three times before you stop asking for the business" (**u901943**).

### The hard-conversation duty (well-corroborated, striking imagery)

Both the AU and US sources frame delivering the price truth as a *professional obligation*, using near-identical medical metaphors:
- "being nice does not help your seller"; "saving someone is not solving their problem" (**u2428**).
- "I might hurt you with the truth but I can't comfort you with a lie" (**u2391**).
- "Go ugly early... The people that don't make a lot of money in real estate, are the ones that don't have the tough conversations" (**u1581**).
- US version: "If an oncologist can walk in a room and tell somebody they have cancer and they have two weeks to live, you can walk in a room and tell a seller they need to reduce their price. You owe it to them" (**u900272**).

## Part 5 — The follow-up cadence

This is the second area where the corpus is genuinely rich and multiple sources converge. There are three cadences operating at different timescales.

### 5a. The trust-building frequency ladder (Steinwede, corroborated)

The most-cited number in the whole shortlist:
> "9 to 12 contacts people start to become your friend, 18 to 24 I want to accelerate that" (**u0073**), stated again as "9 to 12 contacts, people start to become your friend. 18 to 24 contacts... you almost start to become extended family" (**u1202**).

The principle underneath it — the "emotional bank" / "trust jar" / "jelly bean" family of metaphors — is one of the most repeated ideas across RealEstate_Gym:
- "Every contact... is building an emotional bank... your job is to make sure you keep it away from you by building the emotional bank so high with daily deposits" (**u0111**).
- "frequency builds trust. And every time you call someone, that's another jelly bean going into the trust jar" (**u0281**).
- "The more contact that you give an owner, the more they'll listen to you and the more they'll trust you" (**u0082**); "the agent that has the most conversations in the market, wins" (**u0281**).

### 5b. The relentless-touch cadence for an active expired target (US body, RealEstate_Gym)

Where the AU sources talk in *number of contacts*, the US units give an explicit **daily-sprint** cadence and a "never stop" rule:
- "I'm calling them every single day for 15 calls" / "it can take 13 to 27 touch points to make contact" (**u901996**).
- "I say never stop until they unsubscribe or I sell them a house or they list with me" (**u902006**); "Your job is to nurture them until they are ready" (**u902006**).
- The differentiator is simply *continuing*: "Most agents stop calling after the third try. So, if you are calling three or more times, you are already surpassing what most agents do" (**u901083**), and the conversion target "if I'm speaking to one person every single day... I'm in five living rooms every single week" (**u901083**).
- Serhant's version, verbatim and vivid: "follow up until, you know, one of you dies" (**u2872**); "We follow up, follow through and follow back until they die" (**u2972**); "80% of sales calls require five follow-ups... And most sales people quit after three" (**u3034**).
- The general 10-touch rule from the AU side: "80% of business is one from follow-up... 10th follow-up is typical breakpoint" (**u901235**).

> **Tension worth flagging:** u901083 says surpassing 3 calls already beats most agents; u901996/u902006/u3034 push to 13–27 touches / "never stop." These aren't contradictory — 3 is the *minimum bar*, 13–27 is the *contact-rate reality*. But note the aggressive daily-sprint numbers are predominantly **US RealEstate_Gym** doctrine; the AU sources lean toward *value-first, permission-based* frequency (see 5d) rather than a 15-day call blitz.

### 5c. The long-game cadence — quarters and years (multi-source)

For owners who withdraw and genuinely go back to sleep, the corpus documents *multi-year* nurture as normal and expected:
- "I've had people literally call me... you've been sending me information and keeping in contact for the last 15 years. I've now decided that I want to list" (**u1100**); Steinwede's near-identical 15-year story (**u2328**).
- "two years later, they call and say... we're getting lots of voicemails from you or lots of letterbox drops from you" (**u2162**).
- Serhant's own five-year follow-up that closed a $17M deal: "Five years to March of 2017. I sent my weekly follow up and the husband writes back... a week later, I sold him a place for $17 million... that deal never would have happened had I not been active about my follow up" (**u2854**); "I just... gave him property advice gave him comps... And I just waited took five years" (**u3032**).
- The reframe that makes this bearable: "97% of the people we talk to are not interested in listing... right now" (**u900180**); only "about two percent of the population... are thinking of selling right now" (**u900851**); so prospecting builds "future seller appointments" (**u0861**), and "Prospecting out of need almost doesn't work. But prospecting for relationships is the whole point of it" (**u0059**).

### 5d. The touch *content* and channels — every touch must carry value

The cadence is not empty check-ins. Every source insists each touch delivers something:
- "Sometimes the best conversation... 'I've got nothing for you, but I just wanted to see if you're doing well'" (**u2325**); "The phone calls are about strengthening your relationship. The real estate stuff will come up while you're doing that" (**u902040**).
- **Channel mix** (combine, never single-channel): SMS beats email/voicemail alone (**u2162**, **u901485**); handwritten cards and stamps for authenticity — "Authenticity is your superpower," hand-sign, real postage stamps not a meter (**u901973**, **u900180**: "handwritten card follow-up"); weekly "just listed / just sold" stats to the farm (**u1046**); voice over letters for the actual re-contact — "just listed and just sold calls now become absolutely critical... not letters, calls. Voice to voice" (**u1267**).
- A concrete US four-step reconnect: "letter, phone call, conversation, thank you note" (**u902040**), and a "33-touches-annual-plan" (**u902038**), and quarterly-themed sphere calls (**u900245**).

### 5e. The post-presentation micro-cadence (if you get the appraisal appointment)

Once you're in front of the owner, a tight 24–72 hour sequence recurs:
- Gift same-day, call next morning with *no* ask: chocolates "within 30 minutes... then I would call them the next morning and say... just want to know if any questions... I never asked if they thought to make a decision" (**u2079**); flowers within 60 min + morning-after call (**u1777**).
- Call *that night*: "I was ringing you that night... just wanted to check... how did you think" (**u0996**).
- Then a three-touch close over ~three days (**u2079**), respecting the owner's stated frequency: "When can I call you? I don't like to pest the people" (**u2199**).

## Part 6 — The full sequence, assembled

Reading the fragments together, the method the corpus describes (not a single unit — a synthesis) is:

1. **Source** the expired/withdrawn owners (RP Data / MLS filters, opposition boards, farm-area tracking) — u0452, u2174, u900555.
2. **Wait for the window** — withdrawn ~day 45 (u2653), expired ~day 50–90 (u0507, u2202), timed to peak frustration / agent-shopping.
3. **Approach as buyer's advocate, not listing-hunter** — u0864, u0798; door-knock or call with a stated value reason (u0241, u2359).
4. **Diagnose with questions + silence** — "what's been going on there?...shut up" (u2174); Plan-B (u1793); motivation/destination/timing (u900382). Never rubbish the last agent (u2653).
5. **Deliver value fast** — pre-listing kit / CMA inside 30–60 min ("dropped off within 30 minutes... The speed is impressive" u0081; "within an hour" u1603; one-page data u901811).
6. **Get face-to-face** — "You can't win a listing without being in someone's house eyeball to eyeball" (u0220); "No agency agreements... unless you face-to-face" (u0747). Never quote commission on the phone (u0220, u0559).
7. **Win the re-list on price via evidence, framed as market feedback** — u0110, u900058, u2717, and the fresh-marketing revival (u2653).
8. **Ask for the business — up to three times** (u901943, u901225).
9. **If not now, enter the long-game cadence** — trust-ladder + value touches + channel mix, for as long as it takes (u0073, u2162, u2854).
10. **Manage your own energy** between attempts — "People can feel it... you're walking around like your dog just died, and you're carrying the last one that you lost" (u900949); "When someone rejects you, it's about them, not about you" (u0915).

## Part 7 — Insight layer: principles, and what Fields can (and can't) copy

### The transferable principles (source-agnostic)

1. **Intent is the asset.** The whole edge is contacting proven sellers whose original motivation persists (u0164, u0203). Everything else is optimisation on top of that.
2. **Timing is a frustration-clock, not a calendar.** The 45/50/60–90-day numbers all track *when the owner starts blaming the situation and shopping* — not an arbitrary date.
3. **Approach sideways.** Buyer's-advocate framing (u0864) beats listing-hunter framing because it removes the defensiveness a failed campaign creates.
4. **Diagnosis before prescription; silence is a tool** (u2174, k01331, u1020).
5. **Price is the lever; evidence is the permission** (u0110). You can only move a seller with service or evidence.
6. **Frequency *is* trust** (u0073, u0281), and follow-up outlasts everyone else's follow-up (u2872, u901083).

### Extensions sharpened for Fields — and the copy/no-copy flag

Fields is a **buyer-first data platform** with a large enriched property database, valuation model, editorial engine, and no in-person agent. That shape makes some of this corpus directly executable and some of it structurally un-copyable.

- ✅ **COPYABLE — the timing clock, automated.** The corpus's 45/50/60–90-day trigger is exactly the kind of signal Fields already computes: the pipeline tracks `listing_status`, sold/withdrawn detection, and days-on-market (steps 103–104, 111, 113–115). A withdrawal/expiry watermark that fires an owner-directed touch at day ~45 is a *data trigger*, not an agent behaviour — Fields' natural home turf. **(Verify the exact field before building — per Rule 8, don't assume `withdrawn_date` exists by name.)**

- ✅ **COPYABLE — evidence-first, value-first touch content.** "People won't reduce price based on... Service or evidence" (u0110) and "call for a reason... What value are you adding?" (u2359) describe *exactly* the asset Fields manufactures: a personalised CMA/valuation with the owner's own home on the cover ("There's a picture of their house on the covering letter," u0048), delivered fast (u0081). This is the corpus's ideal expired-listing touch, and Fields can produce it at scale where a human agent produces it one at a time — this is a genuine *advantage*, aligned with the existing direct-letterbox and owner-subject-article assets.

- ✅ **COPYABLE — the long-game cadence via automation.** The 33-touch annual plan (u902038), monthly→quarterly→annual nurture (u900245), and multi-channel mix (SMS>email, u2162) are automatable; Fields already has SMS/text follow-up infrastructure (u901485) and a direct-mail strategy.

- ⚠️ **PARTIALLY COPYABLE — the diagnostic conversation.** The silence-and-tonality method (u2174), the Plan-B question (u1793), and Voss's tactical empathy (k01299) are *human, real-time* skills. Fields' chat/Samantha/messenger layer could carry a *scripted* version of the discovery questions, but the corpus is explicit that the power is in tonality and the pause — which a text channel cannot reproduce. Treat this as a lead-*qualification* form, not a substitute for the live conversation.

- ❌ **UN-COPYABLE (structurally) — the face-to-face close and the pre-filled-contract move.** "You can't win a listing without being in someone's house eyeball to eyeball" (u0220); "No agency agreements... unless you face-to-face" (u0747); the pre-filled contract at the kitchen table (u901001). These require a listing agent in the room. Fields, as a platform, cannot execute the closing step this corpus describes — so the realistic Fields play is to *generate and warm the lead to the point of an in-person appointment* (with Will or a partner agent), not to close the listing itself. This is the honest boundary.

- ❌ **UN-COPYABLE — the human "last man standing" persistence as differentiation.** Panos's edge (u0507) is that *most agents give up*; a platform that automates touches doesn't get tired, which sounds like an advantage — but the corpus is equally clear that what converts is *relationship and authenticity* (handwritten, hand-stamped: u901973; "Authenticity is your superpower"), which automation dilutes. Flag: automated persistence can win the *timing* but not the *trust* — the emotional-bank deposits (u0111) that actually convert are person-to-person.

### Where the corpus is silent — stated plainly, not invented

- **No single verbatim expired-listing phone script.** The openers exist (u0241, u0740, u0545); the connective full script does not.
- **No specified multi-letter sequence contents.** u0164 references "multi-letter follow-up" and u902040 gives "letter → call → conversation → thank you," but the *letters' text* is not in the corpus.
- **No hard data on expired-listing conversion *rates*** specific to the withdrawn/expired segment (general figures like ~80% rapport-call conversion in u0801, 10–20% withdrawal rate in u2269 exist, but not a validated expired-conversion rate).

---

### Bottom line

The corpus does not hand you one script, but three independent sources converge on the same method: **contact proven-intent sellers at the ~45–90-day frustration point, approach as their buyer's advocate, diagnose the (almost always price) problem with questions and silence rather than a pitch, deliver fast evidence-based value, get face-to-face, ask three times — and if it's not now, out-follow-up every competitor for as long as it takes.** For Fields, the timing-trigger, the evidence-rich personalised touch, and the automated long-game cadence are directly buildable and even advantaged; the diagnostic conversation is only partially reproducible in a text channel; and the face-to-face close is structurally out of reach for a platform — so Fields' realistic role is to *manufacture and warm the expired/withdrawn lead to the appointment*, not to close the listing.
