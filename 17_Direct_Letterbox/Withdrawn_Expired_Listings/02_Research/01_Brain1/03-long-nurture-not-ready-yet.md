# The Follow-Up Discipline for the Not-Ready Seller: The 6–24 Month Nurture

## Framing: This Is the Biggest Market, Not the Leftover One

Across every source in the corpus — Australian auction coaching, US team-scaling, direct-response marketing, B2B sales theory — the single most repeated structural claim is that the seller who is *not ready today* is the largest and most valuable market, not a consolation prize. The Full Fee Agent and SELL LIKE CRAZY put a number on it: **k90088** states that "The real money is in the 97% of people who are not in buying mode," and **k01009** frames the strategic task as developing "long-range efforts that reach the 95 percent of buyers not yet ready to buy." The coaching corpus agrees emotionally: **u1128** (Shane Beaumont) — "I actually get really excited about people that have no interest in selling because you like uncontested listings in the future" — and **u1803** — "I love future sellers, not immediate sellers."

The reason this cohort is prized is the **uncontested listing**. When a not-ready seller is nurtured to readiness, they don't shop agents. **u0586**: "They've already pretty much made up their mind that they're going to go with you because they've been having this relationship, this courtship going, that's been going for months, if not years." **u0311** confirms the same: even if it isn't strictly uncontested, "They've already pretty much made up their mind that they're gonna go with you." The CoreLogic data point in **u901781** (BLAC SALT AU — *corrected 2026-08-10: originally cited as u1781, which does not contain this quote*) anchors this: roughly "60% of them said, we actually selected the agent who we're gonna use before we met them."

So the discipline below is not niche. It is, per this corpus, the core of a durable listing business.

## Part 1 — The Segmentation That Precedes Any Cadence

Before cadence, the corpus is unanimous on one prerequisite: **you must classify readiness, and you must not treat everyone the same.**

**The immediate-vs-future split.** **u0881** names the failure directly: "The number one problem my friends with presentations is this. That most people that go to the haven't worked out, are they an immediate seller or are they a future seller appointment?" Tom Panos formalizes this as the ISA/FSA (immediate seller appointment / future seller appointment) two-wheel model — **u0536**: "You need both wheels turning in real estate. Short term long term short play long play," and crucially, "these long term listings are going to be the ones that pop out out of your pipeline in the years to come that are your free kicks."

**Timeline buckets.** The finer segmentation is by stated timing. **u1413** requires learning three things about every database contact — motivation, timing, and destination — where "timing = when (3 months, 6 months, 1 year, 2+ years" and, critically, "follow-up strategy differs by timeline." **u1991** operationalizes this into "segment follow-up by urgency three-six-twelve months," with the honest constraint: "I don't really want to go back there until they're ready to list." **u1053** extends the horizon fully: "Pipeline just means those people that are looking for sale on the next, you know, 30 days, 90 days, six months, 12 months, two years, five years, or never."

**The hot/pond split (US team model).** The Zillow Flex material draws the same line with different labels — **u902043**: "hot (0–90 days) vs pond (long-term nurture)," with the pond often owned by a dedicated role (**u902044**: virtual assistants operate in the "pond" while the agent focuses on 0–90 day hot). **u2726** frames the qualifying question plainly: "are you a pipeline seller next 90 days, or are you a nail seller?"

**How to elicit timing without pressure.** Two techniques recur. The direct ask — **u2269**: "Do you actually ask that question? Yep, yep, yep. When are you looking at selling?" And the hypothetical, which surfaces latent motivation, **u1653**: "Hypothetically, if somebody was to make you a really good offer on a really long settlement... do you think it'd consider that?... Bang, now you've got a level of motivation." **u1793** offers the plan-B version: "can I ask you what's your plan B?"

**Insight (extension for Fields):** These sources segment by *self-reported* timing, elicited by a phone question. Fields has a structural advantage none of these coaches had: **behavioral timing signals** at scale — repeated address-specific searches, valuation re-checks, deck dwell time. The corpus's nearest analogue is **u900434**'s behavioral trigger ("When they save a property as a favorite... we send them a text") and **u901064**'s "long-form questionnaire reveals seller motivation and timeline." Fields can infer the 3/6/12-month bucket from observed behavior rather than an elicited answer — an un-copyable version of **u1413**'s segmentation. (Flag: the corpus does not describe behavioral-timing segmentation of *sellers* at a property-data-platform level; this is an extension of its principle, not a documented method.)

## Part 2 — The Touch Cadence: What the Corpus Actually Specifies

This is where the corpus is richest and most concrete. Cadences cluster into a **default frequency**, a **declining-cadence-by-stage** model, and an **anniversary/annual floor**.

### 2a. The default: weekly-to-monthly, and the "38 touches" target

The most-cited baseline cadence for the top performers is **weekly email**. **u1511 / u1875 / u2562 / u2726 / u1511** all report the same finding from Andrew Bell's cohort: **u2562** — "Every single one of them does a weekly email. Every single one of them does a call every quarter." So the modal specification across the coaching corpus is **weekly email + quarterly call**.

Frequency-as-mechanism is stated repeatedly and treated as the causal lever, not a nicety:
- **u0722**: "it's just being in their face. So these people get something how often from you? That's a week. Once a week."
- **u1913**: "You need to hit that every three to four weeks. Make sure that you're constantly in the buyers or seller as I... In their mind." (This is the looser floor — every 3–4 weeks.)
- **u2036** gives explicit minimums: "There needs to be touch points...every 30 days via email, every year as a minimum by a phone call, that as a minimum."

The single most precise cadence target in the entire corpus comes from **u900912**: "38 touches per year is the magic number that increases the likelihood of conversion, which is usually between 3 to 6%," combined with "quarterly_check_in_calls." Thirty-eight annual touches ≈ roughly one every 10 days across mixed channels — consistent with weekly email plus periodic calls, mail, and events.

For the depth-of-relationship view, **u1202** ties touch *count* to relationship state: "9 to 12 contacts, people start to become your friend. 18 to 24 contacts, you need to, you almost start to become extended family."

### 2b. Declining cadence as the timeline lengthens (the US structured model)

The eXp/Sisu material specifies a **stepping-down cadence** that is the clearest documented structure for stage-based contact — **u901288**: current clients 0–3 months call every 7 days; 3–6 months call every 14 days. And it locates the revenue window precisely — **u901289**: "All of the money is between month six and month 18... all of the money is in being able to carry and nurture for longer than 6 months which is what most agents are not willing to do." **u901273** places the seller nurture window at "a good 3, six, nine months out."

Zillow's per-stage tunability, **u900319**: "keep-in-touch frequency customization... (e.g., in-contract buyers every 3 days, warm sellers every 3 weeks." So the US-tech consensus: **warm-seller pond cadence ≈ every 2–3 weeks**, tightening as they heat up.

### 2c. The intensive front-end sprint, then long nurture

Several US sources describe a **short high-intensity burst on first contact, then a decaying long-tail** — not a flat cadence from day one. **u901996**: "I'm calling them every single day for 15 calls... Stats show that it can take 13 to 27 touch points to make contact," followed explicitly by "nurture campaign continues after fifteen-day call sprint." **u3034**: "80% of sales calls require five follow-ups to get to the close. And most sales people quit after three." **u1229** gives the seller-research corollary: "80% of sales are made from the fifth to the 12th contact," and "people will research you six to 18 months online and in the market before they actually introduce themselves." **u1235** echoes "10th follow-up is typical breakpoint."

### 2d. The anniversary / annual floor — the most agreed-upon single touch

If there is one touch every source in the coaching corpus endorses, it is the **property anniversary**. **u1414**: "the property anniversary which i think is the hero out of every engagement strategy." **u0179** (Tom Panos): "Anniversary calls should be the number one. Anniversary calls." **u2067**: "anniversary calls strategy" for "past vendor and past buyer outreach." The differentiator claim is explicit — **u901685** (BLAC SALT AU — *corrected 2026-08-10: originally cited as u1685, which does not contain this quote*): "How do we know less than 5 to 10% of agents are actually doing the anniversary strategy with a card?" and **u0379**: "how many of those people actually say to you, oh, that agent sends me an anniversary card... Not many do."

The US CRM tooling automates this exact floor — **u900775**: "we're going to set those purchase and sale anniversaries for you automatically," and **u900319**: "anniversary date triggers: purchase anniversary, sale anniversary, birthday automatically populate agenda." **u1182** turns the annual touch into a promise: "For the life of while you live in this property, I will be providing you with a health update on the real estate market and the value of your home each year."

**Cadence synthesis:**

| Horizon to readiness | Corpus-specified cadence | Key units |
|---|---|---|
| Immediate (0–90 days, "hot") | Weekly to every 3 days | u901288, u900319, u902043 |
| Warm seller (3–6 mo) | Every 2–3 weeks | u900319, u901288 |
| Future seller (6–24 mo) | Weekly email + quarterly call; ~38 touches/yr | u2562, u900912, u1511 |
| Dormant / annual floor | Property anniversary + annual value update | u1414, u0179, u1182 |

## Part 3 — What Content and Contact Keeps It Alive

The corpus is emphatic on one rule that governs *all* nurture content: **it must carry value or information, and it must not smell of commission.** **u0542**: "Don't have commission breath because people get turned off by that." **u2276**: "He calls with a purpose. He calls as a trusted advisor. Not one of these calls high, I'm just calling to touch base." **u2359**: "The secret to cold calling... is to call for a reason... What value are you adding?" The give-first framing is universal — **u3029**: "Give, give, give, give, give"; **u3030**: "You're not asking for anything right away... You're there to help. People love free help."

### 3a. Market data as the workhorse content

The dominant nurture content is **the market report / value update**, and it is what makes a no-agenda touch legitimate. **u900855**: "they get market reports um for their area every two to three weeks." **u0402** gives the permission script: "do you mind if I send you a monthly market report because I know you live in Mya... 'Hey, sure. Go ahead.'" **u2416** reframes a value update positively: "The good news is we now know what your home's worth." **u1415** specifies the report cadence as "end of year / financial year sms market report strategy (half-yearly preferred." **u1097** points to the *best* content being proprietary and fresh: "The most valuable information is the stuff in our head... the stuff that isn't in a soul gallery" — i.e., insider market intelligence, not a generic PDF.

The B2B/marketing sources describe the identical mechanic with a longer arc — **k01185** (Epic Content Marketing): "Every other month, send them an important link or report... 'Thought this report would be of interest. Check out page 4' goes a long way," across a "12 to 18 months" relationship where "That doesn't mean you mention anything about selling the company." **k00004**: "Email nurtures are a great way for you to get your buyers & sellers prepared for their next steps, and to set yourself apart as a THE go-to area expert."

**Insight (Fields advantage, extending u1097 + u2416):** The corpus's best-content principle — proprietary, fresh, address-relevant market intelligence — is exactly what Fields' platform produces natively (per-property valuation, comparable ranges, off-market decks, suburb absorption data). Where a coach in the corpus manually recalls "the stuff in our head" (**u1097**), Fields can generate a *personalized, property-specific* value/market update as an automated recurring touch — the un-copyable version of the market-report drip in **u900855 / u0402**. **Editorial constraint (CLAUDE.md Rule 5):** any such touch must stay data-only, use comparable ranges not single-figure headline valuations, and carry the methodology/confidence disclaimer.

### 3b. The handwritten card — the corpus's single most emotionally-loaded touch

The coaching corpus places the **handwritten card** above nearly every other single touch, and treats it as un-automatable. **u1414**: "Magic happens not with a phone call the magic happens with a handwritten card." **u1415**: "the magic happens not with a phone call the magic happens with a handwritten card this two x pluses the value of the initial call." **u0380**: "The handwritten card will never, ever, ever be replaced by AI. Never." The card also outperforms call volume on cut-through — **u900852**: "i much rather everyone do less calls and send out the card than do a heap of calls and not send anything because your cut through is going to be nine times out of ten much much better."

Card *volume* is specified — **u900339**: "Five just-to-note cards will take your business to the next level. 20 just-to-note-to-say cards per week will put you in the elite level, but you've got to be consistent and patient." The US corpus corroborates the ROI and timing — **u900162**: "if you can get a handwritten note in the mail to them and they get that within 3 days of the open house... you will double your openhouse ROI."

### 3c. Tangible mail over digital, and the anti-automation warning

A distinct thread argues **physical mail cuts through precisely because inboxes are saturated** — **u1776**: "don't underestimate the value of getting something tangible in a letter box rather than an email because it is so much of it out there at the moment coming into your inbox it's diluted." The mass-SMS anti-pattern is called out explicitly — **u0382**: "who gets automated SMSs from different companies? We all get automated SMSs. And do you know instantly it's an automated SMS? It's a bulk mass SMS," followed by the corrective: "We need to know their motivation. We need to know the destination. We need to know their timing." Letterbox drops as an ongoing farm touch are endorsed but with a hard commitment gate — **u1411**: "if you're not going to commit to doing drops don't do them. I think it's a waste."

### 3d. Gifting and experiential touches (memory-making)

The corpus documents non-informational touches whose job is to be *remembered*. **u0982**: "go buy yourself 50 bottles of red wine... just go visit people with your wine, hey, didn't want to send you a Christmas card, I want to thank you so much." **u1408**: "They seem to remember the pizzas more than anything else... It's the pizza one that they remember," with "if you're going to give, don't put ceilings on it. Just give." **u1776** on seasonal gifting: "gingerbread houses that only arrive a few days before Christmas they always end up on the center of the Christmas table...it's a talking point." **u1415** adds "calendar magnets as brand reinforcement touchpoint." **Caveat from u2590:** gifting only works *on top of* consistent contact — "if they randomly get a bottle of wine but haven't spoken to you in two years they're going to be like what where the hell this come from."

### 3e. Social/passive omnipresence as the always-on layer

Alongside active touches, the corpus describes a **passive visibility layer** that keeps the agent top-of-mind between direct contacts. **u1568**: "they're following me on social media, they're seeing what I'm doing, that other agent hasn't called them." **u1785**: "Visibility and trust don't work on a tap... They actually compound." **u1783**: "12-month visibility/trust cycle (see, recognize, trust, chosen." **u2804** (Serhant): social/brand investment "returns for you maybe next week, maybe a year, maybe two years from now... that's called compounded interest." **k01231**: "People trust people they can relate to—and who share their experiments, wins, and failures." **u2488**: "become the local expert. Not about real estate, just the local expert with the best parks, the best cafes."

### 3f. The phone call remains the conversion instrument

For all the emphasis on cards, mail, and social, the corpus is clear that **the phone is what actually converts** and uncovers intent. **u2633**: "Phone call is highest... but at the end of the day, it's not going to become a listing till you have that final conversation, which gets you into the door." **u1751**: "if you build relationships with vendors on the phone and you ask them what they want, nine out of 10 times, they'll tell you." **u0557**: "Those that pick up the phone sell the most." **u1414** offers a reach tactic: "i rather leave a voicemail so that i know that they're going to call me back." **u2162** pairs channels for reliability: "SMS is really important because a lot of voicemails, people... I don't get my voicemail straight away anymore."

## Part 4 — Reactivation: Waking the Dormant Contact

A sub-discipline distinct from steady-state nurture is **reactivating a contact who went quiet** — the "blast from the past."

- **u1157 / u1431**: "blasts from the past (reactivating dormant relationships"; "gold contacts are prior clients who drifted off."
- **u0439**: "We printed out every single open home list from buyers that came through from January... We wrote them an email and we phoned every single buyout" — the phone+email combo reactivation, which **u0450** reports drove "my second best month ever."
- **u2040**: a structured "four-step reconnect plan: letter, phone call, conversation, thank you note."
- **u1512**: "ring previous buyers from 18 months prior to identify repeat purchasers" — an 18-month re-engagement window.
- **u0542**: "This activation letter, agents tell us they get incredible results with that activation letter."

**The trigger that converts a dormant seller is market feedback, not a check-in.** **u1938**: "When the price is adjusted the magical buyer always appears," delivered from a neutral stance — "Don't shoot the messenger - I'm the messenger." **u2416** uses third-party market dynamics as the nudge: "I'm a little bit worried that your home now is becoming a reference point and it's helping sell other homes." The off-market buyer pool is a concrete activation lever — **u2411**: "I've sold quite a few off market this way... I have about 40 buyers in market shape." And expired/withdrawn listings are a named dormant-seller source — **u2474**: "I started noticing a lot of listings on the market that have been on for 60 days. And I just saw the golden opportunity there," approached as a buyer-advocate, not a lister.

**Key mindset for non-response:** silence is not rejection. **u901766**: "unengaged leads are future opportunities not dead leads." **u901223**: "I've never had someone say, 'Hey, you're bugging me, Payson'... It's just all of a sudden they're like, 'Hey, we're ready to go now'." **u3032**: "Just because someone isn't responding to you doesn't mean they don't like you," paired with "I just... gave him property advice gave him comps... And I just waited took five years."

## Part 5 — The Difficulties and Obstacles (Where This Discipline Fails)

The corpus is candid about why most agents cannot execute this.

1. **Consistency collapse when novelty fades.** The single deepest obstacle named is psychological. **u900623**: "Consistency isn't about wanting it more. It's about what your brain does when all the novelty fades and discomfort goes up." The corrective is habit, not motivation — **u1913 / u1660 / u1165 / u1813**: "the motivation is what got me started, but habit is what keeps me going."

2. **Momentum decay is asymmetric — easy to lose, slow to rebuild.** **u0643**: "The minute you drop momentum, it takes forever to pick it up." **u2147**: "one crap month can become one crap quarter... one crap quarter can become one crap year." **u2267**: "after 14 days...it tapers off very quickly." **u1699** documents the specific failure mode: a "6_12_week_pipeline_drop_from_owner_disconnect" — the pipeline collapses when the owner disconnects.

3. **The "not-ready → pocket-listing" gap.** Notably, the coaching corpus's *only* named mechanism for holding a genuinely unmotivated vendor without marketing is the pocket listing — **u1750**: "I know that you're not ready now, but how open would you be for me to get your listing? Put it in my top pocket. If a buyer comes along, I can tell them about it. But we don't market it." But **no cadence or content is specified for that hold** — it's a permission structure, not a nurture plan.

4. **Callback-commitment failure.** **u1538**: "if you say they're going to ring them back next Tuesday, you're going to ring them back next Tuesday, not on Friday." **u2593**: "how do you actually sell property... if you don't follow through."

5. **The diagnosis-before-prescription discipline.** **k90123**: "A prescription without a diagnosis is malpractice," and its ethical corollary, "some prospects won't be good fit - ethical to not sell to them." **u1749**: "When you ask questions, you learn when you make statements, they judge you."

6. **Manual-only doesn't scale; automation-only feels fake.** The corpus resolves this by pairing automated infrastructure with a human touch — **u1040**: "personalized newsletters at scale with actual agent touch"; **u1570**: "there is nothing more soul destroying than when you get an offer at a million bucks the owners say no and then a month later they've taken 920 thousand dollars" (the cost of *not* educating during dormancy). The line to hold is **u0382**'s: automation is fine until it reads as a "bulk mass SMS."

## Part 6 — Insight Layer: Core Principles and Sharpened Extensions

### The four load-bearing principles (source-agnostic)

1. **Follow-up is the constraint, not lead-gen.** Stated identically across the AU coaching corpus and US material: **u1940** — "the biggest challenge we had in real estate is not that the lack of leads but it's... we have a follow-up challenge"; **u1943** — "real estate agents do not have a lead generation challenge they have a follow-up challenge"; **u1235** — "80% of business is one from follow-up." *Principle:* the scarce resource is disciplined, sustained contact, and it is cheap — **u2918**: "follow up is free."

2. **Frequency builds trust mechanically.** The "jelly bean / trust jar" model, **u0281**: "frequency builds trust. And every time you call someone, that's another jelly bean going into the trust jar." **u1785**: trust "is accumulated. They've seen him consistently add value with no obvious agenda." *Principle:* trust is a function of accumulated value-carrying touches, not any single persuasive event.

3. **Value-first, no-agenda, over years.** **u2325**: "Look, I've got nothing for you, but I just wanted to see if you're doing well." **u1093**: "Be their agent before they need an agent. So when they need an agent, they've got an agent, like a dentist and a doctor." *Principle:* the relationship must be economically unconditional to survive the 6–24 month gap.

4. **The compounding, non-linear payoff.** Conversion arrives unpredictably and can be years out — **u1437**: "A listing is the remuneration for good follow-up work. It took 11 years." **u2680**: "I appraised them eight, nine years ago, and they're just dropping in your lap now." **u2854** (Serhant): "Five years... I sent my weekly follow up" → "$17 million." **u2328**: "I've been speaking to her 15 years." *Principle:* you cannot time individual conversions, so you must maintain the whole cohort at a steady floor.

### Sharpened / extended methods for Fields (with copy-ability flags)

- **Behavioral timing-inference replacing the elicited timing question (extends u1413, u900434).** The corpus segments by *asking* "when are you selling?" (**u2269**). Fields can segment the same 3/6/12/24-month buckets from *observed* property-page behavior, valuation re-checks, and deck engagement. **Un-copyable by a solo agent** — it requires the data platform. The nurture cadence and content, however, stay exactly as the corpus specifies.

- **The recurring personalized value-update as the automated 38-touch backbone (extends u900912, u1097, u2416, u1182).** The corpus's ideal content is fresh, proprietary, address-specific market intelligence; its ideal cadence is ~38 touches/yr with an annual value floor (**u1182**'s "health update... each year"). Fields can generate a *per-property* value/market update automatically — hitting both the content-quality bar and the cadence bar that a manual agent hits only for their top 20% (**u1680**). **Partly un-copyable.** **Hard constraint:** must obey CLAUDE.md Rule 5 — ranges not single figures, methodology/confidence disclaimer attached, data-only, no "you should sell."

- **Market-feedback as the reactivation trigger, delivered from a neutral data stance (extends u1938, u2416, u2411).** The corpus's dormant-seller trigger is objective market movement ("Don't shoot the messenger"). Fields' off-market buyer decks and comparable-sale data are a native, credible, non-commission-breath reason to re-contact — the platform version of **u2411**'s "40 buyers in market shape."

- **The un-copyable that Fields must NOT try to replace: the handwritten card and the physical/experiential touch.** The corpus is explicit — **u0380**: handwritten cards "will never, ever, ever be replaced by AI. Never"; **u1776**: tangible mail beats the "diluted" inbox. These are the touches Fields' automation *cannot* substitute for and, per the corpus, are precisely the highest-cut-through touches. The correct architecture is **automation for the data layer (value updates, alerts, segmentation) + reserved human/physical touches (card, call, gift) at the anniversary and reactivation moments.** The strategic memory already reflects this — Fields' documented direct-mail strategy (`17_Direct_Letterbox/`) is the physical arm the corpus insists on; note the recorded reality-check that cold letterbox response is ~0.9%, so the mail must be *warm* (nurtured contacts), which is exactly the corpus's condition in **u2590**.

## What the Corpus Does *Not* Give You (stated plainly, not invented)

The internal knowledge-graph units (the u9xxxx BLAC SALT set) contain **no single named, spec'd "6–24 month not-ready-seller nurture playbook"** — the closest are timeline segmentation (u1413, u1991), the pocket-listing hold with *no cadence attached* (u1750), and anniversary touches (u0179, u1414).
