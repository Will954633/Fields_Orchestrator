# W5 — Real Estate "Farming" by Mail: A Reality Check

**Research date:** 2026-08-08
**Question:** In real estate specifically, what does geographic/database farming by mail actually deliver — with real numbers, from operators and from critics?

**Label key:**
`[PRIMARY DATA]` — a survey, rate card, or government source that measured or set the thing it reports.
`[COACHING CLAIM]` — asserted by a coach, brokerage, or vendor who profits from the behaviour, with no workings shown.
`[PRACTITIONER ANECDOTE]` — a named or semi-named operator reporting their own tracked result. n=1.
`[UNSOURCED]` — a number circulating with no traceable origin.

---

## 0. Read this first: the evidence base is worse than thin, it is contaminated

This needs stating before any number below, because it changes how you should read all of them.

**Finding A — There is no primary research on real estate farming outcomes.** Across ~20 distinct searches I found **zero** academic studies, zero MLS-derived analyses, zero brokerage-published cohort data, and zero randomised or quasi-experimental tests of farming mail on listing acquisition. There is no equivalent of a clinical trial here. Every "2–6% of the farm will list" style number traces back to a blog written by someone who sells postcards, or to nothing at all.

**Finding B — The search results for this topic are dominated by vendor content, and increasingly by AI-generated content-farm pages.** A large share of the top-ranking 2026 results (jamilacademy.com, goliathdata.com, dealmachineos.com, mailpro.org, geekmamas.com, milehightitleguy.com, realestateagentleads.com) share the same tell-tale structure — confident decimal-precision statistics, no methodology, and citations that point at each other. Example of the failure mode:

> "The average cost per lead in real estate hit **$503 in 2026**, up 12.3% from the prior year, according to a **NAR survey of 5,400 professionals**."
> — repeated across RealScout, Goliath, DealMachine OS et al. `[UNSOURCED]`

I could not locate any NAR publication containing this figure. NAR's actual member research (the *Member Profile*) does not report cost-per-lead at all. The number appears to be fabricated and then laundered by repetition. Treat any "real estate marketing statistic" from a 2026 listicle as fabricated until traced.

**Finding C — Citation chains in this category are circular.** DirectMail.io's 2026 playbook cites MailPro; MailPro cites the ANA/DMA *Response Rate Report*; the ANA report is paywalled, is a self-selected survey of marketers (not a census), and measures "response" across all industries — not real estate listing acquisition. Three hops from "real estate farming works" to a survey that never studied real estate farming.

**Methodological limits of this report, stated honestly:**
- Reddit is blocked to this agent (both search and fetch), so the r/realtors / r/RealEstate practitioner layer — the single richest vein of honest failure reports — could not be mined directly. This is a real gap.
- Inman (inman.com) returns HTTP 403 to this agent; Inman figures below come from search-result snippets only and are marked as such.
- ActiveRain and HousingWire returned 403. NAR's full *Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers* is member-paywalled; its headline numbers are taken from a secondary source that quotes it line-by-line.
- WebSearch budget (200 calls/session) was exhausted; the Australian agent-spend question is under-covered as a result.

## 2. The numbers as measured

### 2.1 The one number that actually constrains everything: NAR

From the **2025 NAR Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers** (transactions July 2024 – June 2025), how sellers found their agent: `[PRIMARY DATA]`

| Channel | Share of sellers |
|---|---|
| Referred by friend/neighbour/relative | **37%** |
| Used an agent they'd worked with before | **29%** |
| Direct personal outreach from the agent | 5% |
| Agent's website | 4% |
| Referral from another agent/broker | 4% |
| Open house | 3% |
| **Direct mail / newsletter** | **2%** |
| "For Sale" or open-house sign | 2% |
| Walked into / called the office | 2% |

And: **66%** hired a referred agent or one they'd used before; **~80% of sellers contact only ONE agent** before hiring.

Source: [BAM — How Home Buyers and Sellers Find Their Agents in 2025](https://nowbam.com/how-home-buyers-and-sellers-find-their-agents-in-2025/) quoting the NAR 2025 Profile; corroborated by [Virginia REALTORS® takeaways](https://virginiarealtors.org/2025/12/08/key-takeaways-from-nars-2025-profile-of-home-buyers-and-sellers/) and [NAR's own release](https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/nar-2025-profile-of-home-buyers-sellers-reveals-market-extremes).

**How to read this correctly.** The naive reading — "direct mail is 2%, therefore farming is worthless" — is wrong, and it is worth being precise about why. This question asks how the seller *found* the agent. A farmed homeowner who receives 18 postcards, then rings the agent whose name they now recognise, will most plausibly self-report as "I already knew of them" or "personal outreach," not "direct mail." Mail's mechanism of action is **name recall at the moment of decision**, not response to a piece.

But the same table is still brutal for farming, for a different reason: **the decision is overwhelmingly made inside a social graph you cannot buy into.** 66% is referral and repeat. And 80% contacting only one agent means there is no shortlist stage where a well-branded outsider gets a look — you are either *the* name or you are nowhere. Farming's honest job description is therefore: spend 12–24 months and five figures to purchase a weak substitute for a referral relationship you don't have. That is a coherent strategy. It is also, on this data, an expensive one with a low ceiling.

### 2.2 The only falsifiable vendor claim I found

[Agent Dominator](https://agentdominator.com/geographic/) (Beatty Carmichael) publishes a **money-back guarantee**, which makes it the only quantitative claim in this category with skin in the game: `[COACHING CLAIM — guaranteed]`

- Personal contacts / SOI list: "**about 1 out of every 3 listings** in your first year"
- Geographic farm / cold list: "**about 1 out of every 7 listings** in your first year"
- Price: **$0.75 per address per month** ($0.65 Lite)
- Claimed test base: "over **75 marketing tests** on more than **2 million mailed postcards**" (raw data not published)

**Do the arithmetic on their own claim.** 1,000-home farm × $0.75 × 12 = **$9,000/year**. At a 5% turnover that farm generates 50 listings; 1-in-7 = **~7 listings** → **~$1,285 in mail cost per listing won**.

Now compare that to the *other* vendor's published figure: DirectMail.io's 2026 playbook says a farm yields "**2–4 listings/year**" at an amortised "**$5,000–$15,000 cost per listing**" `[COACHING CLAIM, uncited]`.

**Two mail vendors, same year, same strategy, disagreeing by roughly an order of magnitude on cost per listing (~$1,300 vs $5,000–$15,000) and by ~2× on listings won.** Neither publishes workings. This is the state of the art.

Also note that Agent Dominator's implied **~14% listing market share in year one** directly contradicts the rest of the coaching corpus, which says 12–24 months to reach 5–10%. A guarantee that outperforms the entire literature by 2–3× in a third of the time should be read as a guarantee with conditions attached, not as evidence.

### 2.3 Practitioner numbers — anecdotal, and the honest ones are bad

These are all n=1 and self-reported. They are still the most honest numbers in the file, because nobody was selling anything.

- **10,000 postcards → 0.25% response.** David Lunsford, BiggerPockets, Oct 2011: *"Mailed 10,000 postcards over the last two months to absentee owners who have been in title for at least 15 years. My response rate was .25 of 1%."* He describes it as "pretty lousy." `[PRACTITIONER ANECDOTE]` — [BiggerPockets thread](https://www.biggerpockets.com/forums/87/topics/67850-direct-mail-response-rate)
- **Rebuttal in the same thread, from a vendor:** Michael Quarles claims *"over the last 6 weeks my return has been above 12 percent and I have seen upwards of 40% reported by some of our customers"* using yellow letters. Quarles sells yellow-letter services. `[COACHING CLAIM]` The 48× gap between two posters in one thread is the category in miniature.
- **1,109 postcards → ~3 calls expected (0.5%)** — BiggerPockets, various. `[PRACTITIONER ANECDOTE]`
- **"I didn't receive a single sale after over a year"** while paying **US$572/month** for SmartZip predictive-farming leads. `[PRACTITIONER ANECDOTE]` — quoted in [DMR Media's SmartZip review](https://www.dmrmedia.org/blog/smartzip-reviews), Apr 2026 (caveat: DMR Media is a competing marketing agency)
- **2,000 DL cards dropped, no direct calls** — cited by Caroline Bolderston (Being Bold coaching) in [Elite Agent's *The Brief*](https://thebrief.eliteagent.com/p/do-letterbox-drops-still-work), 11 Nov 2024. Her framing: this is *normal and fine*, because "they need to know you to choose you." `[PRACTITIONER ANECDOTE + COACHING CLAIM]` — this is the most useful single AU data point in the file, precisely because a coach volunteered a zero-response result and then argued the zero doesn't matter.

**Critical caveat on all BiggerPockets numbers:** BiggerPockets direct mail is overwhelmingly *investor* mail — "we buy houses" to absentee/distressed owners, where the goal is a discounted purchase. That is a different product, different list, different economics, and much higher measured response variance. It is **not** agent geographic farming. The fact that the only forum with real tracked numbers is measuring a different activity is itself the finding: **agents don't publish their farming numbers.**

### 2.4 Response-rate benchmarks (and why they're the wrong metric)

- ANA/DMA *Response Rate Report*: **~9% B2C house list, ~5% B2C prospect list** (2024 edition); the 2025 edition is quoted at **4.4% average**. `[PRIMARY DATA — but paywalled, self-selected marketer survey, cross-industry, not real estate]`
- Real-estate-specific claims from vendors: "0.5–1.2% generic postcards, 1.5–2.5% targeted, 3–5% AI-personalised" `[UNSOURCED]`; "1–3% with monthly consistency over 12+ months" `[COACHING CLAIM]`.

Response rate is the wrong metric for farming and every serious operator implicitly knows it. Farming does not work by producing responses to a piece; it works by being the recalled name when a life event occurs (§2.1). Vendors quote response rates anyway because they are the only number that makes mail look competitive with email/social — and because a 1% response rate sounds like a lot until you notice that in a 500-home farm, 1% is five people, of whom perhaps zero are selling this year.

## 3. The critics

**This section is thinner than it should be, and that is a finding.** Reddit — where the sharpest agent scepticism lives — was inaccessible to this research. And organic search for critical terms ("waste of money", "farming didn't work", "I stopped farming") returns *almost nothing but vendor pages that have SEO-captured those exact phrases* in order to rebut them. Wise Pelican literally owns the ranking page for "the truth about real estate postcards." The critique layer has been strip-mined by the sellers.

What critique I could substantiate:

### 3.1 The affordability critique (strongest, and it's NAR's own data)
Median Realtor total annual business expenses: **US$9,530** ([NAR 2025 Member Profile](https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/even-in-a-tougher-market-realtors-are-holding-their-ground)) `[PRIMARY DATA]`. The playbook's own recommended spend ($6,000–$11,000/yr for a 250–500 home farm) consumes 63–115% of that. Farming is only rational for agents who are already succeeding by other means — which means most published farming success stories are survivorship-biased by construction. Coaching sources concede the mechanism while framing it as a virtue: "prepare for one year of marketing without revenue" ([Rev](https://www.revrealestateschool.com/tips/geo-farming)).

### 3.2 The unfalsifiability critique
The doctrine is built so that failure is never evidence against it. Wise Pelican, asked directly why postcards don't work for some agents, answers: *"A lot of real estate agents truly can't handle the fact that there is a proven system"* — i.e. the failure is the agent's discipline. Bolderston, given 2,000 cards and zero calls, answers that the return is *delayed*, not absent. Landvoice-cluster content says agents quit "between months four and eight — right before the compounding effect would have started." **Every one of these converts a negative result into a demand for more spend.** A strategy whose only failure mode is "you stopped too early" cannot be disproven, and therefore cannot be trusted.

### 3.3 The predictive-farming critique (specific and evidenced)
Andrew J. Rohm, DMR Media, Apr 2026, on SmartZip/Offrs `[COACHING CLAIM — from a competitor, so discount accordingly, but the mechanics check out]`:
- **US$500–$1,000+/month, minimum US$6,000/yr, mandatory 12-month lock-in with auto-renewal.**
- **The data is non-exclusive:** "your competitors can and likely will buy the exact same list." A predictive seller list sold to every agent in the postcode confers zero advantage and simply raises everyone's mail cost — a textbook rent-extraction structure.
- Vendors claim **~72% accuracy** predicting who sells within 12 months; the accuracy claim is unaudited and, critically, meaningless without the base rate. If neighbourhood turnover is 5%, a model can be ~95% accurate by predicting "nobody sells."
- Breaking even requires ≥1 closing per year, before time cost.
Source: [DMR Media — SmartZip Reviews](https://www.dmrmedia.org/blog/smartzip-reviews) (2026).

**The base-rate point generalises and is the most important analytical insight in this report.** Every farming claim should be stress-tested against the turnover rate. In a 500-home farm at 5% turnover, 25 homes will list this year regardless of what anyone mails. The entire question is what share of those 25 you convert, and no one has published a controlled measurement of that share — ever.

### 3.4 The opportunity-cost critique
NAR's own data (§2.1) says 66% of listings come from referral and repeat, and mail is 2%. The dollars spent farming strangers are dollars not spent on the past-client and sphere database that the data says produces two-thirds of all listings. Agent Dominator's own published guarantee concedes exactly this: **1-in-3 listings from personal contacts vs 1-in-7 from a cold farm** — their own numbers say your existing database is **2.3× more productive per dollar than a geographic farm.** That is a vendor arguing against its own headline product, and it is the most credible piece of comparative evidence in this file.

## 4. What gets mailed, and what ranks

**The formats in circulation:** Just Listed / Just Sold cards · market updates & neighbourhood stats · newsletters · automated home-valuation / "what's your home worth" cards with QR codes · calendars, magnets and sports schedules · handwritten notes and yellow letters · "your neighbour just sold" pieces · seasonal/holiday cards · "thinking of selling?" cards · recipe cards.

**Practitioner ranking, per the coaching consensus:** Just Sold and "your neighbour just sold" pieces rank highest (they carry proof and are relevant to the recipient's own asset), followed by market-data updates, then valuation offers, with calendars/magnets/recipe cards ranked as pure frequency plays.

**Is there anything beyond opinion? No.** I searched specifically for A/B evidence comparing formats. What exists:
- No published head-to-head test of Just Listed vs Just Sold response. The best answer available is a vendor dodge — "together they tell a complete story" ([Truly Engaging](https://www.trulyengaging.com/blog/real-estate-just-listed-and-just-sold-marketing-postcards)). `[UNSOURCED]`
- Agent Dominator claims **21 split A/B tests, 37 message variations, 17 design/layout tests across 2M+ postcards** to determine the highest-converting message and design ([source](https://agentdominator.com/geographic/), 2026). **The results are not published** — the tests are cited as a reason to buy, not as findings. `[COACHING CLAIM]`
- DirectMail.io asserts variable imaging (personalised image per recipient) lifts response **30–100%** at a cost premium of $0.05–$0.15/piece, and that multichannel lifts response **27–118%** over mail-only. The multichannel figure cites "LettrLabs direct mail stats 2025" — another mail vendor. The variable-imaging figure cites nothing. `[UNSOURCED]`

**Net:** format ranking in this industry is folklore. Nobody has published a controlled test, and the one operator who claims to have run 75 of them treats the results as trade secret. **Anyone with a mail programme and a control group can generate better format evidence in 12 months than currently exists publicly.** That is an opportunity, not just a gap.

## 5. The "here's what your home is worth" play, specifically

### 5.1 How common
Ubiquitous, and productised. Wise Pelican, MyRE Postcards, Corefact, RSPUSA and Way Better Postcard all sell an "automated home valuation postcard" as a standard SKU. The standard mechanic: **a unique per-recipient QR code → a landing page → email capture → the estimate**, with the agent alerted on scan.

Vendor claims: *"scan rates as high as 2% per mailing"* and *"home value estimate leads are 5× more likely to list in the next 90 days."* `[UNSOURCED]` — no methodology, no denominator, no control.

### 5.2 The critique that matters, and it is real
The best-argued objection I found, from Inman (Feb 2026, *"You Are Not Zillow, And Your Conversion Rate Proves It"*, accessible only via snippet as inman.com 403s this agent):

> *"Free home valuation gets clicks, but it also attracts a lot of low-intent curiosity. Many homeowners are not raising their hand to list; they are testing the water, arguing with an online estimate, or shopping with agents to see who will tell them the highest number."*
> `[COACHING CLAIM / industry commentary]`

Three concrete failure modes follow:
1. **Adverse selection on the number.** A valuation offer preferentially attracts the homeowner who wants a *high* number. You either give them one — and buy an overpriced, unsellable listing — or you give them an honest one and lose to whoever didn't. The play systematically recruits the worst-priced sellers into your funnel.
2. **Low intent.** The scan is a curiosity signal, not an intent signal, and curiosity is enormously more common than intent (base rate again: ~5% of homes sell annually; far more than 5% will scan a code out of nosiness).
3. **Self-service leakage.** If the mailer's value proposition is "a number," and you deliver the number, the transaction is complete. You have taught the homeowner that the number is free and commoditised.

### 5.3 The evidence that reframes the whole play — and this is the most useful finding for Fields
**Homeowners already know roughly what their home is worth.** They are not short of the number.

- American Housing Survey data, 2001–2019: US sellers **overestimate their home's value by ~1.3% on average**. `[PRIMARY DATA — academic, Empirical Economics](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00181-022-02326-1)`
- Quicken/Rocket "Home Price Perception Index": homeowner estimates ran **1.9% above** appraisals at one point and **0.77% below** at another — i.e. the gap oscillates around zero. `[PRIMARY DATA — lender-published, appraisal-matched, large n]` ([HousingWire, 2018](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/40081-homeowners-continue-to-overestimate-their-home-value/) · [Rocket Mortgage](https://www.rocketmortgage.com/learn/homeowners-appraisers-home-value))
- Meanwhile the AVMs are *worse* than that: Zillow's Zestimate is off by a **median ~7.5% on listed homes and materially more off-market**, and one peer-reviewed analysis found Zestimates **exceeded actual transaction values in 63.44% of transactions**. `[PRIMARY DATA]` ([Counselors of Real Estate — Accuracy of Zillow's Home Value Estimates](https://cre.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/04/Accuracy_of_Zillow.pdf), 2017)

**The implication is sharp: an AVM number posted to a homeowner is, on average, less accurate than the guess already in their head.** Mailing them a point estimate is not an information gift, it is an invitation to a dispute you will lose. What homeowners *do* lack is: which specific properties sold, for what exact price, how those properties differed from theirs, and what that implies about the range. **The evidence supports mailing comparable evidence and adjustments; it does not support mailing a number.**

(This aligns precisely with the existing Fields position — comparables range over single figure, and CLAUDE.md's "no single valuation in headlines" rule. The external evidence independently justifies a rule you already have.)

## 6. Australian context

### 6.1 The structural difference: in Queensland, unaddressed mail is legally refusable

This is the single biggest AU/US divergence and it is decisive for a Gold Coast operator.

- Australia Post instructs delivery officers **not to deliver unaddressed material to any letterbox displaying a "No Junk Mail" message**. `[PRIMARY DATA — Australia Post]`
- In **Queensland specifically**, it is a **statutory offence**: delivering junk mail into a letterbox bearing a "No Junk Mail" sticker breaches **s107 of the *Waste Reduction and Recycling Act***, and Brisbane's *Health, Safety and Amenity Local Law 2021* lets Council fine publishers, advertisers **and** distributors. Political material, community notices and infringement notices are exempt. `[PRIMARY DATA — Brisbane City Council]` ([source](https://www.brisbane.qld.gov.au/laws-and-permits/complaints/dealing-with-junk-mail))
- Australia Post handles only ~10% of Australia's unaddressed mail; for the other ~90% via private distributors, "No Junk Mail" carries no binding obligation **outside Queensland**. `[UNSOURCED — Paperlust]` (i.e. QLD is the strict jurisdiction)

**Consequence:** an unaddressed letterbox campaign in Robina / Varsity Lakes / Burleigh Waters has a legally-mandated, unmeasurable hole in its coverage — and you cannot know which homes you failed to reach. **Addressed mail is not "junk mail" and is unaffected.** For a business that already holds ~40,000 cadastral address records, this converts an incidental data asset into a genuine structural advantage over any competitor doing letterbox drops.

### 6.2 AU mail economics (rate-card level)

**Unaddressed (Australia Post Unaddressed Mail / UMS):**
- AgentMail (real-estate-specific reseller, current): **$300 ex GST per 1,000 delivery points in QLD**, $345 other states → **~$0.30/letterbox in QLD**. Minimum 1,000. Max size 130 × 240 mm. Suburb-level targeting only — **specific streets cannot be targeted**. `[PRIMARY DATA — published price list]` ([source](https://agentmail.com.au/letter-box-drops/))
- PostGrid AU, from the AusPost rate card: **$0.220–$0.510 Standard, $0.280–$0.585 Premium** per item by size/weight/state. Standard = 15 working days; Premium = 7 working days, single state only. `[PRIMARY DATA]` ([source](https://www.postgrid.com.au/letterbox-advertising-cost/))
- Australia Post raised UMS Standard and Premium rates by an average **18.3% effective 31 October 2025.** `[PRIMARY DATA — AusPost pricing update]`
- Paperlust quotes $0.12–$0.25/piece distribution plus **$0.15 print** for 360gsm DL. The distribution figure is materially below both other sources and should be treated as optimistic. `[UNSOURCED]`

**Addressed (Australia Post Promo Post — the compliant path for promotional bulk mail):** `[PRIMARY DATA — AusPost rate card, current]` ([source](https://auspost.com.au/business/shipping/letters-domestic/large-volume/bulk-mail/promo-post))
- Minimum lodgement **4,000 articles**
- Small (88×138 to 130×240 mm, ≤125 g): **$0.825 same-state**, $0.860 interstate, $1.630 unbarcoded
- Small Plus (to 162×240 mm): **$1.100 same-state**
- Large (to 260×360 mm, ≤125 g): **$1.560 same-state**
- Large heavy (125–250 g): **$2.060 same-state**

**The AU decision this forces.** Unaddressed ≈ **$0.30–0.50/piece** landed (distribution + print) but suburb-granular, legally holed in QLD, and unattributable. Addressed Promo Post ≈ **$1.00–1.30/piece** landed for a small article, but street-and-house precise, immune to No Junk Mail, personalisable, and — critically — **attributable**, because you know exactly which address received which piece. Addressed mail costs **~3× more per piece and is the only version that can be measured.** For a data business, that premium buys the entire experimental capability.

**Worked example for a Fields-scale programme:** 1,000 addressed homes × 12 drops/year at ~$1.15 landed = **~$13,800/year**. At Gold Coast turnover of ~5%, those 1,000 homes produce ~50 listings/year. Winning the coaching-consensus 5% share = 2–3 listings ≈ **$4,600–$6,900 in mail cost per listing**. Winning Agent Dominator's guaranteed 1-in-7 = ~7 listings ≈ $1,970 each. Both are survivable against an AU commission; neither is proven; and the 4,000-article Promo Post minimum means you cannot run a compliant addressed campaign smaller than 4,000 pieces per lodgement anyway.

### 6.3 AU practice and commentary

- **How Australian vendors actually choose an agent** — the AU counterpart to the NAR table, and it is a very different shape: **45% found their agent after researching online**, **39% referred by friend or family**, and **10% took action on an agent's marketing/sales material**. Nearly **50% of first-time sellers and ~40% of experienced sellers search for their agent online.** Sourced to the InfoTrack/Securexchange *2024 State of Real Estate Report* (reached 130,000+ people in property transactions, May 2023–Apr 2024) and REA Group's *2024 Property Seeker Survey* (13,400 consumers), reported via [Cloutly](https://cloutly.com/blog/vendor-agent-trends-au/). `[PRIMARY DATA — secondary reporting; I could not verify the exact line items against the primary PDFs, which are gated]`
  - **This is the most important AU number in the report.** Where NAR shows US sellers overwhelmingly inside a referral graph (66%) with mail at 2%, AU shows **online research as the single largest discovery channel (45%)** and marketing material at 10% — five times the US direct-mail figure. Australian sellers shop online before they choose. That is a materially better market for a data-first, search-visible operator than the US farming literature would suggest.
- **Elite Agent / Caroline Bolderston (Being Bold), Nov 2024:** recommends four foundational channels — social (3×/week), letterbox, email, SMS (each monthly). Concedes the 2,000-cards-zero-calls result and argues the return is delayed: *"They need to know you to choose you."* `[COACHING CLAIM]` ([source](https://thebrief.eliteagent.com/p/do-letterbox-drops-still-work))

Panos has called Facebook "the letterbox drop of 2016." His Oct 2025 prospecting session with Avi Khan (iD4me) contains **no drop volumes, response rates, listings-won or cost figures at all** — the discussion is entirely about layering and consistency. `[COACHING CLAIM — no numbers]` ([source](https://tompanos.com.au/competitive-edge-in-prospecting-avi-khan-x-id4me/))
- **Josh Phegan:** frames the database, not the geography, as the asset — "your database is your community," minimum viable record = first name, surname, email, mobile, street address; prospect in the morning, appointments in the afternoon, 45-minute blocks. **No published ratios of contacts-to-appraisals-to-listings** in accessible material. `[COACHING CLAIM — no numbers]`
- **myrealestate.express (Ink Digital Media):** AU letterbox campaigns "from under $100 for 1,000 homes," recommends ~1,000-home farm areas, weekly-to-fortnightly drops, cites McGrath's Mat Steinwede as a letterbox practitioner — **with no performance data attached.** The sub-$100/1,000 figure is irreconcilable with AusPost's own rate card (~$300/1,000) and should be assumed to exclude distribution, print, or both. `[COACHING CLAIM — internally inconsistent]`

Neither Panos nor Phegan, the two most-cited AU coaches on prospecting, puts a number on letterbox ROI anywhere I could reach.

### 6.4 AU compliance notes
- The **Spam Act 2003** governs email/SMS/MMS only and **does not apply to physical addressed mail.** Physical mail to a residential address is not opt-in-gated the way electronic messaging is. `[PRIMARY DATA — statutory scope]`
- **Agency licence numbers must appear on all marketing material** (state licensing requirement).
- **Australian Consumer Law s18** (misleading or deceptive conduct) and **s4(1)** (representations about future matters made without reasonable grounds are deemed misleading) apply squarely to any mailed valuation, price claim or "I have a buyer" representation. `[PRIMARY DATA]`
- **NSW Fair Trading** has actively enforced against price misrepresentation — unannounced visits to 64 businesses on the Central Coast producing fines and an underquoting investigation, plus "please explain" notices to 25 agencies. Queensland introduced a **pre-contract disclosure regime in August 2025.** The regulatory direction of travel is toward *more* scrutiny of agent price representations, not less. `[PRIMARY DATA — NSW Government]`

**Compliance conclusion for a mailed valuation programme:** a posted document containing a price claim about a specific identified home is squarely within ACL s18, and any forward-looking element ("worth $X if you sold now") engages s4(1)'s reasonable-grounds test. The defensible artefact is **evidenced comparables + stated method + stated error band**, not a headline figure. Fields' documented valuation limits (±12% band containing the sale price only 61% of the time; the $1M–$2M design envelope) are exactly the kind of thing that must be disclosed on the artefact rather than buried, and mailing a bare figure without them would be hard to defend.

## 7. Digital-first / no-cold-call agencies, and what replaced mail

- **Purplebricks Australia** built its agent proposition explicitly on the abolition of prospecting: agents would do **"no door knocking"** and never "worry about where their next valuation was coming from," using Local Property Experts fed by central marketing instead of street presence. It entered Australia Aug 2016 with heavy fanfare and exited after ~2.5 years, recording a **A$21.2m loss** on Australian operations for the year to April (widely reported as a "$20 million mistake"). `[PRIMARY DATA — reported financials]` ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/business/20-million-mistake-why-purplebricks-failed-in-australia/3DXQ6Q53ECRNG3WQPUJ4DOWESM/) · [SmartCompany](https://www.smartcompany.com.au/startupsmart/analysis/purplebricks-leaves-australia/))
  - **The stated cause of death is the interesting part:** commentary attributes the failure not to removing prospecting but to *what replaced it* — expensive TV advertising "with a lot of wastage," and a failure to nurture long-term relationships digitally, "missing that people consider selling a home up to 12 months in advance." Purplebricks swapped a slow local-recall mechanism for a fast mass-reach one and lost the 12-month nurture window that is the entire mechanism farming exploits.
- **Upside Realty** (fixed-fee, founded 2017 by Adam Rigby, the model Purplebricks' exit was supposed to vindicate) entered **voluntary administration on 12 July 2023**, framed as a capital restructure; administrators Peter Hillig and Erwin Rommel Alfonso of Smith Hancock appointed. `[PRIMARY DATA — ASIC published notice / REB]` ([REB](https://www.realestatebusiness.com.au/industry/26129-upside-realty-enters-voluntary-administration-to-facilitate-capital-restructure) · [ASIC notice](https://publishednotices.asic.gov.au/browsesearch-notices/notice-details/UPSIDE-REALTY-ACT-PTY-LTD-631617340/a6e50d28-1d8b-4abb-8c67-a6f9a4ea9067))
- **The current AU replacement pitch — "the digital letterbox drop."** Elite Agent (Apr 2026) argues explicitly that **"the paper was the method — not the goal,"** and that the real objective of the Just Sold drop was always *social proof* delivered to the neighbourhood after a sale. Their substitute: AI-assisted third-party-published story (agent phones in the sale → AI structured interview → human edit → published on eliteagent.com) then shared and boosted to the neighbourhood — **200+ stories published** to date. `[COACHING CLAIM — Elite Agent sells the tool (getailsa.com)]` ([source](https://eliteagent.com/the-ai-edit-ep-5-the-digital-letterbox-drop-why-paper-was-never-the-point/))
- **Predictive farming (SmartZip / Offrs)** is the US digital-first replacement for geographic farming: buy a model-scored list of likely sellers instead of blanketing a suburb. Claimed **~70–72% accuracy** predicting sellers 6–18 months out; **US$500–1,000+/month on 12-month lock-in**; **non-exclusive data**. See §3.3 — the non-exclusivity is fatal to the value proposition, and the accuracy claim is unaudited and base-rate-free. `[COACHING CLAIM + critique]`

**Pattern across all four:** everyone who removed mail replaced it with something that reaches more people faster. Nobody replaced its actual function — *sustained, local, personally-relevant presence across the 12-month window in which a homeowner privately decides to sell.* Purplebricks bought reach and died; Elite Agent's proposal (a genuinely local, verifiable, evidence-bearing story about a specific nearby sale, distributed digitally to that neighbourhood) is the only substitute that preserves the mechanism.

## 8. Verdict — what a data-first business should take, and what it should ignore

### 8.1 What is actually established

1. **Sellers hire people they already know of.** 66% referral/repeat, ~80% contact only one agent (NAR 2025). `[PRIMARY DATA]` Whatever you mail, its job is to make you the one name — not to produce a reply.
2. **AU sellers research online first — 45%, the largest single channel, versus 39% referral and 10% acting on marketing material** (InfoTrack 2024 / REA 2024). `[PRIMARY DATA, secondary reporting]` **This is the most consequential number in this report for Fields.** The AU discovery pattern is search-led, not referral-locked — which is precisely the pattern a data-first, indexed-content operator is built to win, and it corroborates the existing Fields finding that ~91% of traffic is Google organic on bare-address queries.
3. **Homeowners already know their number to within ~1–2%; AVMs are off by ~7.5% median.** `[PRIMARY DATA]` The value you can add is evidence and method, not an estimate.
4. **In Queensland, unaddressed mail is legally refusable (s107 WRR Act) and street-untargetable via AusPost.** Addressed mail is neither. `[PRIMARY DATA]`
5. **AU rate card:** unaddressed ~$0.30/piece QLD; addressed Promo Post small $0.825 same-state, 4,000 minimum. `[PRIMARY DATA]`
6. **Median Realtor total annual business expenses are US$9,530** — the recommended farming budget consumes most of it. `[PRIMARY DATA]`

### 8.2 What is folklore and should be ignored outright

- "2–6% of the farm lists annually to you" / "5–10% of the area's listings within 12–24 months" / "20% market share by year 5" — **no source, ever.**
- "It takes 12–18 months" — plausible as a mechanism, but it functions primarily as an unfalsifiability device that converts every negative result into a demand for more spend. Do not adopt it as a reason to keep spending; adopt a **pre-registered kill criterion instead.**
- All response-rate benchmarks (1–3%, 4.4%, 9%) — wrong metric, wrong industry, paywalled or fabricated source.
- All 2026 "real estate marketing statistics" listicles — assume fabricated (see §0, the phantom NAR $503 CPL).
- Format rankings (Just Sold beats Just Listed beats calendars) — folklore. The one operator claiming 75 A/B tests won't publish them.
- Predictive "likely to sell" lists sold non-exclusively — structurally worthless; check the base rate before believing any accuracy claim.

### 8.3 What a Fields-shaped operator should do differently

1. **Mail addressed, not unaddressed — and treat that as the core decision.** It costs ~3× more per piece ($1.00–1.30 vs $0.30–0.50 landed) and it buys three things nothing else can: legal immunity from QLD's No Junk Mail regime, house-level targeting, and **per-address attribution**. A business with 40,000 cadastral records and a valuation model should never buy a channel it cannot measure. Note the 4,000-article Promo Post minimum in campaign design.
2. **Mail comparable evidence, never a point estimate.** The homeowner's own guess beats your AVM. Mail *which houses sold, for exactly what, and how they differed* — the thing they demonstrably do not have and cannot get from Zillow/Domain. This is also the only version defensible under ACL s18/s4(1), and it is what the existing "no single valuation in headlines" rule already requires. Put the method and the error band on the artefact.
3. **Do the one experiment the entire industry has never published.** You have the addresses, the sold data, and per-address attribution. **Randomise: hold out a matched control group of addresses from the mail programme, and measure listing rate in mailed vs unmailed homes over 12–24 months.** Every number in sections 1–4 of this report exists because nobody has ever done this. It costs nothing beyond the discipline of not mailing everyone, and it would produce the first real evidence in the category — which is both an operating advantage and, published, a marketing asset in its own right.
4. **Pre-register the kill criterion before the first drop.** Write down now what result at month 12 means stop. Absent that, §3.2's unfalsifiability trap will consume the budget indefinitely, because every source you will read is engineered to tell you that quitting is the only way to fail.
5. **Spend against the database before the geography.** Agent Dominator's own guarantee — 1-in-3 from personal contacts vs 1-in-7 from a cold farm — says known contacts are ~2.3× more productive per dollar. That is a vendor's own number arguing against its own headline product. Fields' equivalent of "personal contacts" is anyone who has ever touched an address page, requested a valuation, or scanned a fridge magnet. Mail them first.
6. **Budget honestly and small.** ~$13,800/year for 1,000 addressed homes at monthly cadence, implying $2,000–$7,000 mail cost per listing at plausible capture rates. Run one suburb, with a control, before three.
7. **Don't repeat Purplebricks' error.** It removed local presence and bought mass reach; it lost $21.2m and the 12-month private-deliberation window in which sellers actually decide. The mechanism worth preserving is *sustained, local, personally-relevant, evidence-bearing presence* — the paper is incidental. If Fields' SEO already reaches 91% of its audience through bare-address Google queries, mail's job is not reach at all. **Mail's job is to reach the specific homes that search has not, and to carry the one artefact a search result cannot: named, verifiable evidence about that reader's own street, addressed to their door.**
