## PART 1 — What the corpus actually says about the sequence

### 1.1 The base principle: mail is a frequency instrument, not a message

The corpus is near-unanimous (within RealEstate_Gym) that printed mail's value is cadence, not content. **u2329**: the flyers are just just listed/just sold plus stats, and "It's more about the frequency" and "It's just being in their face." **u0199** frames the tradeoff explicitly: "you are better off doing 5,000 letter box drops a week to the same people each week than just doing one person once in a month and doing 20,000 in a whole area." **u0660** reports the discipline it takes — "I've been dropping those letter boxes twice a week every week without fail for the last seven or eight years" — and its lag: "After about three months, the three to six months you start to get called in on appraisals."

**Corroboration from a different source:** **k01086** (the business/print source) gives the *why* behind print's role in the mix that RealEstate_Gym only asserts: "The printed word is still perceived as more credible to many people than anything on the web... 'If someone invested enough to print and mail it, it must be important.'" And it distinguishes print's *function* from digital's: "The web is where we go to get answers, but print is where we go to ask questions." This is the single most useful cross-source bridge in the corpus for Fields, and I build a play on it below.

### 1.2 The one hard number on print-plus-online — and its date

**k00173** is the only unit with a quantified combined-channel effect: "homes advertised both in print and online are 20% more likely to be sold than those that use just one of these marketing methods. There are lower discount levels too, and the properties spend less time on the market." **u1356** corroborates the mechanism from RealEstate_Gym — "A third of our marketplace... offline drives online" and older, wealthier demographics "were brought up with the internet, yet they're the cashed up people" — and **u1350** restates it: "A third of our marketplace will first touch your property in the paper for the first time."

**Temporal caveat:** most `u####` units carry the date 2026-07-18 (retrieval date, not necessarily content date); **k00173** carries no date field in the shortlist. I cannot verify when the 20% figure was measured, so treat it as an undated claim, not a current market stat.

### 1.3 The canonical print-anchored sequence

Two units give an explicit ordered sequence, and they agree:

- **u1507** (RealEstate_Gym): "Before there is even a sign bought up, you let a box drop coming soon in your street. Then there's a phone call. Then we do another letter box drop." This is a **6-touchpoint hot-spot sequence** anchored on a live event (a listing about to launch).
- **u0731** independently describes the same event-triggered mail rhythm: letterbox drops "timed to coming soon, just-listed, just-sold," with an "immediate callback protocol after attendee qualification."

So the documented sequence is:

**Trigger event (a nearby listing/sale) → coming-soon letterbox drop → phone call → second letterbox drop (just listed) → in-person (door knock / open home) → just-sold letterbox drop → callback.**

The trigger is almost always **a neighbouring transaction**, not a calendar date. **u0414**: "Just listed or just sold because you've got a reason to speak to them." **u2425** gives the exact consultative script that rides the trigger: "your neighbor is selling their home and I can put together a report on how their sale will impact your home's value." **u0730**: "Find a reason to door knock them. Just listed is a good reason."

### 1.4 What triggers the *next* touch

The corpus names three trigger types:

1. **External event trigger** — a nearby list/sale/withdrawal/expiry. Sources: RealEstate_Gym (**u0414**, **u2425**, **u0164** on expireds), Agent School (**u0452**: "Withdrawn to me was huge"), and Serhant-source life-events (**u2933**: "the moment they're ready to become real buyers," **u2188**: COVID/work-from-home shifting readiness).
2. **Cadence trigger** — the calendar itself. **u0051** lays out the full ladder: "daily calling, weekly letterbox drops, monthly letters, quarterly high-effort marketing, biannual CMA reports." **u2036** gives the database-wide minimum: "every 30 days via email, every year as a minimum by a phone call." **u0645**: "four quarter market reviews... an eight page, beautiful glossy booklet."
3. **Signal trigger** — the prospect does something. This is Part 2.

### 1.5 The channel-by-channel roles the corpus assigns

- **Print/letterbox** — credibility + frequency + first-touch for passive/older buyers (**k01086**, **u1356**, **k00173**). Requires long consistency before ROI (**u1486**: agents quit "because they do it... and then they don't get a direct call from it," but it builds "personal brand"; **u0660**).
- **Phone** — the primary conversion channel. **u0557**: "Those that pick up the phone sell the most... Nothing replaces the interaction between two humans." **u0303** (Panos): "Phone over email follow-up." **u2633** ranks it: "Phone call is highest. And after that... Price update... text message... email."
- **SMS** — higher engagement than email, and *rising* as email declines. **u2162**: "email rates are declining... everybody... gets it from the SMS." **u0660**: "SMS is really important because a lot of voicemails, people... I don't get my voicemail straight away anymore." **u0464**: three-minute SMS appraisal — "In an age of convenience, convenience wins every time."
- **Social** — passive/always-on prospecting and pre-meeting research. **u2072**: "follow my Instagram and I can see what's selling." **u0553**: "78% of property seekers research you." **u2241**: Facebook *retargeting* of warm website visitors, explicitly "because it's warm... traffic" — the anti-spam move.
- **Door-knocking** — long-horizon relationship pipeline, reason-anchored. **u0076** (Steinwede): "The door knock today isn't about finding the lead today... It's about the connection... to secure the listing in two years time." **u0212**: the "Magic 40" — 10 each side, 20 opposite a just-sold.
- **Email** — owned first-party channel and the universal weekly nurture among top billers. **u2726**: "all 10 agents that ride over 600 a year... Every single one of them does a weekly email." Corroborated cross-source by **k01026**: "owned subscription sources are still primary... first-party data."

**One explicit multi-channel warning** — from RealEstate_Gym itself: **u0037**: "One of the biggest things that you can do is really piss people off by too many forms of marketing." This is the guardrail for any Fields sequence.

### 1.6 What "unavoidable" looks like when it works

**u0139** is the clearest statement of the compounding effect: "I touched them 50 times not in a creepy way... I touch them digitally, directly mail, emails, calls, see them in open home so it's the collectiveness of them." **u2471** puts saturation at scale: "40,000 roughly... it would be very hard to ignore you... if they're going to miss you on social we're going to pick you up on sideboards or letterbox drops." The operating rule that ties every channel together, repeated across dozens of RealEstate_Gym units and stated most simply in **u0986** and **u0199**: they work "in combination, not in isolation."

## PART 2 — The quiet engager: someone who has shown a signal but not enquired

This is the heart of the question, and the corpus is unusually rich here. There are two distinct sub-populations, and the best operators treat them differently.

### 2.1 Silence is data, not rejection

The strongest cross-source agreement in the entire brief is here, and it does **not** rest only on RealEstate_Gym:

- **Serhant-source, u3032**: "Just because someone isn't responding to you doesn't mean they don't like you... Don't take a non-response as a non-response forever. It might just mean they're not ready for you." The same unit gives a 5-year worked example: "I just helped the entire time gave him property advice gave him comps different listings And I just waited took five years."
- **Serhant-source, u2933**: "Persistently following up every week with a buyer I know is a buyer, but not ready to pull the trigger right away, over and over and over for two years... Because I've reached her at the right time."
- **RealEstate_Gym, u1568**: "after three to six months to nine months to two years, and they've been getting every quarter called from me, they're following me on social media... they're seeing what I'm doing" — then the trigger event fires and they list.
- **Agent School, u0494** (Steinwede) adds the *restraint* dimension — reading whether the signal is real: "sometimes people... say to me. Did you ring those buyers yet? And I'll say... no, I didn't. I just don't think it's the right moment." And: "Be patient and read the play intention... is it that emotional window now where they're ready to go."

So all three sources converge: **a quiet signal starts a nurture clock measured in months to years, not a sales push.**

### 2.2 The engagement signal itself — how the best operators detect it

The corpus names the specific quiet signals:

- **Attending an open home without enquiring.** This is the richest signal in the corpus. **u0731**: "20% of those are going to be sellers. They're doing research, they're checking out agents." **u2136**: "Most vendors that are not absentee vendors will be walking into an open home before they list their property." **u0900** puts a window on it: "before a person puts a home on the market, they start doing the rounds by going to open homes and they normally do it three to four months before." **u2706**: "we get the full name, the post code... And also they might want to come up any address."
- **Following on social.** **u1128**: "follow my Instagram... The days are so giving me a business card... I say, look, follow me Instagram." **u3041** (Serhant-source): "He started following me on social and I put him into my email database... Now he's hired me to sell one of the biggest projects."
- **Consuming content / downloading a lead magnet.** **k00512**: "69 views and 38 downloads in three months" → "we have converted a ton of them into clients." **k00005** (post-auction targeted emails source): "Email/phone integration with call list prioritization based on engagement."
- **Digital behaviour on your site.** **k00801**: "test, measure, and optimize every step in the funnel." **u2241**: retarget the warm website visitor.

### 2.3 What the best operators DO with a quiet engager

Six documented moves, from most-corroborated to least:

**(a) Enter them in the database at the ownership threshold and start the cadence — do not ask for anything.** **u2036**: "Anyone that... owns a piece of real estate... is eligible to actually live in your database," with "every 30 days via email, every year as a minimum by a phone call." **u2200** (Serhant-source): "I never asked for anything. I just give, give, give." **u1301**: "shift the intent from so I can get a listing to so I can provide value." This is cross-source (RealEstate_Gym + Serhant-source).

**(b) Offer a concrete, low-friction next artifact — the impact-on-your-home report — NOT a listing ask.** **u2425**: "I can put together a report on how their sale will impact your home's value." **u0528**: "The CMA report is Matt Steinway's main method." **u0048** (independent source): the CMA is "the greatest tool... It's literally like the secret weapon," delivered in three days with "a picture of their house on the covering letter." This is the exact place Fields' data platform substitutes for a personal track record — see **u2857** and **u2857**: "I don't tell people they need me. I have them tell me they need me."

**(c) Give the buyer/quiet-seller a permission-based opt-in that captures address, not phone.** **u1730** is the cleanest documented micro-conversion off a soft signal: "if they say, 'I'm going to come to the open', you can then say, 'would you like me to let you know what it sells for?'" This converts a browser into a subscribed, permissioned contact with a single yes. Fields' own memory (contact-capture reality) says the public won't give phone/email — so this address-capture move is the one to industrialize.

**(d) Same-day speed after any in-person signal — but for buyers, not a listing push.** **u2120**: "Speed is highly valued... a conversation with you is more likely to be positive if the conversation is shortly after the inspection, versus on a Monday." **u1295**: "Two hours is no good, two minutes is good." **k01598** (business source) corroborates cross-source: a two-minute social response "builds customer lifetime value."

**(e) Keep the touches value-only and reason-anchored, so frequency never reads as pressure.** **u0281**: "I know you're not selling yet and I'm going to be brief. The reason I'm calling is because number 27 Smith Street has just come onto the market." **u0098**: "Have you had any thoughts of selling at all... Simple to the point finished gone." **u1777**: "I have a lot of people say to me that we really like that you didn't push us." **u1716** names the risk of pushing early: "if I've pushed for the business too early, I might list it. But they'll wake up tomorrow morning and feel like they've been pushed."

**(f) Treat an objection or a question as the buy signal it is.** **u1836**: "Objection is simply a request for more information. Be more concerned if you're not getting them."

### 2.4 The conversion trigger

Across sources the quiet-engager converts on **a life/market event intersecting with your accumulated presence**, and the operator's job is to be the most *recent* touch when it fires. **u1440**: "Recency trumps loyalty... The agent with momentum gets more." **u1691**: "who did that that person's going to either wake up tomorrow I'm going to sell my house who do I ring first." **u2328**: "I've been speaking to her 15 years. She rings today... we think we're ready." **u1229** gives the threshold figure: "80% of sales are made from the fifth to the 12th contact."

### PLAY 1 — "The Print Question, The Web Answer"

**Bridges:** **k01086** (print = where we ask questions; web = where we get answers) × **u2162**/**u0464** (SMS beats email; 3-minute appraisal) × **u2241** (retarget warm traffic) × Fields' per-address mini-site.

**Mechanism:** k01086 gives a non-obvious division of labour no RealEstate_Gym unit states — print is *credible and provokes a question*; web *answers* it. Most agents use mail to answer (stats, prices) and get ignored. Invert it: the letterbox piece poses the question print is trusted to pose — a single line, per k01086's "call of action" rule (**u0063**) — e.g. a printed prompt "Text this code to see what [their street]'s latest sale means for your home." The **printed piece deliberately carries no data**; it carries a QR/short-code. The *answer* lives on Fields' address-specific mini-site (the web, where answers belong). SMS (u2162: rising engagement) is the bridge channel. The retarget (u2241) then picks up anyone who visited the mini-site but didn't enquire — closing the "warm but silent" loop from Part 2.

This is the one place the corpus's own logic (print≠web function) plus Fields' auto-generated per-address pages produce something neither coach nor generic-marketing source described: **print whose entire job is to trigger a digital self-serve answer, with the silent responders swept up by retargeting.**

## PART 4 — Where the corpus is silent (do not invent)

Stated plainly, per instruction:

- **No quantified channel-attribution or sequencing A/B data.** The corpus asserts sequences work (u1507, u0139) but the only hard combined-channel number is k00173's undated "20% more likely to be sold." There is **no** unit measuring, e.g., mail-then-SMS vs SMS-then-mail conversion. Fields should treat the sequence order as coaching-doctrine, then test it (k00822: "test it and measure it again").
- **No guidance on optimal SMS/mail *timing intervals*** beyond coarse cadences (u0051's daily/weekly/monthly ladder; u1913's "every three to four weeks"). The gap between touches is asserted, never optimized.
- **No treatment of email deliverability, spam compliance, or Australian direct-mail regulation.** u2306 mentions a print-fulfilment vendor ("Bing... get past the junk mail") but nothing on consent law.
- **No data on the quiet-engager conversion *rate*** — u1229's "80% of sales... fifth to 12th contact" is about contact count, not what fraction of silent engagers ever convert.

Fields should fill these by instrumenting its own funnel rather than importing a coaching number as fact.

## One-paragraph summary for Will

The corpus's documented print-anchored sequence is **event-triggered, not calendar-triggered**: a nearby sale fires coming-soon mail → phone → just-listed mail → in-person → just-sold mail → callback (u1507, u0731), with print doing *frequency and credibility* while phone/SMS do *conversion* (k01086, u0557, u2162). For the quietly-engaged-but-silent — the person the question centres on — every source agrees silence is a months-to-years nurture clock, not a rejection (u3032, u2933, u1568, u0494), and the operator's job is to be value-only, permissioned, and the *most recent* touch when a life event fires (u1440, u1691). Fields' unfair advantage is that its data platform can auto-generate the exact artifact the coaches hand-made as their "secret weapon" (u0048's CMA) — so the five plays above all reduce to: **let print pose the question, let Fields' per-address data answer it, and let behaviour (not the calendar) decide when a human calls.** Caveat throughout: this is ~65% RealEstate_Gym doctrine, corroborated where noted but not proven industry consensus — test the sequence, don't assume it.
