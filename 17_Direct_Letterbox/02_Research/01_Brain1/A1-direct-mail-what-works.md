### Formats that WORKED (named in corpus)

1. **Just-listed / just-sold flyers with monthly stats** — the dominant recurring format. u2329: "just normal flyer, like just list, just sold, months later, stats. It's more about the frequency." u1046, u0722 corroborate.
2. **Personalized CMA / market report with a photo of the recipient's own house on the cover** — u0048: "when they get the report, they're going to open it up. There's a picture of their house on the covering letter." Called the primary listing method by Mat Steinwede in u0528: "The CMA report is Matt Steinway's main method that he built his business."
3. **Handwritten notes / cards** — repeatedly named as the *highest-response* format. u2367: "The amount of phone calls that I get off that handwritten card versus what a generic DL drop would be is incredible." u0520: "The handwritten note continues to be an ethical bribe that gets calls coming in." u2180: "you've got to do 20 30 or 40 of these a day they will get you indoors."
4. **Personalized letters in an envelope** (vs. bulk unaddressed) — u0198: "personalized letters in an envelope that go to an owner. Continue to be another source of getting your appraisals."
5. **Creative packaging** — coloured/pink envelopes (u2160: "I put them in pink envelopes... they might think it's a Valentine's day"); seasonal physical gifts like gingerbread houses at Christmas (u1776).
6. **Glossy brochures, fortnightly** — u0526: "Doing fortnightly letter box drops with really really nice glossy brochures."
7. **Targeted geographic newsletters** pinpointed to a defined ~1,000-home area — u0571, u1018 (monthly / 4-week cadence).
8. **Print + online combined** — the ONLY format with external empirical backing (k00173, 503K properties, 20% lift).

### Formats / approaches that FAILED (named in corpus)

1. **Isolated / one-off DL drops** — u0037: "The idea of just constantly sending DLs doesn't work anymore, but saturation marketing does." The generic un-personalized DL is explicitly called the low-response baseline (u2367).
2. **One-time wide spray** — u0199: "you are better off doing 5,000 letterbox drops a week to the same people each week than just doing one person once in a month and doing 20,000 in a whole area."
3. **Drops in isolation from other channels** — u0986: "They work in combination, not in isolation." u1726, u1174 repeat verbatim.
4. **Quitting early** — the most-named failure mode. u1486: "What most agents, why they give it up is they do it, and then they do it, and then they don't get a direct call from it." u2306: "whatever you do, you've got to do it for 12 months. None of this I'll try it for two."
5. **Pure advertising with no value/info** — u1486: "The first cord needs to be of service and of value." u2063/u2063: "it's getting back to that, and it's information giving with those letters."

### The lag-to-result curve (consistently named)

Multiple independent agents give the **same** timeline — this is the most internally-consistent quantified claim in the corpus:

- **0–3 months: no ROI.** u2219: "For the first three months I did not see any return on investment at all."
- **3–6 months: appraisal calls start.** u0660, u2219: "the three to six months you start to get called in on appraisals."
- **6+ months: sole-of-mind.** u2219: "From about six months onwards... I see you is in my little us. That is the only person I see in my little box."
- **Very long tail:** u2162 (2-year recall to call), u2328 (15-year relationship → listing).

### What the corpus does NOT contain (stated plainly, not invented)

- **No cost-per-response or ROI-per-dollar** on letterbox at scale (only per-piece cost: u1730, "$40... probably $20 and if you can get the boss... you're talking $10").
- **No A/B test data** comparing formats head-to-head with matched samples.
- **No data-first / analytics-led letterbox format** — every "value" letter in the corpus is a CMA or just-sold stat, not the kind of proprietary suburb-intelligence Fields produces. **This is a gap, and it is Fields' opening** (see plays below).
- **No digital-address-capture → mail loop** described; the corpus treats mail as outbound-only.

### Play 1 — "The Photo-Cover Intelligence Report" (bridges u0048 × k00173 × Fields' valuation engine)

**Bridge:** u0048 says the CMA's power is that "There's a picture of their house on the covering letter" — personalization forces the open. k00173 proves (503K properties) that print+online together produce a 20% sales lift. Fields already computes a **reconciled_valuation** and per-feature adjustments for ~9,232 houses. **The corpus's best-performing format (photo-cover report) has never been fused with a genuine proprietary valuation** — every corpus example uses a generic desktop CMA.

**Mechanism:** Post an unaddressed-mail-safe report whose cover is the recipient's own roofline (Fields already has aerial/satellite blob imagery) plus a one-line comparable **range** (never a single figure — CLAUDE.md Rule 5 / [[feedback_no_valuation_in_headlines]]), driving to the property's live page. This is the u0048 open-rate mechanic riding Fields' actual data moat, and the QR/URL closes the print→online loop k00173 quantifies. **Envelope-envelope caveat:** stay inside the **$1M–$2M design envelope** ([[valuation_design_envelope]]) — outside it, print "directional" language only, never a range.
