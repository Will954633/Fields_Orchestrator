# What eXp Realty and BLAC SALT actually say about posted mail

**2026-08-08** · Mined from 325 transcribed videos (**2.54 million words**) across the two channels.
Method: keyword-windowed extraction → 1,505 passages → three independent analysis passes.
Raw passages: [`shards/`](shards/) · pipeline: [`README.md`](README.md)

**⚠ EVIDENTIAL HEALTH WARNING, up front.** Across 2.5 million words there is **not one measured
response rate for a printed piece**. There is one costed drop with no outcome, one drop-cost
compared against one digital spend, one hand-audit of 30 listings, and an explicit admission from
inside the field that *"many agents never implement the systems in order to track the results from
the money being spent on the mailers."* Every conversion ratio quoted below — 1-in-2,000 drops, 270
buy letters per opportunity, 60/40, 80%-from-follow-up, 7.5–12.5% of a database moving — is
asserted by a coach with **no method, sample or citation.** Treat all of it as **priors to test, not
numbers to plan on.** The evidence lives in [`../02_Web/`](../02_Web/); this file is for **method and
language.**

Both channels are self-published marketing by organisations selling training or recruitment. And the
corpus is survivor testimony: 80 of 100 new agents quit within two weeks, so every success story
here comes from someone who did not.

## 1. ⚠ The finding that most threatens Fields' thesis

An Australian operator, on why the "what's your home worth" letter stopped working — **and the
reason he gives is precisely Fields' own product**:

> *"we've done price drive letters for years, which we're doing appraisals in your street. It's a
> really cold approach… they're just not having the same hit rate that they used to. I think a lot
> of people can find out roughly what their home is worth very quickly by lots of different websites
> and so needing an agent to give them a price update for the most part is it's not really what
> people are after anymore."*
> — Michael McClure, BLAC SALT · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v25-fTfD9_g · **[OPINION]**

**Take this seriously.** The commodity is the *number*, and the number is free everywhere. A mailer
whose proposition is "we will tell you what your home is worth" is competing with an instant, free,
zero-friction web estimate — and losing.

**What survives the objection** is the part the free tools cannot do: **the working.** The named,
dated, checkable sales. Each one adjusted, with the adjustment itself shown. The width of the range
as an honest statement of how little four sales can settle. The refusal to draw a line through a
quarter that cannot support one. Corroborated from the US side by the only speaker who addresses
automated valuations directly:

> *"I'll print off the page that shows what the value of their property is. Why do I do this? Because
> that's what they're looking at, right?… They're not accurate most of the time or maybe like all the
> time, right? Maybe 10% of the time."* — Brad Korn, eXp · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zN4LfkM3lp4

**Design consequence:** the piece must never lead with a figure, because the figure is not scarce.
It must lead with something the reader cannot get elsewhere. The `anchor` variant — *you already
have a number for this address; where did it come from?* — is the direct answer to this objection,
and this finding promotes it from "one of six variants" to **the opening piece.**

## 2. The format finding — an AU practitioner independently reproduces the RCT result

> *"the people that live in that home are getting every second week a price drive letter. Every second
> week. And my one isn't there. And my one isn't there is because we put ours in an envelope, whereas
> the rest of theirs are printing them and just sticking it in the letterbox. Which means that of
> these tenants that were living there, they were never taking it inside. It just stayed in the
> letterbox, whereas mine made it inside."*
> — Michael McClure · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t-bwrtAt-U · **[OPINION, but observational]**

This is the same result as Cochrane/Edwards 2009 (double postcard vs page-in-envelope, **OR 0.47** —
roughly halved response) and the same direction as Canada Post's own neuroscience ranking the
postcard **worst of five formats.** Three independent lines, two of them controlled, one of them a
guy watching what happens to paper in a letterbox.

**The envelope is the single most defensible format decision available**, and it kills the postcard
concept in `00_SCOPING.md`.

The same operator on switching his programme:

> *"our drops, we're tracking our lead sources and almost none of them were from drops directly… I'd
> say maybe one a year from drops. And so we changed last year… we now do a monthly market report
> that's delivered in an envelope… as soon as we've changed that, we're consistently getting probably
> three prizes a month."* — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kNKEDFCXJ9s · **[NUMBER, self-reported, no control]**

One a year → three a month, on the same audience, by putting it in an envelope and making it monthly
and informative. No control group, so treat it as a hypothesis — but it is the exact hypothesis this
programme would be testing.

## 3. Where the "post every week" idea comes from — and what it actually said

The weekly premise traces to one speaker:

> *"if you're working statistically a service area of about 1500 homes, it takes about seven weeks.
> Every week though, not once a month, every single week before that neighbourhood actually a large
> percentage knows you as an active agent… Most agents never do 1500 per week or eight consecutive
> weeks. I'd say less than one in a hundred do it."*
> — Manos Findikakis, BLAC SALT · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lIw45hahqHo · asserted as *"a proven fact"*, **no source**

> *"2000 drops in isolation. Don't bother doing it unless you're going to do it the following week,
> the following week… you need to be doing letterbox drops on a weekly basis until you have at least
> 10, 20, 30% market share… Then you can go to a fortnightly and possibly even a monthly drop."*
> — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fAPzK7SOOIM

**Three distinctions that change what this means for us:**

1. **It is about unaddressed letterbox drops**, hand-delivered at ~$0.25/home — not addressed mail
   at ~$2.50 all-in. **Ten times the unit cost changes the calculus completely.**
2. **The claimed outcome is recognition, not response** — *"knows you as an active agent"*. That is a
   brand-awareness claim, and awareness genuinely does respond to frequency in a way that response
   does not.
3. **The operator who drops the most says the drops do not produce leads.** McClure: *"I'd said $500
   submitted to one mass drop and I never get appraisals off it"* — against $300 of Facebook/Instagram
   producing *"three direct appraisals… and then five address searches."*

So the weekly doctrine is: cheap, unaddressed, for recognition, from one uncited voice, and
contradicted on outcomes by another operator in the same programme. Set against a survey-measured
tolerance ceiling of **one piece per month per sender**, ~63% measured cannibalisation between a
sender's own mailings, and a 7.6-day in-home life with response peaking near three weeks — **weekly
addressed mail is not supportable.** See [`../../01_Strategy/01-WHAT-THE-EVIDENCE-SUPPORTS.md`](../../01_Strategy/01-WHAT-THE-EVIDENCE-SUPPORTS.md) §2.3.

## 4. Australian cost anchors — useful, and one of them does not add up

| Item | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Dropper labour | *"25 cents a drop each"* | Joel Hood · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RLmQT4_pVMY |
| Full unaddressed drop, print + labour | **$500 per 2,000 ≈ $0.25/home** | McClure · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0t-bwrtAt-U |
| C5 posted envelope | *"a dollar 50 a stamp because they're a c5 envelope"* | Joel Hood |
| Fridge magnets | *"67 cents each"* (magnets.com.au) | BLAC SALT |
| Annual farm budget | *"this is going to cost me x 50 grand for 12 months"* | Joel Hood |
| Marketing as % of turnover | 3–5% of GCI | BLAC SALT |
| ⚠ **4,200 posted letters quarterly for "$1,200–1,500"** | ≈ **$0.29–0.36 each** | David Dwyer |

**That last one cannot be postage.** Australia Post's cheapest addressed tier (Promo Post, 4,000
minimum) is ~$0.83/article and unaddressed is ~$0.41. $0.29–0.36 is below both. It is almost
certainly print-only, or an unaddressed walk described loosely as "mail". **Do not plan against it.**
Get a real quote.

**"No junk mail" enforcement is real, not theoretical** — which corroborates the Queensland WRRA
s 107 finding in the legal doc:

> *"it does go against the requirements and I have had an agent breached"* — Immy Callister
> *"i've probably had 20 complaints… maybe 25 in the 18 or so months"* — Joel Hood

And the farm has to be netted down for it — McClure's 2,000 is *"taking away the junk mails and
housing commission houses."*

One more, offered as a personal 15-year observation and not measured: *"This year… there's been
probably a 50% drop in the frequency of agents dropping in the mailbox… when everyone pulls back,
that's the time that… it becomes even easier."*

## 5. The subscription framing — the strongest language finding

Two operators independently arrive at the same move, and it is **not** "here is your valuation". It
is **an offer of a standing relationship with recurring data**:

> *"i delivered cmas with basically the last five or ten sales to them and i'd say i give one of
> these to every single person in the area in this estate every three months for free… would you like
> to receive this every three months for free and they go what's the catch nothing i said it's just an
> opportunity for us to build a relationship may give you value over the years until you're ready."*
> — Paul Ogandzides · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MHhRg4xVP4I

> *"give them a market report, summary of their market and say, look, this is a market report that
> we're producing monthly… Would you like to be a receiver of this?"* — Christian Bartley · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lRLQ-IF0rwU

This is the **endowment framing** from the psychology doc arrived at independently — *"your report"*
rather than *"a report"* — and it maps directly onto Option 4 (the Standing Report), whose evidence
base (Opower) is the strongest in the whole file. **Quarterly, free, no catch, forever** is a
proposition that both the RCT literature and two Australian practitioners land on separately.

And a no-advice data hook that already satisfies CLAUDE.md Rule 5 verbatim:

> *"Did you know 35 property has just sold in the local market place in the last 60 days over the
> spring period and the highest sale price was 1.4 million, the lowest sale price was 800,000. If
> you're curious to know where your property lands, give me a call."*

## 6. Trigger events — including two lists Fields can build from its own data

**The "Magic 25s"** — mail the 25 nearest homes on each event, five event types: *just appraised ·
just listed · open-home invite · just sold · buyers missed out.* Note that **just appraised fires
whether or not you win the listing**: *"some people have said to me guys but manos what if i don't
list it it doesn't matter people aren't that intuitive."*

**Mail around your competitors' boards, not just your own** — justified by an unsourced 60/40 claim:
*"60% of sellers are influenced with the sold board in front of their property. 40% are not… you
should be prospecting around your competitors' boards to pick up that 40%."* Whatever the split, the
principle is sound and Fields is uniquely placed for it: **we hold every agent's sale events, not
just our own.** We have no boards at all, so this is the only version available to us anyway.

**⭐ Recent buyers are the highest-yield list named anywhere in the corpus, and Fields can build it
tonight:**

> *"When buyers resell, 80% do not use the agent they bought off… the highest I've seen is 50% sell
> within 12 to 36 months… if there was 100 sales in your service area last year… at least 20 of them
> are going to come to market 12 months from when they bought the property. Why 12 months? Because
> principal place of residence, they're not gonna pay tax on it."*

Unsourced, and the tax reasoning is shaky — but **the query is trivial for us.** We hold sale dates
for every property in four suburbs plus 53,313 historical events. "Bought 12–36 months ago" is a
one-line filter, it needs no browsing data, and the buyer has no incumbent relationship to displace.
This is a targeting idea that costs nothing to test and is **not currently in any option.**

**Withdrawn / lost listings, re-checked monthly for six months** — Brad Korn (eXp): *"if they list
with somebody else, I have a follow-up campaign that follows up reminds me every month for six
months to keep checking their listing."* Brain 1 separately names expired/withdrawn as the **#1
cold-start channel**, and it is a data-identification problem — the one thing we are structurally
best at.

**Neighbour-of-a-listing, automated** — Lofty (eXp vendor): *"Every time a listing hits the market,
it's going to market to the 10 houses, 15 houses around that listing with a direct postcard… it's
more likely the neighbors are more likely to list always."* Product claim, no results, but it is
Option 3's mechanic described by someone selling it.

## 7. What they say does not work

| Warning | Source |
|---|---|
| **Never ask "are you thinking of selling."** *"i just think that's a silly question… in my eyes people don't think of selling they think of upsizing selling as a solution"* | Ogandzides |
| **The 2/98 problem.** *"there's only about two percent of the population that are thinking of selling right now… all of our marketing material… are built around finding the two percent… but what we're doing is we're letting the 98 fall through our fingers"* | Ogandzides |
| **Half-committing is worse than not starting.** *"If you're not going to commit to doing drops don't do them… Nothing happens in isolation"* | Findikakis |
| **Single-shot just-listed/just-sold cards are a known money-loser.** *"the postcard just ends up in the local landfill"*; *"do not waste money on sending out one card… and then you never send another card again"* | Robertson (eXp) |
| **Your dropper may not be dropping.** Two independent accounts of undelivered material — *"the boot was full of letterbox drops… you just left them in your boot"* | BLAC SALT |
| **Recipients bin agent mail at the door.** *"I literally grab the mail and I go stand in front of the trash can… the other one is just like every door delivered one-page glossy realtor mail"* | eXp interviewer |
| **Generic newsletters.** *"three generic sentences that everyone knows it's not you"*; *"Please don't send them recipes. Please don't send them garbage they're not going to use"* | eXp |
| **Data alone loses the room.** *"Most agents these days are delivering a price finder or an RP data printout and paper and paper and paper… If you're delivering the exact same, expect no different result"* | Danny Grant |
| **Mail leads arrive colder** than content leads | Ali Garced interview (eXp) |
| **The stat without the interpretation is ignored.** *"you can't just make a video and say 'listings are up 35% in October'… but what does that mean?"* | Levi Lascsak (eXp) |

### ⚠ One warning that directly contradicts Fields' brand

> *"Because you gave them everything, mate"* … *"do not put the sold price on them… Because they
> will never click."* — Immy Callister, BLAC SALT

This is the opposite of everything Fields publishes, and it is the same instinct the shipped TEASE
guardrail deliberately rejects. **Do not adopt it** — withholding a fact about someone's own home to
force a click is exactly the leverage the guardrail exists to prevent, and Fields' entire
differentiation is transparency.

But it is worth recording as a **real, testable tension**: full disclosure may genuinely lower
click-through while raising trust. Those are different metrics, and Fields has deliberately chosen
the second. If the QR scan rate comes back low, this is the most likely explanation — and the
correct response is *not* to start withholding.

## 8. Farm sizes actually run, and the capacity rule

Sizes named by practitioners: **190** (Findikakis' first patch) · 700–754 · **1,188** (Ogandzides) ·
1,300 · 1,500 · 2,000 (Bartley) · 4,200 (Dwyer, posted) · 6,000 · 10,000.

The capacity claim — *"most million dollar agents service a suburb or an area of about 5,000
dwellings… If you've got 5,000, you need 5,000 to do a million"* — is immediately contradicted
in the same session: *"there's about 7,500 homes in Frankston South… we were servicing about 1000
of those homes. And we were able to be in the top three agents in the suburb."*

**The anti-pattern is stated explicitly**, and it is exactly Fields' current shape:

> *"I don't want people doing this. I drop 5,000 homes once every three months, instead of dropping
> 1000 homes every single week for three months."*

Turnover base rates are quoted inconsistently across the same corpus — 4% (*"one in 25 homes you
drive past"*), a member's measured **2.6%**, 5%, and *"eight to 12%… It is a statistical fact"*.
Our own ABS/Cotality computation is **4.67%**, and Brain 1 converges on 4–5% from three independent
units. **Use 4.67%. Ignore the 8–12%.**

Time-to-result, as claimed: six months to a first listing (Ogandzides) · *"four years for my figures
to take off"* (same) · 80% market share in five years after 6–12 months of 5 a.m. drops (Bartley) ·
12-month minimum commitment (Hood) · *"three to five years, not three to five days"* (Robertson).

## 9. What this changes in the strategy

1. **The envelope decision is settled.** Three independent lines agree. The postcard concept is dead.
2. **§1 promotes the `anchor` variant to piece 1.** The number is free; the working is not.
3. **The weekly premise is explained and set aside** — it is about 25-cent unaddressed drops for
   recognition, from one uncited voice, contradicted on outcomes within the same programme.
4. **Two new targeting lists, both free, both one query away:** homes bought 12–36 months ago, and
   withdrawn/expired listings re-checked monthly. Neither needs browsing data, neither has an
   incumbent relationship, and neither is in the current options.
5. **"Every quarter, free, no catch, forever"** is the language two AU operators reached independently
   and it is what Opower's RCT evidence supports. That is Option 4's script, already written.
6. **Mail around other agents' sold boards** — we hold every agent's events, and we have no boards
   of our own, so this is the only form of the play open to us.
