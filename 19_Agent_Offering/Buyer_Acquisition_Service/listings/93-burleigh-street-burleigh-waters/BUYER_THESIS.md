## The thesis, in one sentence

> **93 Burleigh Street lets a buyer acquire the two things that cannot be renovated — a big
> non-waterfront landholding and a walk-to-beach Burleigh position — at roughly $1.9m, when every
> comparable sale that paired those two things cost more than $2.2m. The price of entry is dated
> 1975 presentation, which is the one attribute on the list a buyer can actually change.**

Note what this thesis does **not** claim: it is not a "best combination of location, land *and house
size*" claim. House size is a weakness here, not a strength, and the data killed that leg outright
(see §Disproven). The thesis stands on **land + location + the gap to renovated stock**, and nothing
else.

## The proof, component by component

Every pillar below is either PROVEN by the comparable-sales dataset (`93_burleigh_comparables.csv`,
23 comps) or flagged as ⏳ pending the inspection.

### PROVEN — location is scarce at this land size

- **Only non-waterfront house over 800m² currently listed within 1.7km of Burleigh Beach.** Every
  other ≥750m² listing in that radius is waterfront (70 Burleigh $5.995M, 47 Kingfisher $2.75M,
  166 Dunlin $3.05M) or an acreage outlier (24 Tropicana, 3,591m²).
- Straight-line 1.487km to Burleigh Heads Beach. ⚠ Straight-line, not walking route — the real walk
  crosses the Gold Coast Highway. ⏳ **Verify the felt walk time on Saturday** before we publish any
  "X-minute walk" figure.

### PROVEN — the land is the asset, and it's genuinely large

- 822.4m² (Queensland cadastre). 155m from mapped water — dry, no canal-frontage cost and no canal
  exposure.
- Block geometry is a **wedge**, not a rectangle: ~18.3m front bounding to 49.7m deep; rectangularity
  0.908, the 49th percentile of 734 comparable-band Burleigh Waters lots. **Regular enough to be
  usable — median, not superior.** Frame as "regular, usable block", never "rectangular" or "better
  shaped than".

### PROVEN — the price gap to comparable sales is real, and it is a condition gap

Seven non-waterfront houses on 800m²+ sold within 1.7km of Burleigh Beach in the 24 months to
20 Aug 2026:

| Sold | Address | Land m² | Price | Condition |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| 2024-12 | 5 Turaco Ct | 833 | **$2,196,785** | fully renovated 0–5 yrs |
| 2025-07 | 59 Bluejay St | 858 | $2,475,000 | fully renovated 0–5 yrs |
| 2026-04 | 29 Acanthus Ave *(Bgh Heads)* | 844 | $2,850,000 | — |
| 2025-05 | 17 Kingfisher Cres | 900 | $2,850,000 | reno 5–10 yrs |
| 2024-11 | 15 Curlew Cres | 815 | $2,930,000 | cosmetically updated 0–5 yrs |
| 2025-02 | 200 Acanthus Ave | 913 | $3,465,000 | reno 0–5 yrs |
| 2025-08 | 85 Burleigh St | 929 | $3,475,000 | reno 5–10 yrs |

- **None sold below $2,196,785.** At $1,915,000, 93 Burleigh is priced beneath the entire comparable
  cohort — by ~$282,000 against the cheapest.
- **Five of the seven were renovated within ten years.** The gap is a renovation gap: the buyer pays
  less and inherits the work, rather than paying more for someone else's finished taste.
- $/m² of land across the seven: $2,636–$3,797. 93 at $1,915,000 = **$2,330/m²** — below the range.

### PROVEN — parking and workshop are a genuine point of difference

- 4 car spaces + the 7.0 × 6.2m workshed. ⚠ We hold no "shed" field anywhere in the database, so
  absence in comparables is not evidence of absence — do not claim "the only one with a shed". Claim
  only what this property has.

### ⏳ PENDING INSPECTION — the downstairs, and the "dated vs money-pit" question

The whole condition argument rests on one distinction the data cannot settle: **is this cosmetically
dated, or does it need real money?** If it's cosmetic, the buyer pool is families who'd live in it
while planning; if it's a money pit, it's renovators only. Resolve on Saturday (inspection brief §D),
plus the downstairs kitchenette/bathroom/MPR — its own entrance, whether it closes off, whether the
kitchenette is plumbed (brief §B).

### ⏳ PENDING DOCUMENTS — B&P, and redevelopment

B&P report (needed before any "building and pest is clear" claim); the "Res B / 6m relaxation"
documentation (our council data says Low density residential, no recorded relaxation — development
stays a **secondary** angle, not a pillar, until a document exists).

## The one uncomfortable comparison the buyer pack must answer

**5 Turaco Court** — fully renovated, 833m², sold **$2,196,785**. A buyer will ask: why pay ~$1.9m
for an unrenovated house when a renovated one on the same land sold for ~$2.2m? The honest answer is
the thesis itself: **~$280k of headroom to renovate to your own taste, versus paying for someone
else's.** If the inspection shows the house needs more than ~$280k of *structural* work to be
liveable, this thesis weakens materially — which is exactly why §D of the inspection matters most.

## What a sceptical buyer's agent will attack

1. **Walking distance** — straight-line understates it; the real route crosses the highway.
2. **Flood overlay** — not in the comp dataset; unanswered here. Handle it with the ICA framing on
   the landing page, don't leave it silent.
3. **Condition** — "dated" vs "needs money" is asserted, not yet proven. Saturday closes this.
4. **Our sold set is a floor** — a competitor with RP Data/Pricefinder may hold a sub-$2.2m sale we
   don't. If one exists on 800m²+ within 1.7km, the headline weakens. Stated honestly, sourced.

---

**Bottom line:** the thesis is sound and the evidence is strong on land, location and the price gap.
It is **not** a size or "best-of" story, and pretending otherwise is the fastest way to lose it. The
two open risks — condition and flood — are both answerable, and Saturday answers the bigger one.
