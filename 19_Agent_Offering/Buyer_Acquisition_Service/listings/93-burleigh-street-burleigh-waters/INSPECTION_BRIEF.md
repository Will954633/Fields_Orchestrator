## B. ⭐ The downstairs — our headline finding

The floor plan shows a **6.3 × 5.1 m multi-purpose room with its own kitchenette (3.1 × 2.6), its own
bathroom and a laundry**. The Domain advertisement never mentions any of it. This is the single
biggest under-sold feature, and the whole "extended family / teenagers / work from home" thesis
rests on what you find here.

- Does the downstairs area have **its own external entrance**, or only internal access?
- Can it be **closed off** from the rest of the house — a door, a landing, a separate stair?
- Is the kitchenette **plumbed and working** (sink, power, any cooking appliance)?
- Is the bathroom **full** (shower/bath) or a powder room?
- Ceiling height downstairs — does it feel like a proper room or a converted underhouse?
- Natural light and ventilation — windows, or is it dark?
- Does the second multi-purpose room (5.4 × 3.4) connect to this, or is it separate?

⚠️ Photograph and video all of it. **Do not** ask Tyler whether it is "approved dual living" in a way
that puts him on the spot — ask factually what is there, and separately ask whether the owners hold
any approvals. We describe what exists; we do not make planning claims.

## C. The things the photos cannot tell a buyer

- **Time the beach walk.** Start at the front gate, walk to the sand, record the time and the route.
  The ad claims "just 1 kilometre". Is it a pleasant 12-minute walk or an unpleasant crossing of a
  main road? This is the single most repeated claim in the campaign — verify it yourself.
- **Street noise.** Our aerial shows the property fronting a **curved through-road with a painted 50
  marking** and moving traffic. Stand at the front boundary, the middle of the block and the rear
  fence. Record 30 seconds of audio at each. Is the rear yard quiet even if the front isn't? That
  distinction decides whether traffic is a dealbreaker or a non-issue.
- **Shed access.** Can a boat or trailer actually reach the 7.0 × 6.2 m shed past the carport? What
  is the narrowest point? Photograph the pinch point. This is the whole tradie/boating campaign.
- **Orientation, light and breeze.** Which way does the rear yard face? Where does afternoon sun
  land? Is there a sea breeze? (Plan marks north to the right of the page.)
- **Parking manoeuvrability.** Can you get in and out of the driveway onto a through-road safely?

## D. Dated versus actually needs money

The most valuable distinction in the entire campaign. A buyer's fear is a money pit; the reality may
be a cosmetic refresh.

Go room by room and sort every item into one of two columns:

| **Dated — cosmetic, cheap** | **Needs real money** |
|---|---|
| e.g. laminate benchtops, carpet, paint colours, light fittings, tapware | e.g. rewiring, re-roofing, restumping, plumbing, structural, waterproofing |

Specifically assess: kitchen (laminate benches, standard appliances per our photo analysis) ·
all three bathrooms · flooring (carpet, recorded as "fair") · windows (aluminium) · roof (tile) ·
external brick and paint (recorded "fair"/"good") · any damp, cracking or movement.

**Does the house feel immediately liveable, or does it need work before you could move in?** That
one answer determines whether the buyer pool is renovators-only or includes families who would live
in it while planning.

## E. Flood — look for physical evidence

Council mapping puts ground level **4.03 m** against a designated flood level of **4.18 m** — 0.15 m
below, with modelled depth under 30 cm. The address is in **none** of the five ICA insurance zones.

- Is the house **raised** — steps up to the entry, a suspended floor, or slab on ground?
- Any visible water marks, or evidence of past inundation in the garage/downstairs?
- Does the block fall toward or away from the street?
- Ask Tyler whether the owners have ever experienced water on the property, and whether they hold
  any flood documentation or an insurance history.

## F. The question we most need answered

**Why hasn't it sold in 163 days?**

Ask Tyler directly, and listen for what he *doesn't* say:

- What feedback have buyers given at inspections?
- How many inspections, how many contracts, and what happened to the one that fell over in May?
- What were the objections — price, condition, the road, flood, the beach distance, something else?
- Which buyer type has shown the most interest so far?
- What did the previous contract fall over on? (Finance? Building and pest? Buyer's own sale?)

We need to know why people **haven't** bought it, not just why they should. Everything in the
campaign should be aimed at the actual objection, not one we imagine.
