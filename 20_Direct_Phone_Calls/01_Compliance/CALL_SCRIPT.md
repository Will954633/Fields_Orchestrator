# CALL SCRIPT — Fields Real Estate, outbound to Gold Coast homeowners

v1 2026-08-15 · Read with `CALLER_CARD.md` open beside it. Legal points trace to `00_SCOPING.md`.

**This is not a reading script.** The bold lines are the shape of the sentence, not the words you must use. Say them the way you'd say them to a neighbour over a fence. The only parts that must survive intact are the four opening elements and the recording question — everything else is yours to phrase.

**Before you dial:** have the row open. You need the **street**, the **hook** (the specific thing we know about their street or their home), and the **number's wash date**. If the hook column is empty, don't dial that row — a call with no specific thing to offer is just an agent asking if they want to sell, and that is not what we are.

---

## 1. OPENING — the first fifteen seconds

Four legally required elements (Standard s9(2)), said naturally, in one breath-and-a-half:

> **"Hi, is that [name]? — it's [your given name] calling from Fields Real Estate. We're the ones who publish the sales data and the market analysis for [suburb]. I'm calling about what's actually been selling on [street name] — and if it's useful to you, to send you the numbers for your own place."**

That covers: your name · the business · who caused the call · **the purpose**. Don't split it up, don't bury the purpose, don't lead with a question about their plans.

If they cut in with "who?" or "what's this about?" — that's fine, they're engaged. Repeat the purpose, not the preamble.

---

## 2. THE RECORDING MOMENT — immediately after, before anything else

> **"Before we go on — this call's recorded so I've got an accurate record of what I told you. Is that alright?"**

**Stop talking. Wait.** Silence is not a yes. A "mm" is not a yes.

- **"Yeah, that's fine."** → **"Thanks."** Continue. Log consent YES.
- **"No."** → **"No problem — I'll stop that now."** Stop the recording (or, if you can't, end the call and note it). Then either continue unrecorded or wind up. Log consent NO.
- **"Why are you recording?"** → **"So there's a record of exactly what I said to you — I'd rather that exist than not. If you'd rather I didn't, that's completely fine."**

Why we do this: IPA **s45(1)** makes *using* or sharing a recording, or a transcript made from it, the offence; **s45(2)(a)** consent of all parties is the fix; **s49A** makes it Will's personal exposure. This is not a formality.

---

## 3. THE HOOK — lead with what we can tell them

The principle: **we open a gap and offer to close it.** We are not asking them for anything at this point in the call. We know something specific about their street; we say enough that it's real, and we offer the rest.

Three shapes that work. Pick by what's in the hook column.

**(a) A sale they'll have noticed**
> **"There was a sale on [street] a few weeks back — [X] bedrooms, [Y] square metres. Most people on the street will have seen the sign go up but never found out what it actually went for, or how it compared to the two before it. We've got that, and we've got where your place sits relative to it."**

**(b) Something structural about the street**
> **"We track [suburb] block by block. What's interesting about [street] is [the specific thing — supply, land size, time on market, the gap to the suburb median]. It's not something you'd see from the suburb-level numbers, which is mostly what's out there."**

**(c) Their home specifically**
> **"We've got a page for your address — sales history, the comparable homes nearby, the land. Most of what's public about a property is one line and a photo. Ours has the working."**

Then the offer, not the ask:

> **"I'm not calling to ask whether you're selling. I'm calling because we've done the work on your street and it seems daft that it sits on our side of the fence. Would it be useful if I sent it through?"**

**Rules while you're in here:** no figure said aloud (s215 — §4 below). No "buyers are looking at your street" unless you can log the grounds (s212(4)–(5), the onus is on us). No opinion on whether they should do anything with any of it.

---

## 4. BRANCHES

### (a) "Not interested." / "We're not selling." / "Not a good time."

**Stop. This is Standard s13(1)(b) and it is immediate.**

> **"No worries at all — thanks for picking up. Have a good one."**

Hang up. **No rebuttal, no "just quickly", no different angle, no callback.** Log the outcome and flag it: **no contact for 30 days** (ACL **s75(2)**). If they said "take me off your list", that's permanent, not 30 days — mark it for deletion too.

The temptation here is the whole risk. A soft "nah, we're right" is an indication they want the call to end. Treat it as one.

---

### (b) ⭐ "What's it worth?"

The one we want, and the one with the trap. **Do not say a number.** POA **s215**: answer a would-be seller's price question and you owe a full comparative market analysis on the spot — ≥3 sales in 6 months, similar, within 5km (Sch 2). ≈$93,000.

> **"I'm not going to throw a number at you over the phone, because it'd be a guess and you'd have no way of checking it. What's actually useful is the sales it'd be based on — what sold near you, what those places had, how yours lines up against them. I'll put that together properly and send it to you. No charge, nothing attached to it."**

If they push — *"Just roughly? Ballpark?"*:

> **"Genuinely can't do rough — it's the bit people get burnt by. Give me your email and you'll have the real version with the comparable sales in it, and you can judge it yourself."**

Then capture: **name · email or postal address · confirm the property address.** That's the whole conversion. Don't book anything else on top of it, don't ask about timing, don't ask if they've had other agents through.

Note for later: a CMA given to a seller can't be given to anyone else without their **written** approval (**s216(6)**).

---

### (c) "How did you get my number?"

**APP 7.6.** They can ask, and the only honest answer is that we bought it. Say it plainly and quickly — hedging here is what makes people angry, not the fact itself.

> **"Fair question. We licence contact data from a provider called ID4ME and match it against the address — you never gave it to us, and I'd rather tell you that straight. If you'd rather we didn't hold it, tell me and I'll get it taken off."**

If they *did* give it to us (Track A — Facebook lead form, or the Analyse Your Home tool), say exactly that and roughly when:

> **"You filled in [the form on our site / a Facebook form] on [date] and left your number — that's the only reason I've got it."**

If they ask us to delete it: that ends the call (branch a) **and** gets flagged for deletion.

---

### (d) "We've already got an agent."

If an appointment **is in force**, POA Reg **s21** applies: **do not solicit an appointment.** Not softly, not "when it expires", not "just so you know what we'd do differently".

> **"All good — I'm not going to get in the middle of that and I'm not asking you for anything. The street data's on our site if you ever want to look at it yourself. Best of luck with it."**

Wind up. Log "has agent" so the row is suppressed.

(The 39 "Listing Nearing Expiry" leads are excluded from round 1 for exactly this reason. Expired and withdrawn appointments sit outside s21 — but they're not in this round either, so on this round the rule is simple: agent mentioned, call ends.)

---

### (e) Genuinely interested

They want the data. Sometimes they'll start talking about their own plans unprompted — let them, listen, don't steer.

**What we book: nothing but the send.**

> **"Easiest thing — I'll email you the comparable sales for your place this week. Have a read, and if you want to talk about it after that, you know where I am."**

Capture: **name · email or postal · property address confirmed.** Optionally, if *they* raise a timeframe: **"Want me to give you a call back after you've had a look? What suits?"** → put the date in column M and honour it.

**Do not**, on a cold call: propose an in-person appraisal appointment, discuss commission, or take any step toward a listing authority. A listing won by cold call is presumed an unsolicited consumer agreement (ACL **s70**) with a 10-business-day cooling-off (**s82(3)(b)**) during which we **cannot supply the service** (**s86(1)**) — market it inside that window and their termination right stretches to six months (**s82(3)(d)**). If a call heads that way, stop and hand it to Will.

---

## 5. THE CLOSE

Regardless of branch, end the same way — short, no last pitch:

> **"Appreciate you taking the call. If anything comes up, our number's the one on your phone now and someone will answer it."**

(Our return number stays reachable for 30 days — Standard s14(1),(3).)

**Log immediately, in the sheet, while it's fresh:**

| Column | What goes in |
|---|---|
| K — Outcome | connected / no answer / wrong number / not interested / **sending data** / has agent / do not call |
| L — Comments | what you actually said, and **the grounds for any claim you made** (s212(4)–(5) onus is on us). Any figure discussed. Anything they told you about the property. |
| M — Call back on | only if they asked for one |
| — Recording consent | Y / N |
| — Stop flag | starts the 30-day no-contact block (s75(2)) |

An unlogged claim is an unprovable claim. If you said it, write it down.

---

## 6. TONE

**We are not an agent asking if they want to sell.** Every homeowner in these suburbs has had that call, and they have a reflex for it. We are the people who publish the data on their street, ringing to hand some of it over. If the call feels like the first thing, it's wrong.

- **Offer, don't ask.** The first thing out of your mouth is something we have. The ask, if there is one, is only ever "can I send it".
- **Data only. Their conclusion.** Say what sold, what it had, how it compares. Never what it means for them, never what they should do. *"That's your call — all I can do is show you the numbers."* If you catch yourself starting a sentence with "I'd say you should", stop mid-sentence.
- **Under-claim.** If you're unsure of a number, say you're unsure and that you'll send the checkable version. Being the one who wouldn't guess is the entire differentiator. A homeowner should hang up thinking *they'd describe my house honestly.*
- **Buyer-first is not a slogan.** We build the audience by being useful for free. A call that gives something and asks nothing is on-strategy even when it converts nothing.
- **Plain, flat, unhurried.** No enthusiasm inflation, no urgency, no "I just happened to be in the area". Short sentences. Full price figures if written, never "1.4m". Suburbs capitalised.
- **The graceful exit is part of the product.** Half these calls end in ten seconds. Ending them warmly and instantly is the job, not a failure of it — and it's the legal requirement (s13(1)(b)). Nobody should ever describe us as the ones who wouldn't get off the phone.

3. **What exactly the "comparable sales" email is** — which existing artefact (property page, appraisal, off-market report) fulfils the §4(b) promise, who generates it, and the turnaround we can honestly promise on the call. The script says "this week"; that needs to be true.

5. **Whether to voluntarily block QLD-only public holidays** — Standard s8(3) catches Cth holidays only. Not decided.

7. **Wrong number / previous occupant** — ID4ME median record age is 3.12 years, so this will be common. No agreed wording, and no agreed rule on whether the new occupant may then be pitched. Assume not.
