Purpose, and the limits of this document

This is a desktop assessment of market value prepared by Fields Real Estate using the direct comparison method. It was produced without an inspection, without contact with the owner, and without a licensed valuer.

It exists because the two things normally on offer — an instant online estimate with no working shown, and an agent appraisal that begins a sales conversation — both ask you to accept a number on trust. This document takes the opposite approach: every comparable sale, every dollar of adjustment, every weight, and every thing we could not observe is set out below so you can disagree with it in specific terms.

This is not a certified valuation

This report has not been prepared by a Certified Practising Valuer and is not a valuation under the Australian Property Institute’s professional practice standards. No member of the API has inspected the property or signed this document.

It cannot be relied upon for mortgage security or lending, litigation or family law proceedings, stamp duty or land tax objections, probate or deceased estate administration, insurance reinstatement, financial reporting, or any other purpose requiring a valuation by a registered valuer. For any of those, engage a Certified Practising Valuer.

It is prepared for the owner or occupier of the subject property, for their own information. Fields Real Estate accepts no liability to any third party who obtains a copy of it, and no duty of care arises to any party relying on it for a transaction.

No advice is given or implied

Nothing here is a recommendation to sell, to hold, to buy, or to list at any particular price. This document reports what comparable properties have sold for and what our method infers from that. What to do with that information is entirely yours, and depends on circumstances we know nothing about.

A sale price is set by a buyer on a day, and can fall outside every figure below.

Method

Direct comparison. Sales of similar properties are adjusted, line by line, for each respect in which they differ from the subject, producing for each one an indication of what the subject would have sold for on that sale’s date. Those indications are then weighted and reconciled to a single figure, and a measured band is placed around it.

50 sales within Robina were examined, adjusted and weighted, and all of them inform the reconciled figure. A sale needing a large adjustment, or sitting further away, or older, is not discarded — it is down-weighted, so that it counts for something in proportion to how much it can fairly say about this house.

Of those, the 8 strongest are set out in full in sections 5 to 7 as the evidence you can actually check. Section 7 is explicit about the gap between what is displayed and what is calculated.

  1. Selection — candidate sales screened on dwelling type, proximity, recency and completeness of attribute data.
  2. Adjustment — each adopted sale adjusted against a published rate schedule (section 4).
  3. Reliability shrinkage — every adjustment multiplied by 0.80. Adjustment rates are estimated, not known; shrinking them toward zero pulls the result back toward the observed sale price, which is the one genuinely hard fact in the calculation.
  4. Weighting — the six factors in section 6.
  5. Reconciliation — weighted mean of the adjusted indications across the whole candidate pool, not only the sales displayed in section 5. Section 7 sets out what that distinction means for reading this report.
  6. Suburb calibration — multiplied by 1.0189, which corrects Robina’s measured systematic bias as at 08 August 2026.
  7. Band — ±12.2%, being the width that contained four in five actual Robina sales when the method was tested against them (section 7).
Scope of the method

This method is built and measured for detached houses selling between $1,000,000 and $2,000,000. Outside that band it degrades, because a weighted mean of adjusted comparables cannot exceed its most expensive comparable and the pool of available sales pulls toward the middle of the market.

This property is assessed at $1,521,883 and sits inside that band, so the figures above are reported in full. Had it fallen outside, we would have withheld both the figure and the range and shown you the comparable evidence only.

The eight are the evidence; the figure is reconciled from all 50

The point figure of $1,521,883 is not the weighted mean of these eight sales. It is reconciled across the full pool of 50 candidate sales examined in Robina, then calibrated. The eight set out above are the highest-quality subset, selected to be shown to you as legible evidence — they carry 19.1% of the total weight behind the figure.

Two consequences you should know. First, the spread of the eight ($1,328,323 – $1,674,272) is narrower than the real evidence spread, which across all 50 runs $1,098,649 – $1,974,456. The selection shown to you flatters the apparent agreement. Second, the weighted mean of these eight alone comes to $1,500,343 — within a few hundred dollars of the reported figure. That closeness is a coincidence of this property, not a property of the method, and we would rather tell you that than let it read as confirmation.

We show eight rather than 50 because a grid of 50 columns is not evidence anyone can check. The full pool is in the downloadable figures.

The Robina calibration factor of 1.0189 is applied to the reconciled figure and to both ends of the range, so the band stays centred on the corrected estimate. It corrects a systematic tendency in this suburb measured on 08 August 2026.

The adopted indications have a standard deviation of $139,885, or 9.4% of the mean — a measure of how much the comparables disagree with each other, which is a different and much smaller thing than how wrong the assessment might be. The band in section 9 is the honest measure of the latter.

The street premium rests on three sales

Three sales is a thin sample, and one of them — 25 Huntingdale Crescent — is also an adopted comparable in this assessment. The damping factor of 0.5 and the 15% cap exist precisely because a small sample can produce an extreme average: the raw 19.27% is halved before use. Treat the street premium as the least robust input to this assessment.

The band is an output of that test, not a setting. It is the width that had to be drawn around our figures for four in five actual sales to land inside it, and it differs by suburb: ±11.2% in Varsity Lakes, ±12.2% here in Robina, ±14.0% in Burleigh Waters. A single pooled number would have flattered the strong suburbs and broken the promise in the weak one.

Read the band correctly

±12.2% is not a confidence interval and this report will not describe it as one. Four sales in five landed inside a band of this width. One in five did not. Roughly one house in twenty-five sells above every comparable available to assess it, and no method built on comparable sales can anticipate that.

We also do not publish a confidence grade for individual properties. When we tested whether our own high-confidence assessments were in fact more accurate than our low-confidence ones, they were not reliably so. Publishing a grade we cannot stand behind would be exactly the unearned reassurance this document is meant to avoid.

Assumptions and limiting conditions

  1. No inspection. Neither the interior nor the exterior has been inspected. All physical attributes are taken from cadastral records, historic listing data and satellite imagery, and are assumed correct.
  2. Good repair assumed. The improvements are assumed to be in structurally sound condition, free of defect, infestation, contamination and hazardous materials. No structural, pest or building survey has been undertaken and none is implied.
  3. Title assumed clear. No title search has been performed. Clear and marketable title is assumed, free of encumbrances, easements, covenants, caveats or disputes not evident from public records.
  4. Planning and approvals. All improvements are assumed to be lawfully erected and approved. No enquiry has been made of the local authority.
  5. Flood, bushfire and overlays. No site-specific hazard assessment has been undertaken for this report. Prospective purchasers should make their own enquiries.
  6. Vacant possession. Value is assessed on the basis of vacant possession of the fee simple interest, with no account taken of any lease, tenancy or occupancy arrangement.
  7. Chattels excluded. The assessment relates to land and improvements only. No furnishings, plant or removable fixtures are included.
  8. Market conditions at the date of assessment. The assessment speaks as at 20 August 2026 and takes no account of events after that date. It assumes an arm’s length transaction after adequate marketing, between a willing but not anxious buyer and seller, neither under compulsion.
  9. Comparable sale data. Sale prices are taken from published records and verified where possible; 45 of the 50 sales examined were independently verified. Sales data may be subject to later revision, and the circumstances of any individual sale are not known to us.
