# Renovation Premium Study — Results (2026-09-09)

**Question:** Does full renovation add sale price in Robina, Varsity Lakes, Burleigh Waters —
and does the *Before You List* Chapter 5 claim survive proper controls?

**The claim under test** (book p.58; also `001_Our_Competitive_Advantages/10_KILLER_DATA_CLAIMS_FOR_THE_REPORT.md`):
> "In Robina and Varsity Lakes, fully renovated properties showed **lower** price per square metre
> than original-condition properties. In Burleigh Waters, renovation appeared to add approximately
> **+14%** to the price per square metre."

The book itself flagged a floor-area confounder ("further analysis is underway"). This is that analysis.

**Script:** `renovation_premium_study.py` (this folder) — fully reproducible.
**Data:** 578 sold houses (24-month window to 2026-09-07), vision-model renovation tier
(`property_valuation_data.renovation`), parsed sale price, floor area, land size, beds/baths,
water class (`shared/waterfront.py`; 14 specialist-market waterfront sales excluded), quarter of sale.
n = Robina 246, Burleigh Waters 200, Varsity Lakes 132. `new_build` excluded (29).

---

## Verdict

| Book claim | Result |
|---|---|
| Burleigh Waters: renovation adds ~+14% | **CONFIRMED — and likely understated.** Hedonic +16.1% [4.2, 29.5], p=0.007. Matched twins +22.9% [11.4, 37.7]. |
| Robina: renovated homes worth *less* | **REFUTED — sign was wrong.** With floor area controlled, fully renovated Robina homes show **+11.7%** [5.7, 18.0], p=0.0001. The book's negative $/sqm gap was the floor-area confounder it itself flagged: renovated homes are bigger, and bigger homes have lower $/sqm. |
| Varsity Lakes: renovated homes worth *less* | **REFUTED as a negative; premium positive but uncertain.** +6.9% [-0.4, 14.8], p=0.065. Small treated cell (n=26). |
| The *suburb difference* (BW rewards renovation more) | **CONFIRMED.** BW's premium exceeds Robina's by ~10pp (interaction p=0.002) and Varsity's by ~7pp (p=0.08). The demographic story (turnkey buyers in BW) survives; the negative sign in Robina does not. |

## Three legs, one direction (24-month window)

| | Leg 1: strata medians (solid cells) | Leg 2: hedonic (primary) | Leg 3: matched twins |
|---|---|---|---|
| Burleigh Waters | +10% to +17% (4 of 5 cells positive) | **+16.1%** [4.2, 29.5] p=.007 | **+22.9%** [11.4, 37.7], 31/70 matched |
| Robina | −7% to +13% (4 of 5 cells positive) | **+11.7%** [5.7, 18.0] p=.0001 | **+10.5%** [1.3, 16.6], 32/71 matched |
| Varsity Lakes | −5% to +15% (2 of 3 cells positive) | **+6.9%** [−0.4, 14.8] p=.065 | **+8.3%** [0.1, 20.6], 8/26 matched |

Leg 2 = `log(price) ~ tier + log(floor) + log(land) + beds + baths + water class + quarter FE`, HC1 SEs,
tier baseline = original+tired. Leg 3 = each fully renovated sale vs unrenovated near-twins
(same beds & water class, floor ±20%, land ±25%, sale ±6 months), prices time-adjusted by suburb
quarterly median deflator. 12-month sensitivity cut: same signs, same ordering
(BW +24.4%, Robina +12.8%, Varsity +2.6% ns) — `results_12m.json`.

Tier ladder is monotonic where it matters: `cosmetically_updated` and `partially_renovated` sit at
≈0% vs original everywhere — **only `fully_renovated` carries a detectable premium.** Cosmetic
updating does not price like renovation; it prices like original.

## Which items carry it? (secondary)

Per-item regression (kitchen / bathrooms / flooring booleans + `modern_features_score`, all controls):
no single item shows an independent premium once overall modernity is held constant — the items are
too collinear with each other and with finish level. The vision `modern_features_score` carries the
signal in all three suburbs: **+4.4% to +5.5% per point, p≤0.01 everywhere.** Read: buyers price the
*overall finished state*, not the presence of any one renovated room. A renovated kitchen inside an
otherwise tired house is not detectably rewarded.

## What this does NOT say (mandatory caveats for any public use)

1. **Premium ≠ ROI.** We observe the price premium, never the renovation cost. +16% on a $1,700,000
   BW home ≈ $270,000 — a full renovation can cost that. The book's "renovation usually doesn't
   recover its cost" framing can remain true *even with these premiums*; what changes is the claimed
   *sign* of the market effect and the suburb ranking.
2. **Selection bias survives.** Owners renovate homes worth renovating. Part of each premium is the
   house, not the renovation. All three legs share this limit; observational data cannot remove it.
3. **Vision-model measurement.** Tiers come from listing photos (GPT-4 photo analysis). Misclassification
   attenuates estimates toward zero — so positive findings are believable; the weak Varsity result is
   partly a power problem (26 treated sales, 5 original).
4. **Thin `original` baselines** in Robina (6) and Varsity (5). The binary contrast
   (fully renovated vs all lower tiers) used in the interaction model is the robust one.
5. Waterfront (canal/river/ocean frontage) excluded per platform policy; lakefront/water-view retained
   as controls.
