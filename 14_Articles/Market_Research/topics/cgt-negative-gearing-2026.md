# Australia's 2026 negative-gearing & CGT reform

**Topic dossier · evergreen · as at 2026-08-24**
**Scope:** the negative-gearing and capital-gains-tax changes announced in the 2026-27
Federal Budget (budget night, 12 May 2026), their mechanics, timeline, projected
impacts, and market effect to date. Primary source: the **Treasury budget factsheet**
"Negative Gearing and Capital Gains Tax Reform" (read in full), corroborated by ATO,
ABC, Baker McKenzie, Grattan.

> **Bottom line.** From **1 July 2027** the Government will (1) **limit negative gearing
> to new builds** — losses on *established* residential investment property bought after
> 7:30pm 12 May 2026 can offset only residential-property income, not wages — and (2)
> **replace the 50% CGT discount** with **CPI cost-base indexation + a 30% minimum tax
> rate** on real gains accruing after 1 July 2027. Existing holdings are grandfathered;
> new builds keep both concessions; the main residence stays exempt. Treasury models
> **small** effects (price growth ~2% lower over a couple of years, rents up <$2/week).
> **The reform is not the cause of the 2026 price falls** — it does not take effect until
> 2027 and is confounded by the rate environment (see [national-market-turn-2026](national-market-turn-2026.md)).

**FACT vs COMMENTARY:** mechanics/dates/thresholds/transitional rules and Treasury's own
modelling are FACT (primary factsheet). Causal attribution of price moves and rent-impact
magnitude are CONTESTED/COMMENTARY. Unverified items listed at the end.

## 1. Exact mechanics (primary — Treasury factsheet)

**Negative gearing**
- From 1 July 2027, losses on **established** residential investment property **bought
  from 7:30pm AEST 12 May 2026** are deductible **only against residential-property
  income (incl. residential capital gains)** — not salary/wages. Excess losses **carry
  forward**. [factsheet p.1]
- **New builds exempt** — investors buying new builds keep full negative gearing **and**
  the 50% CGT discount. [p.4]
- Applies to individuals, partnerships, companies, most trusts. **Excluded:** widely-held
  trusts (most MITs) and **super funds incl. SMSFs**. Residential property only —
  commercial and shares unaffected. [p.1, p.4]
- Scale: ~1% of taxfilers acquire a negatively geared property each year (~230,000
  individuals, 2022-23). [p.4]

**CGT**
- The **50% discount** (individuals/trusts/partnerships) replaced by **(a) CPI cost-base
  indexation** (as 1985-1999) **and (b) a 30% minimum tax rate** on real gains accruing
  from 1 July 2027; no impact until realised; no effect on those already taxed ≥30%.
  Means-tested income-support recipients exempt from the minimum tax in the realisation
  year. [p.1-2]
- Applies to assets held ≥12 months. **Unchanged:** main-residence exemption, the four
  small-business CGT concessions, and the **60% discount for qualifying affordable
  housing**. [p.2, p.4]

## 2. Timeline & transitional rules (primary)

- **Announced** 7:30pm AEST 12 May 2026; **effective 1 July 2027** (both measures).
- **Negative-gearing grandfathering:** held at announcement (incl. contracted-not-settled)
  → keeps negative gearing indefinitely; bought announcement→30 Jun 2027 → negative gear
  only until 30 Jun 2027; bought from 1 Jul 2027 (established) → none against non-residential
  income. [p.3]
- **CGT transition:** bought & sold pre-1 Jul 2027 → unchanged; owned-before-sold-after →
  **split** (pre-period gets 50% discount, post-period gets indexation + minimum tax; the
  1 Jul 2027 value is the cost base for the new-rules portion), via either a **valuation at
  1 Jul 2027** or an **ATO apportionment formula**. Pre-1985 assets deemed reacquired at
  market value 1 Jul 2027. [p.3-4]
- **New-build eligibility:** off-the-plan, KDR that *increases* dwelling count, builds on
  vacant land = eligible; extensions/granny flats/like-for-like rebuilds, and **subsequent
  purchasers** of a new build = **not** eligible; a new build occupied >12 months before
  first sale loses eligibility. [p.4, Table 2]

## 3. Projected impacts (Treasury published these forecasts)

- Price growth **~2% lower over a couple of years** ("small and temporary" — a growth
  slowdown, **not** a level fall). [p.5; ABC 12 May 2026]
- Rents up **<$2/week** (~$100/yr).
- **~75,000 additional owner-occupiers over the decade** (investor→owner-occupier shift).
- **~35,000 fewer homes built** from lower investor demand, stated "more than offset" by
  other budget supply measures.
- Revenue: ABC reports **$77.2bn over a decade / $3.6bn short-term**; a secondary
  "$20bn+/year within a decade" figure **could not be reconciled** — treat as unsettled.
- **Independent:** OECD's Australia report had recommended cutting the CGT discount and
  phasing out negative gearing (noted in the factsheet); **Grattan** supportive ("the
  budget we've been waiting for"); **Opposition** (Wilson) predicts fewer homes / higher
  rents. [ABC; grattan.edu.au — secondary characterisation]

## 4. Who it hits

- **Existing investors:** largely protected (grandfathered); possible **lock-in effect**
  (hold to keep grandfathering) reducing future listings. [cameos; commentary]
- **Future investors in established property:** worse off — deduction **deferred, not
  lost** (carry-forward). [cameo p.6]
- **Investors in new builds:** advantaged — policy channels capital here. [p.4]
- **Owner-occupiers / first-home buyers:** intended beneficiaries (~75,000 over a decade).
- **Renters:** small negative per Treasury (<$2/wk), contested by opposition/investor bodies.
- **Sellers:** mixed — because indexation taxes **real** gains, **low-real-return holders
  can pay less** than under the 50% discount, **high-real-return holders pay more**. Not a
  uniform increase. [p.8 cameos]

## 5. Market reaction to date (weak — measured vs sentiment blurred)

- PropTrack: national prices **-0.3%** July 2026 (4th straight fall); Cotality **-0.7%**
  (sharpest since Dec 2022). Auctions low-volume; Melbourne ~59.6% clearance first week Aug.
- **⚠ Attribution caveat:** these falls are **not cleanly attributable to the reform** —
  it doesn't bite until 2027, holdings are grandfathered, and rates/macro are a major
  confound. Early effects are described as **sentiment/anecdotal** (softer investor demand
  for established stock, some repositioning to new builds), not a measured reform effect.

## 6. Queensland / Gold Coast angle (thinnest — commentary, no data)

- Local GC/QLD firms (Avenue Property, MAP Lawyers, Kilmartin Legal, May 2026) argue the
  change may **reduce interstate (Sydney/Melbourne) investor competition** in SE QLD —
  giving local owner-occupiers/first-home buyers more room — and that new-build incentives
  could favour Gold Coast/Brisbane **growth corridors**. All **opinion-tier** (agency/law
  blogs).
- **⚠ Honest gap:** **no reputable QLD/Gold Coast-specific quantitative modelling** of the
  reform exists as of 2026-08-24. The regional angle is qualitative commentary, not data.
  *(This intersects our own arbitrage finding — reduced interstate investor competition is
  a plausible mechanism behind southern→GC equity migration, but is not yet quantified.)*
