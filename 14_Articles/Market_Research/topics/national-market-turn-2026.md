# Why the Australian housing market turned in 2026

**Topic dossier · evergreen · as at 2026-08-24**
**Scope:** the national dwelling-value turn from growth to decline through H1–mid 2026,
its causes, and the seller/buyer behaviour behind it.

> **Bottom line.** The 2026 turn is best explained by a **demand shock from higher
> interest rates and a war-driven inflation spike**, on top of an **affordability /
> borrowing-capacity ceiling**. The **May 2026 federal negative-gearing + CGT change
> is a real, announced policy** — but the weight of reputable evidence treats it as a
> **confidence-and-investor accelerant, not the root cause**: prices had already
> turned by June 2026, and the tax changes do not take effect until 1 July 2027.

**FACT vs COMMENTARY convention:** claims below are tagged. Primary/near-primary data
(rate path, inflation, the budget announcement, sentiment indices, listings/clearance/
discount data) is FACT. The *weighting* of causes and forward-looking claims are
COMMENTARY. Anything unverified is listed at the end, not smoothed over.

## 1. The CGT / negative-gearing hypothesis — VERDICT: real policy, but amplifier not trigger

**FACT — the change is real.** In the **2026–27 Federal Budget (budget night, 12 May
2026)**, Treasurer **Jim Chalmers** announced:
- **Negative gearing** abolished for **established** residential property **bought
  after 7:30pm 12 May 2026**, effective **1 July 2027**; losses may only offset rental
  income / future rental capital gains. Holdings at budget night are **grandfathered**;
  **new builds exempt**.
- **CGT**: the **50% discount replaced** by **cost-base indexation + a 30% minimum tax
  rate**, on **gains accruing after 1 July 2027**; pre-existing assets keep the old
  discount (with apportionment options); new builds retain the 50% discount.
- Sources: ABC News, "Government breaks promise… negative gearing/CGT," 12 May 2026
  (https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-05-12/budget-2026-government-breaks-promise-negative-gearing-cgt/106669860);
  ATO "Tax reform – Boosting home ownership"
  (https://www.ato.gov.au/about-ato/new-legislation/in-detail/individuals/tax-reform-boosting-home-ownership-reforming-negative-gearing-and-capital-gains-tax);
  Baker McKenzie "Budget Bites — CGT Discount and Negative Gearing," May 2026
  (https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/05/australia-budget-bites-cgt-discount-and-negative-gearing);
  budget.gov.au factsheet (https://budget.gov.au/content/factsheets/download/tax-explainers-negative-gearing-capital-gains-tax.pdf).

**COMMENTARY — market impact.** Treasury (via ABC's reporting) reportedly projected the
changes would slow price growth ~2%/yr, shift ~75,000 dwellings investor→owner-occupier,
reduce new supply ~35,000, and lift median rents ~$100/yr. Agents/analysts attribute a
**post-budget collapse in investor demand and auction activity** to it. Sources: ABC,
"Property market transforming," 21 July 2026
(https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-07-21/how-the-australian-property-market-is-transforming/106904826);
propertyupdate.com.au market update, Aug 2026.

**Why it is an amplifier, not the trigger:** the market's **largest monthly fall since
Dec 2022 was reported 1 July 2026** — *before* the budget could fully propagate — and
the **tax does not take effect until 1 July 2027**. So the budget worked chiefly through
**sentiment and investor withdrawal** on a market **already falling** from rates and
inflation.

## 2. What actually changed (fundamentals) — the strongest-supported drivers

- **RBA rate hikes (FACT, high confidence).** Three hikes in H1 2026 (~75bp total), then
  **held in July and August 2026**. Endpoint is source-inconsistent (**~4.1% vs 4.35%**;
  see limitations). Sources: RBA media conference 11 Aug 2026
  (https://www.rba.gov.au/speeches/2026/mc-gov-2026-08-11.html); Domain "RBA August 2026…
  held at 4.35%" (https://www.domain.com.au/news/rba-august-2026-1541698/).
- **War-driven inflation spike (FACT).** The **Iran conflict / Strait of Hormuz**
  disruption drove an oil-price spike; headline monthly inflation surged to **~4.6%**
  (RBA target 2.5%) — the proximate reason the RBA hiked into a slowing economy. Sources:
  RBA "Inflation and the Impact of the Middle East Conflict," 19 May 2026
  (https://www.rba.gov.au/speeches/2026/sp-ag-2026-05-19.html); ABC 6 May 2026
  (https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-05-06/rba-warns-of-harsh-iran-war-reality-interest-rates/106645124).
- **Affordability / borrowing-capacity ceiling (FACT + strong commentary).** Cotality's
  price-tier split: over the three months to July, **upper-quartile values −3.2%** while
  **lower-quartile +0.3%** — the top end, most sensitive to borrowing capacity, fell
  hardest. Source: Cotality Monthly Housing Chart Pack July 2026
  (https://www.cotality.com/au/insights/articles/monthly-housing-chart-pack-july-2026).
- **Migration / supply (MIXED — context, not trigger).** Net overseas migration is
  moderating (ABS ~301k in 2025; Treasury ~225k forecast 2026-27) but still high;
  dwelling completions remain below Housing Accord targets. Undersupply **cushions** the
  fall rather than causing it. Source: AMP Econosights housing supply
  (https://www.amp.com.au/resources/insights-hub/econosights-an-update-on-housing-supply).

## 3. Seller psychology — behaviour shifted decisively (FACT via data)

- **Listings surged** +12.4% in July 2026 to **278,984** (propertyupdate market charts,
  Aug 2026).
- **Vendor discounting widened** to a median **~3.6–3.8%** across combined capitals (from
  ~3.0% in the March quarter); **median days-to-sell ~35**.
- **Auction flight:** combined-capital **clearance fell from ~66% (Feb) to low-40s% by
  June–July 2026**; **~20% of auctions withdrawn** in the week to 21 June (vs 11.5% a year
  earlier); **~40% of vendors signing before auction day**; auctions' share of new
  listings fell ~45% (Nov 2025) → ~30% (June 2026). Sources: ABC "Worst auction results in
  years," 28 June 2026 (https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-06-28/worst-property-auction-rates-in-years-for-sydney-melbourne/106852602);
  SBS "They'll walk away" (https://www.sbs.com.au/news/article/auction-clearance-rates-home-selling-early-offers/57c3rvj7x).
- **COMMENTARY:** agents describe vendors capitulating on price expectations (~25.5% of
  vendors cutting guides; "add $100k… but in reverse") — ABC 21 July 2026.

## 4. Buyer psychology — confidence collapse, then a partial thaw (FACT via indices)

- **House-price expectations crashed:** Westpac–Melbourne Institute House Price
  Expectations **−14.9% to 128.2 in June 2026** (first sub-average in 3 years), then to a
  fresh 3-year low **~110.8 by August** (>100 still = net optimism). Sources: Westpac IQ
  consumer sentiment Jun–Aug 2026 (https://www.westpaciq.com.au/economics/2026/06/consumer-sentiment-june-2026;
  …/2026/08/consumer-sentiment-august-2026).
- **"Time to buy a dwelling"** hit an extreme low (~72 in May 2026) then **recovered to
  ~85.4 in July** as rate fears eased — panic → cautious wait-and-see. Source: Westpac IQ
  July 2026 (https://www.westpaciq.com.au/economics/2026/07/consumer-sentiment-july-2026).
- **At the coalface:** open-home groups ~15 → ~5; realestate.com enquiry reportedly
  **−50% post-budget**; days-on-market ~27 (Nov 2025) → ~42 (June 2026).
- **COMMENTARY:** HSBC chief economist **Paul Bloxham** — the downturn is "just the
  beginning," first-home buyers won't "catch a falling knife" (ABC 21 July 2026).

## 5. Underlying factors — ranked by weight of evidence

1. **Interest-rate hikes into 2026 (strongest, FACT).** ~4.1–4.35%; compresses borrowing
   capacity — evidenced by the upper quartile leading the fall.
2. **War/oil inflation shock (strong, FACT).** Iran/Strait-of-Hormuz oil spike — the
   causal spark behind the hikes.
3. **Affordability / borrowing-capacity ceiling (strong).** Prices had outrun incomes;
   the top end broke first.
4. **Confidence collapse (strong, FACT via indices).** Westpac sentiment/expectations at
   multi-year lows; buyer and seller expectations de-aligned.
5. **May 2026 NG/CGT budget change (moderate — real but amplifier).** Cooled investor
   demand and confidence immediately, though the tax bites only from 1 July 2027.
6. **Supply/migration (weak as a *turn* driver; context).** Undersupply cushions the fall.

**Most consistent narrative across reputable sources:** rate hikes + war-inflation +
stretched affordability turned the market; the budget tax change and collapsing
confidence accelerated it.

  — treat as commentary.
- Some relays (propertyupdate, propertyinvestmentprofessionals) are industry blogs
  summarising Cotality/Westpac — reliable relays, paired with primary sources where possible.
