## The five factor clusters
1. **Credit / debt / lending standards** (`cluster1`) — household debt-to-income,
   mortgage-credit growth, interest-only & investor share, high-LVR/high-DTI lending,
   serviceability, arrears as a leading stress signal, APRA macroprudential limits.
2. **Monetary / interest-rate / macro** (`cluster2`) — cash-rate shocks, the
   sensitivity of dwelling prices to rates, unemployment/income, migration,
   transmission of the 2022-23 tightening.
3. **Overvaluation & bubble-detection** (`cluster3`) — price-to-income / price-to-rent,
   deviation from fundamentals, GSADF/PSY explosive-root date-stamping applied to
   Australian capitals, IMF/OECD overvaluation gauges.
4. **Market microstructure leading indicators** (`cluster4`) — auction clearance rates,
   turnover/sales volume, days-on-market, new listings, search activity, construction
   approvals, rental vacancy — the activity signals that turn before prices.
5. **Early-warning systems & Australian episode post-mortems** (`cluster5`) — BIS
   credit-to-GDP & property-price gaps, IMF House-Prices-at-Risk, Reinhart-Rogoff,
   the Great Mortgaging, ML crisis prediction, and the AU 1989-91 / GFC / 2017-19 /
   2022-23 episodes.

## Headline empirical throughline (what the literature converges on)
- **Credit is the discriminating factor.** House-price booms accompanied by rapid
  mortgage-credit growth are ~twice as likely to end in a bust (Cerutti et al.;
  Jordà-Schularick-Taylor; IMF WP/25/50). The credit-to-GDP gap and the household
  debt-service ratio are the best-performing early-warning indicators (Drehmann-Juselius).
- **Leverage governs the depth.** Highly indebted households cut spending harder on a
  shock, so prior debt build-up amplifies the downturn (Mian-Sufi-Verner; Price et al.;
  IMF WP/19/76).
- **Rates are the trigger.** ~1pp sustained real mortgage-rate rise ≈ ~8% lower prices
  over years (Saunders-Tulip); a 100bp cash-rate rise ≈ ~2.3% lower prices in the median
  region, more in expensive areas (He-La Cava).
- **Overvaluation + credit + tighter conditions jointly forecast downside risk** up to
  3 years ahead (Deghi et al., House-Prices-at-Risk).
- **Activity leads price.** Auction clearance rates, turnover and time-on-market turn
  before prices (RBA SMP Feb-2014 Box B; Leal et al.; Genesove-Mayer; Stein; search-index
  work by Møller et al.).
