# EVIDENCE AUDIT: "Is there a proven way to get a higher price for your home?"

**Bottom line up front:** across every evidence file in this repo, the internal Fields-specific claims have all been audited *downward*, and the strongest academic evidence actually runs **against** a simple "proven way to get more" story. The most defensible claims are (a) *don't overprice* (well-evidenced, large effect), (b) *precise not round list prices* (peer-reviewed, experimental), (c) *agent process creates a wide outcome range* (not "better agents = more money"). The 9.6 pp agent premium **does not survive** robustness checks.

### This is the closest thing to a direct answer to "a proven way to get a higher price"

**Finding 1: List at 1–5% above true market value (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**
- **Nikiforou, Dimopoulos & Sivitanides (2022)** — *Journal of European Real Estate Research*. **538 transactions, Cyprus.** In transparent markets (sold prices published, like Australia) the optimal Degree of Overpricing is **~1.5%**. A 1% increase in DOP increases probability of selling within 30 days by **1.23%**.
- **Bucchianeri & Minson (2013)** — *Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization*. **14,000+ transactions, US.** Higher listing prices do anchor higher sale prices, "**but the effect is tiny: overpricing by 10-20% yields only 0.05-0.07% additional sale price. The anchoring benefit does NOT compensate for the TOM penalty.**"

**Finding 2: Overpricing beyond 10% is destructive (HIGH CONFIDENCE)** — CoreLogic Australia: 10–15% above eventual sale price → 2–3x longer on market. Zillow Research (2017): sale 10% below list → **5x** as long on market; after 2 months homes sell for **5% less** than listing price. Taylor (1999): TOM stigma effect. Knight (2002): **38.4% of listed properties undergo price changes**; large changes → longer AND lower.

**Finding 3: Underpricing does NOT generate bidding wars in private treaty (HIGH CONFIDENCE)** — Bucchianeri & Minson: "there are seldom enough buyers to create a 'herding effect'". Implication for the 98% private-treaty Gold Coast: deliberate underpricing to create competition is **not supported**.

**Finding 4: Agents systematically underprice (MEDIUM CONFIDENCE)** — **Levitt & Syverson (2008)**, *Review of Economics & Statistics*, **98,000 Chicago home sales**: own homes **+3.7%**, **+9.5 days**. "The price an agent recommends is optimal for the agent, not the seller."

**Finding 5: Market conditions change the equation (HIGH CONFIDENCE)** — **Haurin, Haurin, Nadauld & Sanders (2013)**, *Journal of Housing Economics*, **Belfast market**. **Anglin, Rutherford & Springer (2003)**, *JREFE*: overpricing punished harder in homogeneous submarkets (Robina), tolerated in heterogeneous ones (Burleigh Waters).

**Finding 6: "Just below" pricing — complicated (MEDIUM CONFIDENCE)** — **Beracha & Seiler (2014)**, *JREFE*: just-below ($999,000) yields the **greatest discount negotiated by buyers**, but because it embeds higher initial overpricing the net can be **+2.5–3% higher sale prices** vs round pricing. "**This conflicts with Cardella & Seiler's finding**" — the playbook resolves it as format vs position.

**The single strongest "proven way" claim in the whole corpus** — **Cardella & Seiler (2016)**, *Journal of Economic Psychology*, described as "**The #1 Academic Finding**" and "the single most actionable academic finding in this entire playbook": precise listing prices ($1,385,000 not $1,400,000) generate the highest final sale price and smallest discount off list; four experimental conditions (Rounded $200,000 / Just Below $199,000 / High Precise $201,326 / Low Precise $198,674). "This effect persists even among real estate professionals — expertise does not eliminate anchoring bias."

Price drivers: location (67% $/sqm variance between cheapest/dearest suburb; streets within BW range −40% to +38% vs suburb median; water views **+45% BW, +16% VL, +6% Robina**); size (**r=0.68–0.79**, every extra sqm = **$6,900–$9,500**); bedrooms (**+$255,000–$607,000** each).

### `fsbo_vs_agent_research.md` — the sharpest counter-evidence to "agents get you more"
Opening: "The industry stat (NAR: FSBO median ~$360k vs agent ~$425k) is **confounded** — FSBO skews toward mobile homes, rural, low-end. **Peer-reviewed studies that control for this consistently find agents do NOT deliver a price premium net of commission.** They DO add value on liquidity."

Full citations with URLs:
- **Hendel, Nevo & Ortalo-Magné (2009)**, "The Relative Performance of Real Estate Marketing Platforms: MLS versus FSBOMadison.com", *American Economic Review* 99(5):1878–98. FSBO pre-commission prices **no lower** than MLS; FSBO took longer, lower probability of sale. NBER WP 13360.
- **Bernheim & Meer (2013)**, "Do Real Estate Brokers Add Value When Listing Services Are Unbundled?", *Economic Inquiry* 51(2):1166–82. Stanford faculty/staff housing, same MLS access. **Broker use reduced price 5.9–7.7%**, sold faster. NBER WP 13796.
- **Levitt & Syverson (2008)**, *REStat* 90(4):599–611. **+3.7%**, **+9.5 days**. "agents push clients to sell cheap and fast (small share of marginal $)". NBER WP 11053.
- **Rutherford, Springer & Yavas (2005)**, "Conflicts Between Principals and Agents: Evidence From Residential Brokerage", *Journal of Financial Economics*. Condos; agent-owned **+4.5% premium**, same time-to-sell.
- **Barwick & Pathak (2015)**, "The Costs of Free Entry", NBER WP 17227. **650k MLS listings, eastern Massachusetts 1998–2011.** Flat-fee homes sold **1–4% less**, but **10 pp less likely to sell at all**, on market a month+ longer.
- **Netherlands "internet agents" study (~2023)** — higher prices and faster sales, but more likely to fail to sell entirely.
- **Hsieh & Moretti (2003)**, "Can Free Entry Be Inefficient?", *Journal of Political Economy* 111(5):1076–1122.

Synthesis table: Price net of commission — agents deliver **zero to negative** premium; Time to sell — agents **faster**; Probability of sale — agents **higher**; Agents' own homes — **3.7–4.5% more**. "**The honest read: agent value is in liquidity (certainty + speed of sale), not price.**"

**Transferability caveats:** "**Not in Fields KB or Google Drive as of 2026-05-14 — sourced via web search**" (i.e. not verified against PDFs). "All studies are US except Netherlands; **Australian commissions (2–3%) lower than US-era (5–6%), so the 'FSBO saves the commission' arithmetic is weaker here. Direct transfer to AU market should be cautious.**" "Studies are 15+ years old (except Barwick/Pathak 2015 and Netherlands ~2023). **Pre-Zillow/Domain universal-exposure era.**" "All studies have **self-selection caveat**: FSBO sellers in samples were more patient/motivated/financially literate than average."
