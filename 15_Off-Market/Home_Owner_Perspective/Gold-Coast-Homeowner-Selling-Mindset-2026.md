## Executive summary — the defining tension

The mid-2026 Gold Coast seller is caught between a **strong local reality** and a **hostile macro headline**, and that split *is* their psychology.

**The local reality is still seller-favourable.** Stock is tight (active listings roughly **20% below the five-year average** [VERIFIED]), good homes still sell in **under 30 days** in many suburbs [VERIFIED], the rental market is in crisis (citywide vacancy **around 0.9–1.1%**, Australia's **highest house rents at ~$900/week** [VERIFIED]), and premium southern suburbs posted double-digit annual growth into late 2025 (Burleigh Heads houses **+13.8%**, units **+17.1%** to a **$1,790,000 / $1,200,000** median [VERIFIED]).

**The macro headline turned against them.** The RBA has reversed *all* of 2025's cuts and sits at a restrictive **4.35%**, held in June 2026, with **55% of economists tipping a further hike** (most likely **August 2026**) and **no major bank forecasting relief before 2027** [VERIFIED]. Forecasters openly disagree on what happens next: **KPMG is bullish** (national houses +7.7%, Brisbane +10.9% in 2026) while **ANZ has downgraded to +2.8%** and warns SE Queensland momentum fades into late 2026/early 2027 as rates bite [VERIFIED].

**Our own numbers show the inflection is already here.** In the target suburbs, medians *peaked around Q4 2025–Q1 2026 and have since softened*, while **transaction volume has collapsed** [FIELDS]:

| Suburb | Peak median (quarter) | Q2 2026 median | Move off peak | Q2 2026 sales (n) vs a year prior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Burleigh Waters** | $1,932,255 (Q4 2025) | $1,623,820 | **−16%** | 43 vs 109 |
| **Robina** | $1,499,749 (Q1 2026) | $1,456,067 | **−2.9%** | 43 vs 97–162 |
| **Varsity Lakes** | ~$1,338,000 (Q3–Q4 2025) | $1,417,960 | volatile / thin | **20** (very thin) |

*(Fields `market_pulse`, house medians. Quarterly medians on small samples are noisy — the **volume collapse** is the more reliable signal than any single quarter's median.)*

The behavioural consequence: **the seller who watched a neighbour sell for a record in late 2025 now senses the peak may have passed** — fewer sales, longer talk of rate hikes, softening quarterly prints — but *still* sits on years of equity and a genuinely undersupplied market. That is not a confident seller and it is not a panicked one. It is an **anxious-but-hopeful "have I missed my window?" seller**, and they are looking for someone to give them the real numbers instead of a sales pitch.

**The one-line strategic read:** the resonant offer is not hype and it is not doom — it is **certainty and timing intelligence**. Transparent local valuation, an honest "sell-now-vs-wait" framing built on the actual undersupply/days-on-market data, and reassurance about the two things that genuinely scare them (the cost of the *next* purchase, and picking the wrong agent). That is precisely the Fields position — "smarter with data."

## 1. The market backdrop these minds are reacting to

Sellers don't experience "the market" as a spreadsheet — they experience it as a **feeling assembled from headlines, a neighbour's sale, and their banking app**. Here's the factual backdrop, and how each fact lands emotionally.

### Macro (the anxiety layer)

- **Interest rates are elevated and *not* falling.** Cash rate **4.35%**, held June 2026 after three 2026 hikes (Feb 3.85% → Mar 4.10% → May 4.35%), fully reversing 2025's 75bp of cuts. **55% of economists expect another hike**, 62% of those naming August; CommBank, NAB and Westpac all push cuts to **2027**. Next decision **11 August 2026**. [VERIFIED]
  - *Emotional read:* This is the **single strongest driver of "I'll be locked out of my next home."** A homeowner can be thrilled about their sale price and still frozen, because the mortgage on the *upgrade* — or even the rent in between — has never looked more expensive.
- **Forecasts openly conflict.** KPMG +7.7% national / +10.9% Brisbane houses vs ANZ +2.8% capital cities, slowing further in 2027. [VERIFIED]
  - *Emotional read:* Conflicting experts *increase* paralysis, not confidence. The seller hears "even the banks can't agree" and defaults to **wait-and-see** — which is why a neutral, data-first voice that presents a *range* (not a single hero number) cuts through.

### Local (the reassurance layer)

- **Supply is genuinely tight.** Active listings ~**20% below the 5-year average** (SQM shows an even deeper deficit). [VERIFIED]
- **Good homes still sell fast.** Median days on market **under 30 in many suburbs** (~26–28 days market-wide) — *but trending up year-on-year* (some sources: 17→26 days). [VERIFIED]
- **Rental crisis is acute.** Citywide vacancy **~0.9–1.1%**; **Australia's highest median house rent at ~$900/week** (+5.9% YoY, ~$120 above Sydney, ~$240 above Brisbane). [VERIFIED]
  - *Emotional read (this one cuts both ways):* It signals fierce underlying demand (good for selling) **but also makes "sell, then rent while I look" feel terrifying** — there is nowhere affordable to land. This is a specific Gold Coast friction that barely exists in looser markets, and it is a major reason a Gold Coast owner delays listing.
- **Premium southern suburbs ran hot into late 2025.** Burleigh Heads houses **+13.8%** / units **+17.1%** YoY to **$1.79M / $1.20M** (Q4 2025), with a structurally **undersupplied** 2026 pipeline (≈$113.2M, only 123 units + 21 townhouses, **no new houses**). Units are outperforming houses as priced-out buyers shift down the ladder. [VERIFIED]

### Where Fields' own data sharpens (or contradicts) the picture

The bullish agency sources (PRD, blog forecasters) describe a market still climbing. **Our transaction data says the target suburbs already rolled over the top and thinned out** in Q1–Q2 2026 [FIELDS]. Both can be true — agency reports lag and lean bullish; our volume data is more current. **For a seller, the felt truth is our version**: their neighbour's record was late 2025, and it's quieter now. Messaging that pretends the market is still surging will ring false to anyone actually watching their street. Messaging that says *"the record-setting run has cooled, here's exactly where your suburb sits now, and here's what tight supply still means for you"* will ring true.

## 2. What they are worried about

Ranked roughly by emotional weight for *this* market. Selling a home is not a financial transaction to these people — it's an emotional one wearing a financial costume: **36% of sellers report crying during the process and 95% experience stress at some stage** [VERIFIED]. Every worry below is amplified by that baseline stress.

### 2.1 "Have I already missed the peak?" (timing regret) — *the dominant Gold Coast fear right now*
The late-2025 records are recent and vivid; the Q1–Q2 2026 softening and volume collapse are real [FIELDS]. Sellers fear both directions at once: **sell now and watch it keep rising** (KPMG's world), or **wait and watch it fade** (ANZ's world). Conflicting forecasts make this worse, not better.
- **What resonates:** honest, suburb-specific "where are we in the cycle" data with a *range*, not a prediction. Show them the actual peak, the actual current level, and the actual supply position — let them draw the conclusion.

### 2.2 "I'll sell fine — but I can't afford to buy back in" (onward-purchase / locked-out fear)
The most rational fear in a 4.35% market. Their sale succeeds; their *next* mortgage is punishing, or there's nothing to buy because stock is 20% below average, or the rent-in-between is $900/week [VERIFIED]. For **upsizers and same-market movers this is often the true blocker** — not the sale price at all.
- **What resonates:** framing the sale as *one half of a two-sided move*, and giving them onward-purchase intelligence (what their equity actually buys now, what tight supply means for finding the next home). Reassurance here unlocks more listings than any price pitch.

### 2.3 "The market might turn before I settle" (downturn fear)
Amplified by rate-hike headlines and the ANZ downgrade. In softer phases, **CoreLogic vendor discounting typically runs 3–6%** [VERIFIED] — a real number a nervous seller feels as "I'll have to drop my price."
- **What resonates:** the genuinely bullish local counter-facts (20%-below supply, sub-30 DOM, rental crisis demand) presented soberly, so the seller can weigh downturn risk against structural undersupply themselves.

### 2.4 "I'll pick the wrong agent" — *the most exploitable fear for a data-led challenger*
This is enormous and well-evidenced:
- **Choosing the wrong agent is framed as a $30,000–$50,000 swing** in the seller's pocket [VERIFIED].
- **27% of Australians (>1 in 4) have experienced dodgy agent behaviour** — most commonly **inflating price expectations to win the listing (10%)**, then **underquoting (9%)**, then **fake bids / phantom offers / photo manipulation (8% each)** [VERIFIED].
- **Real estate agents rank among Australia's least-trusted professions** — Roy Morgan puts them **third-least-trustworthy** (only ~9% rate them highly, stuck between 7–12% since 1987; nurses top at 92%) [VERIFIED].
- Agent guide prices are described by Finder's head of consumer research as **"a fantasy designed to push buyers into the system"** [VERIFIED].
- **What resonates:** *This is Fields' single biggest wedge.* The seller's distrust of agents is not a barrier to us — it is our opening. Independent, methodology-backed valuation ("here's exactly how we arrived at this range") is the antidote to the two things they've been burned by: **overquoting to win the listing** and **underquoting to move stock**. Sellers now explicitly want **verified offer logs, written pricing methodology tied to data, and aligned incentives** [VERIFIED] — a near-perfect description of a data-intelligence product.

### 2.5 "It won't sell at all" (the universal #1 fear)
**The number-one seller fear nationally is that the property simply won't sell** [VERIFIED]. On the Gold Coast the fast-DOM data is genuine reassurance — but only for *well-priced* homes, and DOM is creeping up.
- **What resonates:** realistic days-on-market and absorption data for *their* suburb and price band, plus honest guidance that competitively-priced listings gain early momentum while overpriced ones stall (**agents confirm early momentum is priced-in from day one** [VERIFIED]).

### 2.6 Costs: commission, CGT, and the frictional bill
- **Commission / selling costs:** every dollar of fee is felt as a dollar off equity; distrust of agents makes this sting more.
- **Capital Gains Tax:** a real anxiety for **investors and second-home owners**, and a point of *confusion* for owner-occupiers. **Note [INFERRED, needs care]:** in Australia a genuine main residence is generally **CGT-exempt**, so most owner-occupiers *don't* owe CGT on the family home — but many don't know that and worry anyway; investors face CGT with the 50% discount for 12-month-plus holds. *We could not verify specific CGT-rule claims in this research (the tax source was unreliable), so any public CGT content must be checked against current ATO guidance before publishing — do not state CGT rules from this document.*
- **What resonates:** a transparent, itemised "true cost of selling" and a plain-English "does CGT even apply to me?" explainer (owner-occupier vs investor). Removing fee/tax fog is pure trust-building and squarely on-brand.

### 2.7 Auction vs private treaty anxiety
Queensland is traditionally a **private-treaty and increasingly off-market** state; auction is less default here than in Sydney/Melbourne, and **Queensland auction clearance sits in a soft ~48–66% range** [INFERRED — from a fetched source, not independently verified]. The fear is public failure (a passed-in auction signalling "unwanted") vs the opacity of private-treaty negotiation.
- **What resonates:** method-neutral, data-led guidance on which sale method suits their property type and price band — again, advice framed as *information*, not a push toward the method that pays the agent most.

## 3. What they are hopeful for (the motivations that get them to list)

Fear keeps them on the fence; **hope is what makes them pick up the phone**. Lead with the relevant hope, then de-risk the matching fear.

- **Cashing out real, bankable equity.** Years of growth are locked in; **national dwelling values rose ~8.6% in 2025 (~$71,400 to the median home)** [VERIFIED from fetched source] and Gold Coast premium suburbs did far better. This equity is *real now* regardless of what 2027 does — a powerful, true, reassuring frame.
- **Lifestyle change / sea-change realisation.** Many Gold Coast owners bought the lifestyle; selling is realising it. Burleigh/Palm Beach/Mermaid owners in particular hold trophy assets they're curious to test at market.
- **Downsizing & retirement funding.** The largest and warmest segment: older owners in family homes (classic Burleigh Waters / Robina / Mermaid Waters profile) whose home *is* their retirement plan. Unit outperformance (+17.1% Burleigh Heads [VERIFIED]) makes the "big house → premium apartment" move financially *and* emotionally attractive — they're downsizing *up* in quality.
- **Upsizing within a rising market** — motivated but most exposed to onward-purchase / rate fear (see 2.2).
- **Relocating** (interstate, tree-change, closer to family) — a mover whose sale timing is driven by *life*, not the market; needs speed and certainty over squeezing the last dollar.

**Motivational truth to lead with:** *"Your equity gain is real and it's yours today. The question isn't whether you've won — you have — it's how to convert it cleanly and land your next move without overpaying."* That reframes the whole decision from "gamble on the market" to "manage a win," which is far less paralysing.

## 4. What they actually need (the job-to-be-done)

Strip away the noise and the pre-listing Gold Coast seller needs six things — and notice how few agents actually provide them:

1. **An accurate, independent valuation they can trust** — a *range* with a visible method, not an agent's listing-bait number. (Directly answers fears 2.1, 2.4.)
2. **"Sell now vs wait" intelligence for *their* suburb** — where in the cycle their specific market sits, with supply and DOM context. (Answers 2.1, 2.3.)
3. **Onward-purchase reality** — what their equity buys now and how tight the buy-side is. (Answers 2.2 — the most under-served need.)
4. **Total-cost transparency** — commission, marketing, CGT applicability, settlement, all itemised up front. (Answers 2.6.)
5. **Certainty and control** — a clear process and realistic timeline that lowers the 95%-stress baseline. (Answers 2.5.)
6. **Someone who isn't a pushy agent** — a neutral, data-first guide who informs rather than closes. (Answers 2.4 — this is the meta-need beneath all the others.)

**Sellers now explicitly articulate need #1, #4 and #6 as "verified offer logs, written pricing methodology tied to data, and aligned incentives"** [VERIFIED]. That is not a wish list Fields has to guess at — it's a product spec the market has already written for us.

## 5. What they're reading and listening to (the news diet)

This shapes the *language* they'll recognise and the anxieties already primed in their heads.

- **RBA / interest-rate coverage — the dominant, recurring story.** Every ~6 weeks the rate decision is national news; the "held at 4.35%, hike possible in August, no cuts until 2027" narrative is inescapable [VERIFIED]. *Every mortgage-holding seller feels each decision personally.*
- **CoreLogic / Cotality, PropTrack (REA), Domain house-price releases** — the monthly "values up/down X%" headlines. Note **Brisbane's +17.3% to Feb 2026** [INFERRED — fetched, not fully verified] and Gold Coast unit medians reportedly overtaking Sydney's — the kind of "we've made it / did I miss it" headline that hits Gold Coast owners hard.
- **The rental crisis** as constant local news — reinforces both "demand is huge" and "don't you dare sell and try to rent."
- **Cost-of-living pressure** — the ambient stressor behind the "should I cash out?" impulse.
- **Migration to SE Queensland** — a persistent positive local narrative (population ~700,000, strong interstate/overseas inflows) [INFERRED — see caveat below], though **whether migration is still accelerating or has plateaued is genuinely unresolved** (see [What we deliberately did NOT conclude](#what-we-deliberately-did-not-conclude)).
- **Local agent/agency marketing** — a torrent of "now's the time to sell!" that they've learned to distrust (fear 2.4). Fields' neutrality is defined *against* this noise.

**Language implication:** they already speak in "rate hikes," "vacancy rates," "median growth," "days on market." Use their vocabulary, then *out-honest* the agency chorus.

## 8. Messaging implications for Fields (what to say, what to avoid)

**The winning posture: neutral timing-intelligence, not hype and not doom.** Concretely:

**Lead with (resonant):**
- *"Where does your suburb actually sit in the cycle — with the real numbers, not an agent's guess?"* (answers the #1 fear + the agent-distrust wedge simultaneously)
- *"Your equity gain is real and bankable today."* (converts paralysis into a managed win)
- *"Here's how we arrive at a valuation — the method, in writing."* (the antidote to over/under-quoting)
- *"Selling is only half the move — here's what your equity buys next in a tight market."* (owns the under-served onward-purchase fear)
- *"The true, itemised cost of selling — including whether CGT even applies to you."* (trust via transparency)

**Avoid (breaks trust / breaks our own content rules):**
- ❌ "Now is the time to sell!" — the exact agency line they distrust.
- ❌ Any single hero valuation figure in a headline — use **ranges** (our standing editorial rule, and the forecasts literally disagree, so a single number is dishonest).
- ❌ Predictions ("prices will fall/rise") — use conditional, data-anchored framing; report indicators, let them conclude.
- ❌ Pretending the market is still surging — our own volume data says otherwise, and sellers watching their street will catch the lie.
- ❌ Forbidden words ("robust market", "rare opportunity", etc.) and advice framing in public content.

**The strategic sentence, for internal alignment:** *We are the one voice in this market that will tell a Gold Coast seller the truth about timing, value and cost — because we make money from being right, not from winning the listing.* Everything above ladders up to that.

## What we deliberately did NOT conclude

Intellectual honesty guardrails — claims that *sound* right but **failed verification** and must not be asserted (internally or publicly):

- ❌ **"Gold Coast homeowners are highly optimistic / 89% of QLD expects price rises."** The Cotality confidence figures (89% QLD bullish; 87% national expecting rises) were **refuted**. Do **not** assert high seller optimism as fact — the mid-2026 reality is *ambivalence*, not confidence.
- ❌ **A single Gold Coast-wide median house price.** Multiple candidate figures ($1.17–1.2M, $1.32M, $1.62M, $1,616,635) were **refuted or conflicting**. Quote **suburb-level** (Burleigh Heads verified; our own `market_pulse` for targets) — never one city-wide hero number.
- ❌ **"Days on market is 32.2 days" / any precise single DOM.** Refuted. Use "**under 30 in many suburbs, trending up**."
- ❌ **"Interstate migration has reversed / people are moving back south."** Refuted (0-3). But we also can't assert migration is *still accelerating*. Migration direction is **unresolved** — check ABS directly before using it either way.
- ❌ **Specific CGT rules.** The tax source was unreliable; verify against ATO before any public CGT content.
- ⚠️ **Bullish local figures lean on agency/marketing sources** (PRD, whichrealestateagent, blogs) with a promotional slant. Treat the *direction* (undersupply, fast sales, rental crisis) as solid; treat *precise* figures as soft.
