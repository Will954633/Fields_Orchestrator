# Artist brief — the retriever

For the `land_prestige` emblem on the off-market discovery deck (2,235 homes).
Two versions have been drawn and neither can be used the way the other emblems
are. This explains exactly why, and what to change.

Send this whole page.

---

## What the deck does to a drawing

Every emblem is rendered as **light strokes glowing on a black page**, arriving
stroke by stroke. The banksia, the reeds, the gum and the golf flag all do this
and they look right.

The way that is produced is by **inverting** the drawing — the artist's black
ink becomes light, the white paper becomes the black page.

## Why the dog breaks and the banksia does not

Inverting swaps every tone. Nobody knows what tone a banksia flower "should" be,
so swapping its tones is invisible — it just reads as a banksia drawn in light.

A golden retriever is not like that. It has an **expected tonal arrangement**
that everyone recognises:

| | Real dog | After inverting |
|---|---|---|
| coat | pale gold | dark |
| eyes | dark | **white** |
| nose | black | **white** |
| the ball | light | **black, disappears** |

Both drawings supplied so far produce exactly that: a dark dog with blank white
eyes, a white nose, and no visible ball. It is not a drawing problem — the same
thing happens to both, because both are honest drawings of a pale dog with dark
eyes.

## Why "just draw it so it inverts correctly" does not work

That would mean drawing a **photographic negative** of the dog: dark coat, white
eyes, white nose, dark ball. Inverting that returns a correct pale retriever.

But inverting a negative gives back the original — so that route produces
exactly the same final picture as simply using a normal drawing of the dog
without inverting it. The two are the same image by definition. There is no
third possibility, and no drawing technique unlocks one.

**So the retriever is not inverted, and cannot be.** What it needs instead is to
*look* like the inverted drawings — which is a matter of texture, not tone.

---

## What to change

The current drawing (`Large_block_dog_v2.png`) is close. The coat is drawn with
fine light hairs packed densely, so it reads as a **solid pale mass**. The
banksia reads as **separate strokes with black between them**, and that
separation is what makes the set look like one family.

**Draw the coat as separated strokes, with the dark ground showing through
between them.**

- Individual hairs, with visible gaps — the way an engraving renders fur, not
  the way an airbrush renders it.
- Let the black ground come through across the whole body, not only at the
  edges and in the shadows. Roughly: at any point on the coat, black should be
  about a third of what you see.
- Keep the hairs varying in weight and length. Even, uniform hatching reads as
  a grey fill once it is reduced to card size.
- **Keep the tonal arrangement correct** — pale coat, dark eyes, dark nose, a
  light ball. Do not draw a negative. The drawing should look like a golden
  retriever on the page.

Everything else about the current drawing is right and should not change:

- Light strokes on a dark ground (scratchboard) is the correct convention.
- Single subject, no ground plane, no cast shadow, no horizon.
- The whole animal in frame with clear margin all round, nothing touching an
  edge.
- The ball in the mouth — it is the element that detaches and travels to the
  next card, so it must be clearly readable.
- PNG, long edge 1400px or more, no JPEG anywhere in its history.

## How to check it before sending

Squint at it, or shrink it to about 300px wide. If the body turns into a smooth
pale shape, the strokes are too dense. If you can still see it is made of
separate marks, it is right.
