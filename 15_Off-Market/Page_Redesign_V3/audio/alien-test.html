Alien signal — the actual recipe

Everything I built before was wrong, and now I know why. I assumed the sound was a pulse — a beat, a carrier, static switching on and off. It isn't.

The signal in Contact is a variation of the TARDIS effect from Doctor Who. Brian Hodgson made that at the BBC Radiophonic Workshop by dragging a house key along the bass strings of a gutted piano and slowing the tape down. Randy Thom adapted it for the film. So it is a scraped metal resonance, stretched — sustained and groaning, not rhythmic at all. No wonder none of the beats were close.

These eight rebuild that from the recipe: a bank of ringing filters tuned to a piano's inharmonic partials, excited by noise wandering across them like a key being dragged, pitched down and thrown into a long room. Click any one — they take a second or two to swell up out of nothing, as it should.

Or: the real thing

Not synthesis and not a film — actual spacecraft recordings. Electromagnetic data from plasma-wave instruments, shifted into hearing range. NASA audio is public domain, so these are free to use commercially with no licence to check. They sound alien because they are, and no synthesis I write will beat that.
