# A five-bedroom home on Moorabbin Place, Robina sold for $1,620,000 — here are the eight sales it should be measured against

One house changed hands in Robina in early July. The interesting question is not the price on the contract, but what that price should be compared to — and the answer is not "three-bedroom homes that sold nearby."

## What the headlines have been saying

The national numbers have been pointing down. Cotality's five-city aggregate fell 0.9% in July 2026 and 2.0% over the July quarter, with Sydney down 3.7%, Melbourne down 3.0% and Brisbane down 0.1% (Cotality, July 2026). The Westpac–Melbourne Institute House Price Expectations Index fell 8% in July 2026 to a three-year low, with 47% of respondents expecting price rises (Westpac–Melbourne Institute, July 2026). Headline CPI rose 3.8% over the year to June 2026, with a trimmed mean of 3.6% (ABS, June quarter 2026).

Rates sit behind all of it. The RBA cash rate is 4.35%, raised in February, March and May 2026 and held since the 16 June 2026 meeting, with the next decision scheduled for Tuesday 11 August 2026 (RBA). On about 10 July 2026 Westpac was forecasting two further hikes to 4.85%; on 30 July, after a softer-than-expected June-quarter CPI, it withdrew that call (reported 30 July 2026). That is the picture an owner in this suburb has absorbed, and it is a fair account of the national aggregates.

## What happened at 26 Moorabbin Place

The home is a five-bedroom, two-bathroom house on 798 sqm, with 214 sqm of internal floor area and a pool. It sold on 2026-07-06 for $1,620,000, after a campaign of 42 days — six days longer than the suburb average of 36, an ordinary length.

It last traded in July 2023 for $1,440,000. The July 2026 result is $180,000 above that, or +12.5% over about three years. That is the whole record of this one address. On its own it says little about the street, which is why the comparison that follows exists.

## The homes it should be measured against

"Comparable" usually means a label match: same bedroom count, sold nearby, so it counts. But two houses with the same number of bedrooms can differ by 100 sqm of floor area, a renovation, a second bathroom or a pool — and each of those is worth money. Matching on labels hides that; pricing the differences shows it.

Take 31 Huntingdale Crescent, which sold on 2026-03-15 for $1,910,000. That is the top raw price in this set, but it is not the same house. It is more heavily renovated than 26 Moorabbin Place (renovation level 4 against 2, worth about $96,032), in better interior condition (8 against 7, about $95,500), it has a third bathroom the subject does not (about $89,036), and it is a two-storey home against the subject's single storey (about $50,000). Strip out what those advantages are worth, and its $1,910,000 sale implies about **$1,565,812** for this home — not $1,910,000. That is the move: *sold for a figure, adjusts to a figure for this specific house.* Every "Adjusted for comparison" number below is that second figure.

The raw sale prices run from $1,300,000 to $1,910,000 — a spread of $610,000. That is what "homes that sold nearby" gives you, and it is too wide to read. Adjusted for their real differences to this home, the same eight run from $1,398,872 to $1,673,126, a spread of $274,254 — less than half as wide. The $1,620,000 result sits inside that adjusted range.

## Did it sell well, or badly?

It sold above six of the eight adjusted comparables. The median of the eight adjusted figures is $1,525,038; the $1,620,000 result is $94,962 above it, or +6.2%. Against the weight-adjusted average of $1,537,974, it is +5.3%. By this set of adjusted sales, the result landed toward the upper end — not at the top, and not in the middle.

## What that says about the market here

The comparables are mostly older than the sale they are being measured against. Their mean age at 2026-07-06 was 230 days — about 7.6 months — with the oldest 348 days earlier and the newest 57 days earlier. So they largely describe an earlier market.

For market movement alone to explain a +6.2% gap over a mean age of 7.6 months, prices would have had to move roughly +10.1% annualised. The independently measured Robina rolling 12-month house median moved +5.8% year-on-year, from 265 recorded sales — same direction, smaller pace. The national aggregates above moved the other way over July. Here is the limit that holds all of it together: +6.2% is inside this method's own error of about 11.0% in this price range. The direction is consistent with the suburb's measured median; the magnitude cannot be established from a single sale, because the gap is smaller than the error bar around it. This is one data point that agrees on direction, not a measurement of the market.

## What the adjustments are doing

In this particular set, the recurring drivers were bedroom count — six of the eight had four bedrooms against the subject's five, each worth about $113,110 — pool (about $159,716 where the comparable had none), and the cluster of interior condition, renovation level and floor area. Those factors did most of the work in narrowing the raw spread. The adjustments account for property differences only; no adjustment has been made for the dates these homes sold.

## What this comparison can't tell you

Eight comparables were used, selected from 32 candidate sales; that is a thin base, and a different eight would move the figures. Tested against actual sale prices in this suburb by leave-one-out backtest, this method's mean absolute error is about 11% in the $1,000,000–$2,000,000 range — a rate we publish. One sale, however carefully compared, cannot separate a home's own qualities from a shift in the market, and it says nothing about any other address. The auction clearance figures in circulation — a 46.0% weighted capital-city average for the week ending 5 July 2026, Brisbane at 23.5% (Cotality) — describe a different sale process again; homes here sell predominantly by private treaty.

The record is eight adjusted sales between $1,398,872 and $1,673,126, and one result at $1,620,000 that sat $94,962 above their median. Read against a suburb median that moved +5.8% year-on-year, it is a single consistent point — no more, and no less.
