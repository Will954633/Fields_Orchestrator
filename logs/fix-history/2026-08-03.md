## [V3-REVEAL-POLARITY] Light-bodied subjects inverted into dark ones — 09:30 AEST
**Symptom:** The golden retriever emblem (`land_prestige`, 12.4% of decks) rendered as a *black* dog with bright fur edges, and the school satchel as a black bag with bright seams. Will flagged it: "too much of its hair is blacked in". The tree, reeds, banksia, palm and whale were all correct.

## [WHALE-INK-STRIP-COMPOSITE] Semi-transparent strips changed the artwork's density — 07:04 AEST
**Symptom:** Rendering the whale with ink-coverage sprites (cream on black) made the animal's density depend on the `strips` slider: mean luminance swung 27% between strips=1 and strips=8. Invisible with the opaque sprites, obvious the moment every pixel became semi-transparent.
**Root cause:** Strips were drawn 1px wider than their step as gap insurance, dating from when sprites were opaque. ## [WHALE-DUTY-THRUST] Burst/glide sliders silently changed the whale's speed and beat rate — 07:29 AEST
**Symptom:** Shortening the glide to make the burst-and-coast rhythm read revealed that the whale was cruising at 1.30 m/s against a set 2.0, with a tail beat of 0.151 Hz — below the measured humpback band of 0.229 +/- 0.039 Hz. Raising the speed slider barely helped (U=2.5 still only realised 1.57 m/s).
**Root cause:** Thrust was calibrated for CONTINUOUS beating (`thrust = drag*U^2`, steady state v -> U). With burst-and-coast the animal only pushes for part of the cycle, so any duty below 1 undershoots the target speed. Because frequency is Strouhal-locked to *instantaneous* speed, the shortfall propagated straight into the beat rate — so a purely rhythmic control was moving two measured quantities out of band as a side effect. Raising U could not fix it: the drag time constant (~8s) is comparable to the burst length, so the whale never reaches U within a burst.
**Fix:** Scale thrust by 1/duty. Mean thrust must equal mean drag, so the instantaneous value during a burst is `drag*U^2/duty`. Duty is the mean of the stroke envelope over one cycle, memoised on the burst/glide/Strouhal params since the renderer re-integrates from t=0 every frame and recomputing 512 samples per step would be prohibitive.
**Verification:** probed the live page (not the model) across a crossing — speed now oscillates 1.72-2.28 m/s and the beat 0.198-0.262 Hz, every sample inside the measured band, and both hold across beats = 1.5/2.0/2.5/3.5. The rhythm sliders are now orthogonal to the physics.
**Also:** default changed to 2 beats + 3s glide, giving ~2 full cycles per 24s crossing at 41% duty.

## [WASH-GREY-TRANSFER] Colour-transferred whale came out grey — 07:59 AEST

## [DOM-SYSTEMATICALLY-FAST] Our days-on-market runs 5-10 days below REA in all three suburbs — 09:00 AEST

**Symptom:** Will cross-checked all three suburbs against realestate.com.au. Our published median
days on market is materially lower everywhere, and our medians run high while our growth runs low.

| | median gap | growth gap | sales capture | DOM gap | price->DOM spread |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Burleigh Waters | +0.8% | -2.2pp | 77% (151/197) | **-10.5d** (30 vs 41) | 24->42 (+18) |
| Robina | +2.8% | -1.2pp | 67% (223/333) | -5.0d (26 vs 31) | 20->32 (+12) |
| Varsity Lakes | +3.7% | -3.7pp | 62% (95/153) | -6.0d (21 vs 27) | 21->22 (+1) |

REA's definition (from their own tooltip): "The number of days that a property has been listed for
sale that falls in the middle of the total number of properties sold over the preceding 12 months."
Same window, same statistic — so this is not a quarter-vs-year artefact. Both are medians over
n=65-97 on our side, which is large enough that sampling noise should not produce a consistent
5-10 day gap in the same direction in all three suburbs.

**Two SEPARATE biases, on different subsets — this is the key insight:**

1. **Sales capture** misses 23-38% of transactions, and the median gap tracks the capture rate
   almost perfectly inversely (77%->+0.8%, 67%->+2.8%, 62%->+3.7%). What we miss is disproportionately
   the CHEAP end, which biases our median UP.
2. **DOM capture** covers only 43-50% of the sales we do hold, and biases DOWN. This cannot be the
   same cause: if we were missing cheap sales, and cheap homes sell faster (24 days vs 42 in
   Burleigh), our DOM would run SLOW. It runs FAST.

**Leading hypothesis (not yet confirmed):** relisting. A home that was listed, expired or was
withdrawn, then relisted and sold, is captured by us as its final short campaign while REA counts
days listed across the whole history. That would compress our figure in every suburb regardless of
price — which matches the ~5-6 day floor present even in Varsity Lakes, where price has NO effect on
days to sell (+1 day across price thirds).

**Will's hypothesis — confirmed for the excess.** Burleigh Waters' gap is roughly double the others,
and it is the only suburb with a long expensive tail (+18 days across price thirds; waterfront homes
at 49 days against 29 for non-waterfront, and $555,000 dearer). Waterfront is NOT excluded from our
medians or DOM — `shared/waterfront.py` gates editorial/valuation pages only — so the effect is
under-capture of that tail, not exclusion.

**Also found:** `is_waterfront()` cannot measure a base rate. It reads listing text and satellite
analysis, which only exist on scraped listings, so bare cadastral records all default to
non-waterfront: Burleigh Waters shows 1.4% of cadastral stock as waterfront against 16.0% of sold
records. For a canal suburb 1.4% is obviously wrong. **That figure measures enrichment coverage, not
incidence — do not use it as a base rate.**

**Exposure:** days on market is a headline stat on every `/market-intelligence/*` page, was the cover
number of the Q2 2026 report draft, and appears in several of the August summaries. It is
systematically fast in all three suburbs.

**Status:** OPEN. Pipeline trace in progress. Nothing corrected yet — the mechanism has to be
understood before the number is changed, because the fix differs completely depending on whether it
is a relisting artefact (recompute from full listing history) or an extraction gap (widen capture).

## [DOM-TRACE-COMPLETE] Pipeline traced: the REA gap is definitional, not an extraction bug — 10:30 AEST

Full trace of `[DOM-SYSTEMATICALLY-FAST]`, following Will's point that two medians over a large
enough sample should converge if they measure the same thing.

**Where our number comes from.** `scripts/backfill_days_on_market.py`, two sources in priority order:
(1) Domain's own `days_on_market` on the matching `property_timeline` sale event; (2) computed as
`sold_date - first_listed_timestamp`. It never overwrites an existing value.

**Finding 1 — the two "sources" are two populations, not two methods.** By suburb, source 2 reads
~10 days higher than source 1 (Burleigh 36 vs 26, Robina 34 vs 20, Varsity 26 vs 16). But on the 80
properties where BOTH are available the median difference is **zero**. Source 2 only runs where
source 1 found nothing, so the split reflects who has a Domain DOM, not a methodological difference.

**Finding 2 — `sold_date` is a DETECTION date, not a sale date.** Matching each sold record to its
own timeline sale event **by price**, our `sold_date` runs late: Burleigh median +1d / mean +14.1d,
Robina +7d / +10.9d, Varsity +0d / +6.9d, with 17-31% more than a fortnight late and a tail to
**+114 days** (14 Honeywood Court: we record 2026-06-04, its timeline records the sale at
2026-02-10 for the exact price we hold, agency named).

**Finding 3 — 11% of sales are binned into the WRONG QUARTER.** 44 of 386 matched sales, and the
direction is almost entirely forward by one quarter (Q1 2025->Q2 2025 x14, Q2 2025->Q3 2025 x10,
Q1 2026->Q2 2026 x8). Only 4 of 44 move backward. **This is a separate, material bug** — it
contaminates every quarterly median and volume figure with ~11% of the previous quarter's sales, and
plausibly contributes to why almost every quarter is flagged `reliable: false`.

**Finding 4 — the 60-day fuzzy-match tolerance is a consequence, not a cause.** Records whose
`sold_date` is more than 60 days late cannot match their own timeline event, so they get no DOM at
all. Coverage is 46% (215 of 469 houses).

**Finding 5 — and this is the one that matters: fixing the extraction does NOT close the REA gap.**
Re-matching by price instead of date tolerance, and taking the event's own `days_on_market`:

| | coverage today -> fixed | median today -> fixed | REA | gap after fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Burleigh Waters | 76 -> 81 | 30.5 -> 30.0 | 41 | **-11.0d (wider)** |
| Robina | 97 -> 97 | 26.0 -> 23.0 | 31 | **-8.0d (wider)** |
| Varsity Lakes | 42 -> 43 | 21.0 -> 19.0 | 27 | **-8.0d (wider)** |

Recovering more Domain DOM moves us FURTHER from REA, not closer.

**Conclusion.** Our days-on-market IS Domain's days-on-market, by construction. Domain's figure sits
systematically below realestate.com.au's for the same suburb and window. Two portals, two
definitions — most likely REA counts from first listing across relisting/withdrawal, Domain counts
the final campaign. **This is not a defect we can fix by better extraction, and we should stop
treating it as one.** What we can do is stop presenting DOM as a figure that checks out against an
independent source, because it does not and will not.

**Actions arising:**
1. DOM stays off the Q2 report cover; page 8 discloses that it is a Domain-basis figure sitting below

2. The quarter mis-binning (Finding 3) is a **real bug, still open** — fix `sold_date` to prefer the

3. Coverage at 46% is worth improving regardless, but must not be sold as closing the REA gap.

## [TIMELINE-SWEEP-CURSOR-BUG] Timeline refresh had never completed a run; 89% of sold records stale — 11:30 AEST

**Symptom:** Will asked whether we had ever run the fresh sweep of Domain property timelines that was
discussed. We had not. `scripts/refresh_property_timelines.py` was crashing on **every** run with
`pymongo.errors.CursorNotFound: cursor id ... not found (code 43)`, leaving **1,370 of 1,542 sold
records (89%) with a timeline that had never been refreshed**.

**Root cause:** the job opened a Cosmos cursor and then held it across the whole processing loop,
which does a Bright Data fetch plus a 6-second rate-limit sleep *per document*. On a few hundred
properties that is far longer than Cosmos keeps an idle cursor alive. This is the SECOND silent
breakage of this job — the first was a missing `cd` in the crontab (fixed 2026-07-22). Neither was
noticed because the job had **no `job_run()` heartbeat**, in breach of Rule 7.

**Evidence it mattered — Will's example.** 25 Huntingdale Crescent, Robina: Domain's live profile
shows the March 2026 sale at $1,731,000 with **26 days listed**. Our copy of that timeline had no
2026 event at all — its most recent entry was from **2023** — so our stored `days_on_market` of 41
came from the fallback computation, not from Domain.

**Fix:** materialise the cursor with `list(...)` before the loop (the projection is three small
fields, so the memory cost is nil), and wrap the run in `job_run("refresh_property_timelines",
cadence_hours=168)`.

**Sweep run:** 470 candidates (houses sold in the last 450 days across the three target suburbs),
**408 updated**, 62 Domain fetch failures (13%).

**Result — coverage transformed:**

| | DOM coverage before | after | median before | after | REA | gap after |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Burleigh Waters | 76/151 (50%) | **135/151 (89%)** | 30.5 | 28.0 | 41 | -13.0d |
| Robina | 97/223 (43%) | **201/223 (90%)** | 26.0 | 24.0 | 31 | -7.0d |
| Varsity Lakes | 42/95 (44%) | **82/95 (86%)** | 21.0 | 20.0 | 27 | -7.0d |

**The REA gap is now confirmed definitional, on solid ground.** The earlier conclusion was drawn from
the 11% of timelines that happened to be current — a biased slice, and I said so more firmly than the
evidence supported. With ~88% coverage of Domain's OWN days-on-market the gap persists and slightly
widens (7-13 days). Domain and realestate.com.au measure "days on market" differently; REA almost
certainly counts from first listing across relisting/withdrawal, Domain the final campaign. **We
inherit Domain's definition. This is not fixable by better extraction and should be disclosed, not
chased.**

**Quarter mis-binning re-confirmed at scale.** On 600 price-matched sales (up from 386):
**11% land in the wrong quarter**, `sold_date` runs a median +7 days and mean +11.7 days late, and
**25% are more than a fortnight late**. Direction is overwhelmingly forward by one quarter. This is
still OPEN — the fix is to prefer the timeline's own sale date, which is now available for far more
records after the sweep.
