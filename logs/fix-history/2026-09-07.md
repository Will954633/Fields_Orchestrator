## [SLICE-HOG-STRUCTURAL-CODESERVER] Workbench unusable — code-server main leaked to 6.53GB over 32d uptime — 19:25 AEST
**Symptom:** "VM is not loading on the browser." Load average 32.37 and climbing (32.4 / 23.9 / 11.6 — minutes old), 11Gi/15Gi RAM used with 1.5Gi free, 10 processes in `D` state, root at **91%** (9.3G free, alert fires at 92%). SSH and TLS both fine throughout; `/` returned 200 at the edge, so only the workbench itself was dead.
**Root cause:** the **code-server main process itself** (pid 841, `/usr/lib/code-server/lib/node …/out/node/entry`) had grown to **6.53GB RSS — 39.8% of system RAM — across 32 days of uptime**, with the extension host adding a further 1.72GB over 3 days. That put the shared slice at `memory.current` 9.66GB against `MemoryHigh=9G`, with page cache crushed to **13.5MB**. Every `D`-state process was parked in `folio_wait_bit_common` — waiting on page-ins, i.e. the slice had evicted its own cache and was re-reading everything from disk. Same slice-thrash end state as 2026-08-24 and 2026-08-26, third distinct trigger.
**Why nothing automated fixed it:** `reap_slice_hog()` *did* detect it and logged `level=critical … slice_hog_structural[NOT killed]` on every pass. That is the guard behaving exactly as designed — pid 841 is on the structural exempt list, because killing code-server main destroys the user's entire workbench, every terminal and every running session. The exemption is correct and should stay. The consequence is that when the hog **is** the structural process, there is deliberately no automated remedy and it needs a human-authorised restart.
**Fix:** `sudo systemctl restart code-server`, after first confirming the blast radius was acceptable — the workbench was already unusable to Will, the two live Claude extension sessions showed no source-file writes in the preceding 15 minutes (only cron/daemon logs) and the 4h35m one had consumed just ~683 CPU-seconds (idle), and Claude Code sessions persist to disk and are resumable. Separately, deleted 2 of 3 local mongodump tarballs after verifying each was **byte-identical in size** to its `gs://fields-mongodb-backups` copy, keeping the newest as a warm local restore.
**Result:** code-server main 6.53GB → **66MB**; slice anon 9.47GB → **36MB**, page cache restored; RAM used 11Gi → 2.9Gi, available 3.9Gi → **12Gi**; load 32.37 → 6.58; D-state 10 → 0; `/healthz` 200 in 3.5ms, external workbench 200 in 0.24s. Disk 91% → **86%** (14G free).
**Open:** (1) No scheduled recycle of code-server — a 32-day-old process reaching 6.5GB is unbounded growth that will recur on roughly this cadence. A weekly restart at a quiet hour would pre-empt the whole class, at the cost of dropping sessions at that moment. Recommended, not implemented. (2) The cohort-aware reaper from `[SLICE-COHORT-THRASH-BACKTEST]` (2026-08-26) is still not built. (3) Disk floor is now structural, not cruft: `/var/lib/mongodb` 5.3G, `/var/lib/snapd` 3.8G (old revisions), local backups 2.5G/night and growing (were 1.5G in August).
**Files:** none changed — operational fix only (code-server restart; 2 verified-redundant backup tarballs deleted).
**Recurrence:** Nth for the slice-thrash class ([SLICE-THRASH-MONGO-LIST] 08-24 single runaway, [SLICE-COHORT-THRASH-BACKTEST] 08-26 cohort, ugrep lockups 08-01/06, [VM-WEDGE-PREVENTION] 07-27). **First where the hog is the structural process itself** — the one shape the reaper is deliberately built never to act on.
