## [STEP113-ROLLING-SWEEP-FALSE-ALARM] Withdrawn detection alarmed nightly for correct behaviour — 13:35 AEST

## [H1-UNIT-INDEXING] Attached dwellings enter the index — 4,908 URLs, one flag, both gates — 13:10 AEST
**Decision (Will, 2026-08-13):** bar C — complex + scheme content — all at once. 4,980 flagged.
**Why a persisted flag and not a rule on each side.** The sitemap (Node) and the route's `meta()`
(TypeScript) must agree exactly. Expressing "has a complex and scheme content" twice in two languages
is precisely what produced `[OFFMARKET-UNIT-SITEMAP-MISMATCH]` on 2026-08-08 — **4,559 URLs (25.7%)
advertised in the sitemap while serving noindex.** So `flag_unit_indexable.py` computes it ONCE in
Python and writes `unit_indexable` + `unit_indexable_reason`; both consumers read that field.
Disagreement is impossible by construction rather than merely unlikely.
**The house rule is untouched.** Units enter the sitemap as a PARALLEL `$or` branch, so
`offmarket_discovery_nightly`'s COVERAGE_GAP_TOLERANCE assertion keeps meaning "indexed house →
deck". Units have their own content path (`unit_content`), so they get their own Rule 7b assertions
in the flag script — including an indexable-rate band of 25–75% (measured ~43%), which refuses to let
the sitemap move a long way unchecked.
**Bar:** attached · sale-history tier · not listed/waterfront/unresolved · linked to a scheme · at
least one scheme-scoped claim. Deliberately NOT requiring a valuation range: a page whose method
honestly refuses still carries complex identity, scheme context, unit market data and sale history,
and for someone searching that exact address it is the page they want. That is also why Burleigh
Waters (588) is in, despite never displaying a figure.
**⚠ THE MISMATCH ALMOST HAPPENED AGAIN, IN MIRROR IMAGE.** After deploying, the route served
`index, follow` on 4,908 unit URLs while the live sitemap still held 10,795 — pages indexable but
unadvertised. Cause: `public/sitemap.xml` is a COMMITTED file regenerated by a **VM cron at 06:15**,
because Netlify's build container has no DB credentials; my local `node generate-sitemap.mjs` never
reached production. Closed by running `regenerate-sitemap.sh` (the proper path — it regenerates,
guards against a property-less sitemap, and commits) then triggering a rebuild. Live: **10,795 →
15,703** off-market URLs.
**Verification:** 6/6 probe slugs agree in BOTH directions — 4 flagged units in the sitemap AND
serving `index, follow`; 1 correctly excluded both ways (`no_scheme_content`); 1 recently-sold home
301-ing to `/property/` and absent from both, which is correct existing behaviour, not a defect. The
`sitemap_robots_invariant` monitor needs no change — units now sit inside the off-market family it
already samples.
**Files:** `15_Off-Market/Units/scripts/flag_unit_indexable.py`; website
`scripts/generate-sitemap.mjs`, `src/routes/off-market.$slug.tsx`, `src/pages/OffMarketPage/types.ts`

## [UNITS-SERP-META] SERP metadata for the 4,980 newly-indexed unit pages — 14:20 AEST
**Why now:** indexing changed the stakes. Until today no unit page was in the index, so its title,
description and JSON-LD were invisible. 4,980 URLs went live to Google this morning, and those three
strings are what a searcher actually sees.
**Checked first, and the worst case was NOT firing:** the audit flagged a hardcoded `${land_size}m²

**Improved:**
1. **The scheme is in the description.** "52/24 Ron Penhaligon Way, Robina — 2 bed, 2 bath **in
   Emerald Lagoon, one of 65 homes**". The complex is the most distinctive and most-searched fact
   about a unit — people look for "Emerald Lagoon Robina" far more than for a lot size — and it is
   what makes two otherwise-identical unit snippets tell each other apart in a SERP.
